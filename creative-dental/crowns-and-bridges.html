What Is a Dental Bridge?

Dental bridges are used to replace one or more missing teeth. A traditional bridge uses the surrounding healthy teeth as anchors, holding artificial teeth securely in place to fill the gap. This helps prevent shifting of adjacent teeth, maintains jaw alignment, and restores normal function.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Crowns and Bridges

What should I know about dental crowns before getting one?

Dental crowns are protective caps placed over teeth that are weakened, cracked, or heavily filled. We use them to restore both the function and appearance of the tooth. They’re shaped and shaded to blend with your natural teeth.

When is a dental bridge usually recommended?

A dental bridge is often used when a patient is missing one or more teeth in a row. We anchor the bridge to nearby teeth to help restore chewing ability, prevent shifting, and maintain proper alignment.

Are there different types of materials used for crowns and bridges?

Yes, crowns and bridges can be made from porcelain, ceramic, metal alloys, or a combination. We typically recommend materials based on the location of the tooth, function, and aesthetic preferences.

How do I prepare my tooth for a crown or bridge?

We begin by shaping the tooth or teeth involved, taking impressions, and placing a temporary restoration. Once the final crown or bridge is ready, we carefully fit and cement it in place for a natural, secure result.

What are some common concerns patients have about this treatment?

Many patients are concerned about discomfort or the process taking too long. We ensure the procedure is as comfortable and efficient as possible, often completed over two visits.

What are the long-term benefits of getting a crown or bridge?

Crowns and bridges help preserve the natural structure of your mouth, support proper bite function, and improve overall oral health. We design them to be both durable and functional for daily use.

Is there anything I should do before getting this type of restoration?

We recommend a full dental evaluation first to determine if a crown or bridge is the best option. We’ll also review your medical history and oral hygiene habits to ensure the best long-term outcome.
