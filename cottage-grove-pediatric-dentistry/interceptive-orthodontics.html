The Benefits Of Interceptive Orthodontics

Prevents Complex Dental Issues

Interceptive orthodontics is designed to identify and address potential dental problems before they become more serious. This proactive approach can prevent issues such as overcrowding, crossbites, and protruding teeth from worsening over time, reducing the need for more complex and costly orthodontic treatment in the future.

Improves Appearance and Confidence

Having straight, well-aligned teeth can significantly improve a child&#x27;s self-esteem and confidence. By correcting dental issues early on, interceptive orthodontics can help your child feel more comfortable and confident in their smile, which can positively impact their social interactions and overall quality of life.

Enhances Oral Health

Interceptive orthodontics doesn&#x27;t just improve the appearance of your child&#x27;s teeth—it also enhances their oral health. Straight, well-spaced teeth are easier to clean, reducing the risk of cavities and gum disease. Additionally, a properly aligned bite can prevent issues such as uneven tooth wear, jaw pain, and difficulties with chewing and speaking.

The Interceptive Orthodontics Treatment Process

Initial Consultation

The first step in the interceptive orthodontics process is an initial consultation with Dr. Okoruwa. During this appointment, Dr. Okoruwa will examine your child&#x27;s teeth, jaw, and bite to determine whether they could benefit from early orthodontic treatment.

Treatment Planning

If Dr. Okoruwa determines that your child would benefit from interceptive orthodontics, the next step is to develop a personalized treatment plan. This plan will outline the specific treatments your child will need, such as space maintainers, growth appliances, or partial braces.

Treatment Implementation

Once the treatment plan has been finalized, the next step is to implement the treatments. This could involve fitting your child with a space maintainer to prevent overcrowding, using a growth appliance to correct a crossbite, or applying partial braces to guide the position of their permanent teeth. This all depends on your child’s unique needs.
