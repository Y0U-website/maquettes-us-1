What are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are root-shaped posts that are surgically inserted into the bone to help replace missing teeth. As the bone grows and attaches to the dental implant over an average of three months, these posts provide a sturdy, long-lasting foundation for crowns, bridges, or dentures.

Dental implants are typically made from titanium or titanium alloys, which allow for bone to grow to their surfaces. Dental implants can also be made from ceramic materials if someone is allergic to metal. Dental implants provide a predictable option for replacing missing teeth as they are not susceptible to decay and are able to withstand chewing and functioning forces that the natural teeth may not.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

Dental implants are the best tooth replacement option out there. They replace the roots of the missing teeth, allowing for a wide range of additional benefits, including stability, comfort, and longevity. To achieve this, getting dental implants requires a multi-step process that takes place over the course of several months. Everyone’s treatment looks a little bit different, but here are the main steps that everyone can expect.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

The first step is to attend a consultation. At this time, we will examine your smile to determine if you are a good candidate for the procedure. We will also go over the timeline and financial aspects of your treatment. Some patients require preliminary procedures, like gum disease treatment and bone grafting, before they can get dental implants. If this applies to you, we will help you get them scheduled. Once your gums and jaw are in optimal shape, we will move on to planning the rest of your treatment.

Dental Implant Surgery

Getting dental implants requires a surgical procedure. We complete this right here at our practice, so you don’t need to work with an outside dentist at a separate location. After the area is numbed with a local anesthetic, an incision is made into the gum tissue so the dental implants can be placed at precise locations and angles in the jawbone. The gums are closed, and protective caps are placed over the ends of the implants to keep them safe while the area heals.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

Over the next 3 to 6 months, the osseointegration process takes place. This is when the titanium dental implants and the jawbone fuse together, creating a stable foundation for your replacement teeth. After the area has healed, we will place small connector pieces called abutments on the ends of the implants. Impressions are taken and sent to the dental lab so your final restorations can be crafted.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

Once your final restorations are ready, you can return to our dental practice. Your dentist will secure your replacement teeth in place. If everything looks and feels great, you will be free to show of your new and improved smile to the world!

Day-to-Day Benefits

Dental implants will blend seamlessly into your smile, improving your everyday routine in many subtle ways. In fact, once they heal, most patients forget they’re even there because they work just like natural teeth, providing benefits like:

  • No Diet Restrictions: Eat all your favorite foods without worrying about your implants slipping like dentures.
  • Improved Speech Clarity: Fill gaps that can otherwise cause speech impediments like mumbling, slurring, or whistling.
  • Easier Oral Hygiene: Brush and floss your teeth as normal. No special tools or products needed.
  • Boosted Confidence: Smile with confidence knowing your new restorations look identical to natural teeth.

Health Benefits

Beyond helping your smile look complete again, dental implants also support your overall health. Because they replace the entire tooth from root to crown, they offer superior stability and help your teeth function naturally, leading to:

  • Improved Chewing and Digestion: Break down food more effectively, so your body can absorb nutrients and reduce stomach issues.
  • No Irritation or Slipping: Avoid the sore spots, rubbing, and movement that can come with dentures.
  • Protected Natural Teeth: Stop nearby teeth from drifting out of place or overworking to compensate.
  • Lowered Overall Health Risks: A cleaner, healthier mouth can reduce risks linked to excessive oral bacteria.

Long-Term Benefits

Dental implants have an incredible success rate often over 95 %. With consistent home care and routine visits to our Buda dental office, your implants can serve you well for decades, offering advantages such as:

  • Long-Lasting Results: Dental implants can often last a lifetime with proper maintenance.
  • Preserved Jawbone: Implants stimulate the bone, helping prevent the shrinkage that follows tooth loss.
  • Younger Facial Structure: A denser jawbone helps keep your face shape looking youthful longer.
  • Better Long-Term Value: While the upfront cost is higher, fewer replacements and repairs make implants an investment that continues to pay out over time.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Missing One Tooth

It takes just one dental implant post to replace a missing tooth. The first step is securing the post to your jawbone and allowing the two to fuse. Once you’ve healed, we will place a crown on top of it, much like how we might restore a natural tooth. We can also make your crown out of tooth-colored materials that match the surrounding teeth. That way, the final result looks as natural as it feels.

Missing Multiple Teeth

If you’ve lost multiple teeth in one arch, we can always replace them with single dental implants. However, that’s rarely the most efficient or cost-effective approach. That’s why we typically recommend that three teeth that are missing consecutively be replaced with two dental implants and a fully customized dental bridge. Multiple gaps throughout an arch, on the other hand, can be filled with a partial denture and an appropriate number of implant posts.

Missing All Teeth

If you’ve lost all of the teeth in a single arch, you can replace them at once with a full denture. If we decide together that this is the best option for you, then we will place 4-6 strategically throughout the arch. Then, we will secure your custom restoration on top. Unlike traditional dentures, implant dentures can be removable or fixed, depending on what you would prefer. They can also prevent bone loss, which will help maintain your youthful face shape and strong bite force.

The Parts of Your Dental Implant

There are several important parts to a dental implant, all of which will incur separate costs. Here’s how we determine the price for each:

  • Number of dental implants: One dental implant to replace a single tooth will cost less than four to six dental implants for an implant denture; however, an implant denture will be more cost effective than replacing each tooth individually.
  • Type of restoration: Implant crowns, bridges, and dentures each cost different amounts. The smaller the restoration, the less expensive it will be.
  • Size or material: Some implants are made longer or shorter than others depending on where they must be placed in the jawbone. They are also available in different materials such as zirconia instead of titanium.
  • Brand of dental implant: Much like different car or smartphone brands, different manufacturers make different dental implants to cater to the unique needs of patients.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

If you take excellent care of them, your new dental implants from Buda Dental Professionals can keep your smile complete, healthy, and strong for a lifetime. However, your implants may fail early if you don’t adopt the habits necessary to keep them in great shape for the long run. Damage to your implants or infections of the gum tissue supporting them can cause them to come loose and may make replacement necessary. Here’s a brief guide to a few excellent habits for keeping your dental implants in great shape.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

While dental implants cannot get cavities, it’s still essential to practice excellent oral hygiene to protect your gums, jawbones, and remaining teeth from infections after receiving them even if you have no natural teeth remaining. Oral health issues like periodontal disease can lead to implant failure, so it’s crucial to brush and floss daily while regularly attending routine checkups and cleanings at our Buda office.

Eat a Healthy Diet

While dental implants allow you to eat whatever you like, it’s best to eat a diverse and nutritious diet that doesn’t include a lot of sugar and acid. Overindulging in foods that are especially crunchy or sticky can also cause undue stress to your implants, but enjoying foods and beverages rich in protein, calcium, and vitamin C will help keep your gums and jawbones healthy and strong.

Break Bad Habits

Certain bad habits can increase your risk of implant failure. Tobacco use in any form is terrible for your oral health since it slows your gums’ healing process and encourages infection. It’s also important to refrain from chewing on hard objects like ice and writing utensils and using your teeth to open packages or bottles.

Protect Your Dental Implants

While dental implants are crafted from incredibly durable materials like ceramic and titanium, they are not indestructible. To help your implants avoid unnecessary wear and tear, wear a mouthguard to protect your teeth when playing sports or a nightguard to bed if you grind your teeth in your sleep.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Come back to Buda Dental Professionals for routine exams and cleanings every six months so we can give your mouth a deep cleaning, monitor your oral health, and address any issues in their early stages. This can prevent all sorts of oral health problems that can compromise your implants and save you from needing thousands of dollars’ worth of restorative dental work in the long run.

Dental Implants Post-Op Instructions

After you’ve undergone dental implant surgery, you’ll need to follow a specific set of guidelines so that your recovery goes swimmingly. Our team will make sure to provide you with the information you need before and after your appointment so that you can prepare ahead of time. Here are some of the common dental implant post-op instructions that you can expect to implement for successful results.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

During your healing process, your mouth will need to form a blood clot around the surgical site, allowing it to regenerate while the metal post fuses with your jawbone. Rest will be essential for this to be properly completed, but you’ll also want to follow these rules during this critical time:

  • Avoid drinking through a straw
  • Do not spit; swallow your saliva or use a tissue
  • Refrain from smoking after your procedure
  • Keep away from touching the implant with your fingers or tongue

Common Side Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

Some of the typical symptoms that can arise from dental implant surgery include:

  • Inflammation : It’s natural for swelling to occur for the first 72 hours following the procedure, but you can minimize this by using a cold compress.
  • Bleeding : Intermittent bleeding can usually happen after your surgery, and you can control this by applying light pressure with a clean gauze or washcloth.
  • Discomfort : Mild pain or discomfort is normal after dental implant placement. You can easily manage this with store-bought or prescribed painkillers.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

To allow your blood clot and implant site to remain undisturbed, it’s best to stick to softer foods like:

  • Mashed potatoes
  • Applesauce
  • Yogurt
  • Scrambled eggs
  • Pasta
  • Soup
  • Pudding
  • Ice cream

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

Most patients should be able to brush their teeth like normal the day after their procedure. However, you’ll need to be careful when scrubbing around your implant so that you don’t bother the recovery process. You can also rinse your mouth with salt water to keep food and bacteria away while also disinfecting the surgical site. Avoid using products containing alcohol, as this can be uncomfortable and aggravate the area.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

After you’ve had your new dental crown, bridge, or denture placed on your dental implants, you’ll be free to show off your brand-new smile! Of course, it’s possible to experience mild sensitivity when receiving your new restoration(s), but this should only be temporary. You can also manage your situation with simple at-home remedies and pain relievers. If you notice any strange symptoms like bleeding or swelling, notify our team so we can help right away.
