Essential information about this procedure

Dental implants are the only tooth-replacement option that addresses the root of the problem — literally. A small titanium post is placed directly into the jawbone, where it fuses with the surrounding bone through a natural process called osseointegration. That fusion is what gives implants their exceptional stability and what makes them feel and function like your natural teeth.

How Implant Treatment Works — 3 Stages

Dr. Deshpande guides you through each stage with clear explanations and check-ins, so you always know exactly where you are in the process.

Implant Post Placed

A small titanium post is surgically placed into your jawbone under local anesthesia, then given time — typically six to eight months — to fuse naturally with the surrounding bone.

Abutment Attached

Once fusion is confirmed, a connector piece called an abutment is secured to the implant post, creating the stable anchor that will hold your final crown in place after a short 1–3 week healing period.

Custom Crown Secured

Your permanent, custom-made crown is attached to the abutment — matched to the color and shape of your surrounding teeth so the result looks and feels completely natural.

Is the implant surgery painful, and what is recovery like?

Implant placement is performed under local anesthesia, so you won&#x27;t feel pain during the procedure itself. Afterward, it&#x27;s normal to experience some swelling and tenderness for a few days, which most patients manage comfortably with over-the-counter pain relievers. Dr. Deshpande will provide detailed aftercare instructions and check in with you during your recovery to make sure everything is healing well.

How long does the entire implant process take from start to finish?

The implant process typically takes several months from the initial placement to the final restoration, because the titanium post needs time to fuse securely with the jawbone — a natural process called osseointegration. The exact timeline varies depending on your individual healing and whether preparatory steps like bone grafting are needed. Dr. Deshpande will walk you through a realistic, personalized timeline at your consultation so you know exactly what to expect.

Am I a good candidate for dental implants, or do I need to see a specialist?

Most adults in good general health with sufficient jawbone density are candidates for implants, though a thorough evaluation is necessary to confirm. Dr. Deshpande provides implant treatment right here at Brilliant Smiles of North Plainfield, so there&#x27;s no need to be referred to a separate specialist for most cases. She&#x27;ll assess your bone density, gum health, and overall oral health during your consultation and give you an honest assessment of whether implants are the right path for you.

How long do dental implants last compared to dentures or bridges?

With proper care, dental implants are designed to last a lifetime — the titanium post that integrates with your jawbone is essentially a permanent fixture. The crown placed on top may eventually need replacement after 15 to 20 years, but the implant itself rarely requires intervention. Dentures and bridges, by comparison, typically need to be replaced or adjusted over time, making implants one of the most durable and cost-effective long-term solutions for missing teeth.
