Dental Braces

Dental braces are metal wires attached to the teeth to gradually move them into the correct position. They can be used to treat various problems, including crooked teeth, gaps in the teeth, and incorrect bites. Traditional braces are a good option for those who have extensive orthodontic needs. While it is usually associated with children and adolescents, adults can also benefit from this type of treatment, too.

Invisalign

Invisalign utilizes a series of clear aligner trays to straighten teeth gradually. Because the aligners are nearly invisible, you can undergo orthodontic treatment without anyone knowing. In addition, Invisalign is more comfortable than traditional braces and can be removed for eating and brushing. As a result, Invisalign is an ideal orthodontic treatment option for adults and children who want to correct mild to moderate orthodontic issues.

Dental Retainers

Retainers are typically made from plastic and wire, and they help to keep teeth in their new, straight position after braces have been removed or Invisalign treatment has been completed. They are usually worn for a few hours each day and can be removed for eating and brushing. Retainers are an effective way to maintain straighter teeth and are often recommended for patients who have had previous orthodontic treatment.

Braces vs. Invisalign

There are two standard methods of orthodontics: braces and Invisalign. Braces are the more traditional method involving the placement of metal wires and brackets on the teeth. Invisalign is a newer method that uses clear, customized aligners to straighten teeth gradually. Both braces and Invisalign are effective at straightening teeth, but they have some key differences.

Braces are more visible and can be uncomfortable for some people. In contrast, Invisalign is virtually invisible, and many people find it more comfortable than braces. With Invisalign, you can also remove your aligners for eating and brushing your teeth, whereas braces are fixed.

With traditional braces, you will need to come into the office for adjustments; with Invisalign, you will be sent home with a few aligners to change out at a rate to be determined by your orthodontist. You will need to come into the office for progress checks with your orthodontist, but you will not need to come in as frequently as with traditional braces. Ultimately, the best orthodontic treatment for you will depend on your individual needs and preferences.

Cost of Braces

The cost of braces will be different depending on the type you choose and the length of time you need to wear them. Metal braces are usually the most affordable, followed by ceramic and lingual braces. Cost of Invisalign

The cost of Invisalign will also vary depending on the individual case, but it is typically more expensive than braces. Factors such as the length of treatment, the severity of alignment, and the number of aligners needed will all determine the cost. The best way to determine the cost of Invisalign for your case is to schedule a consultation with our orthodontist.
