What is a Dental Bridge?

A dental bridge is a false tooth secured to both sides of a missing tooth. In other words, a dental bridge spans the gap left by a missing tooth, connecting two healthy teeth. A dental bridge can be made of several materials, including porcelain, alloys, and gold.

A dental bridge can be used to replace one tooth or several teeth at a time. Before deciding what type of dental bridge you may need, it’s essential to understand that there are three types. The type of dental bridge you’re recommended will depend on the health of your surrounding teeth.

Your dentist will be able to determine your suitability for each type of bridge.

The first step in receiving a dental bridge is having your dentist examine the teeth and gums. The X-ray will allow your dentist to see the health of your teeth and gums, which is crucial in choosing the best dental bridge for you.

Once you’ve been given an X-ray, your dentist will clean and shape the gums around the teeth supporting the bridge. This can also be called gum grafting. Your dentist will then take impressions of the teeth to determine each tooth’s shape and size accurately.

Finally, your dentist will select the material for the bridge and have the bridge made by a dental lab. The dentist will then place the dental bridge, securing it to the teeth on either side.

The cost of a dental bridge will depend on your specific situation. Your insurance coverage, the type of dental bridge you receive, and the dental lab that makes the bridge will all affect the cost.

The average cost of a dental bridge will range from $500 to $1,250. Some dental bridges will require added fees, such as the cost of X-rays and the cost of having the dental lab make the bridge.

What is a Dental Crown?

Dental crowns and bridges are a kind of restorative treatment that will completely cover your entire tooth. Why You May Need A Dental Crown?

If your tooth has been damaged in some way, you may be in need of a dental crown. The most common reasons for needing a dental crown include:

Cracked teeth

If your teeth have become chipped or cracked, a dental crown can help prevent the problem from worsening.

Decayed teeth

A dental crown can be used to cover the affected area if you have a cavity or other dental decay and the tooth can’t be treated with a filling.

Exposure to tooth roots

If gum disease has led to a receding gum line and you have exposed tooth roots, a dental crown can help protect the roots from infection.
