What is Scaling and Root Planing?

Scaling and root planing is a non-surgical periodontal procedure that cleans away plaque and hardened deposits beneath the gumline to treat and control gum disease. The treatment involves two essential steps designed to remove infection and support healing.

Scaling

Scaling removes plaque and hardened tartar (calculus) from the surfaces of the teeth and beneath the gums. These deposits contain bacteria that irritate gum tissue and contribute to inflammation and infection.

Removing this buildup helps reduce bacterial activity and allows the gums to begin healing. In some cases, digital X-rays may be used to evaluate the extent of buildup and monitor the health of the supporting bone.

Root Planing

Root planing involves smoothing the surfaces of the tooth roots. This step helps the gums reattach more securely to the teeth and makes it more difficult for bacteria to accumulate again.

Together, these steps create a healthier environment for the gums and support long-term periodontal stability.

Benefits of Scaling and Root Planing

Scaling and root planing offer several important benefits for patients with periodontal disease. By removing infection at its source, this treatment supports both immediate and long-term gum health.

Together, these benefits help restore gum health and support the long-term stability of your smile.

  • Stops the Progression of Gum Disease: Removing plaque and tartar beneath the gumline helps control the bacterial infection that causes periodontal disease.
  • Reduces Gum Inflammation: Once bacteria are eliminated, gum tissue can begin to heal, reducing swelling, bleeding, and discomfort.
  • Helps Prevent Tooth Loss: Treating gum disease early helps protect the structures that support your teeth.
  • Improves Oral Hygiene: Smooth root surfaces make it easier to maintain healthy gums with daily brushing and flossing.
  • Supports Overall Oral Health: Healthy gums provide a strong foundation for long-term dental health.

Recovery and Aftercare Tips

Most patients recover quickly following scaling and root planing. Mild sensitivity or gum tenderness may occur temporarily but typically resolves within a few days.

Following these aftercare recommendations can help support healing:

Maintaining consistent oral hygiene is essential for preventing the recurrence of gum disease. Preventive services such as fluoride treatments and dental sealants may also help protect teeth as part of a comprehensive oral health plan.

  • Brush twice daily with a soft-bristle toothbrush.
  • Floss daily to remove plaque between teeth.
  • Use antimicrobial mouthwash if recommended by your dentist.
  • Avoid very hard foods immediately after treatment.
  • Attend all follow-up and periodontal maintenance visits.
