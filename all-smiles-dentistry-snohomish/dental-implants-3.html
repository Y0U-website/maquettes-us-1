What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that is surgically placed into the jawbone, where it fuses with the surrounding bone tissue in a process called osseointegration. Once healed, a custom-made crown is placed on top to restore the look and function of the missing tooth. Think of it like planting a new root; the implant becomes a stable, permanent foundation that supports a lifelike restoration above the gumline.

Unlike removable dentures, implants are set in place. You brush them, floss them, and forget they aren’t your natural teeth. They don’t slip, don’t require adhesive, and don’t need to be removed at night.

Are Dental Implants Right for You?

Most adults in good overall health are candidates for implants, but a thorough evaluation is needed to confirm it. In general, you may be a strong candidate if you meet the following criteria.

One or More Missing Teeth

Whether you are replacing a single tooth or several, implants can be tailored to your needs and blend seamlessly with your natural teeth.

Adequate Bone Density

Enough healthy jawbone is needed to support the implant. If you do not have enough bone, treatments like bone grafting can help rebuild it first.

Healthy Gums and Good Habits

A commitment to good oral hygiene, along with addressing any active gum disease first, sets your implant up for lasting success.

Certain conditions, such as uncontrolled diabetes or active gum disease, may need to be addressed first. Research supports the longevity of dental implants as a tooth replacement option. A 20-year meta-analysis published by the National Institutes of Health found that approximately 4 out of 5 implants remained functional two decades after placement, underscoring the value of proper planning, placement, and long-term follow-up care.

Restoration and Final Results

After your implant is placed, a healing period allows the post to fuse with the surrounding bone in the process called osseointegration. This stage is what gives your implant its strength and stability, and it typically takes a few months depending on your body and your treatment plan. We check in with you along the way so you always know what to expect.

Once osseointegration is complete and your implant has fully integrated with the bone, we place a custom crown that is matched to the color and shape of your surrounding teeth. The result is a restoration that blends naturally with your smile.
