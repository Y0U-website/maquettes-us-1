What Happens During a Professional Dental Cleaning

Many patients are surprised to learn how much takes place during a standard cleaning appointment. The visit isn’t simply a quick polish and a goodbye. Our hygienists take a thorough, systematic approach designed to protect both your teeth and your gum tissue.

A typical cleaning begins with scaling, a process that carefully removes plaque and calcified tartar deposits from the tooth surfaces and below the gumline. Even with excellent brushing habits, tartar accumulates in areas that bristles cannot access. After scaling, the teeth are polished using a gritty paste that smooths the enamel and removes surface stains. This step also makes it harder for plaque to adhere between visits.

Flossing helps to clear debris from between the teeth and check on the health of your gum tissue. We also use intraoral cameras to give you a detailed, real-time view of your own mouth so you understand exactly what we’re seeing. This is a reflection of our commitment to patient education, not just treatment.

Our team pairs this process with oral hygiene instruction tailored to you. Whether you have a specific trouble area, use certain dental appliances, or are simply looking to improve your daily routine, we take the time to give you guidance that makes a real difference between appointments.

Why Dental Cleanings Matter for Your Overall Health

It’s easy to think of a cleaning as a cosmetic service, something that makes your smile look and feel fresh. In reality, professional cleanings are one of the most effective tools available for protecting your long-term health. The connection between oral health and systemic health is well-established, and routine preventive care plays a central role in managing that relationship.

According to the CDC, nearly half of adults aged 30 and older show signs of gum disease . Left unaddressed, periodontal disease has been linked to increased risk for heart disease, diabetes complications, and other systemic conditions. Regular cleanings help interrupt the cycle of bacterial buildup that leads to gum disease in the first place, making them far more valuable than most people realize. Our team also performs oral cancer screenings at routine visits, adding another layer of protection that can make a significant difference in outcomes when changes are caught early.

How Often Should You Schedule a Cleaning

For most patients, a cleaning every six months is the standard recommendation. This interval gives us enough time to monitor any developing changes while preventing buildup from reaching problematic levels. That said, some patients benefit from more frequent visits.

If you have a history of periodontal disease, are undergoing orthodontic treatment, are managing diabetes, or are prone to rapid tartar accumulation, we may recommend cleanings every three to four months. This more frequent schedule, often called periodontal maintenance, keeps inflammation in check and reduces the risk of progression.

We also pair cleanings with comprehensive dental exams to get a complete picture of your oral health at each visit. Rather than treating each appointment as a standalone event, we track your progress over time and adjust our recommendations based on what we’re observing in your mouth.
