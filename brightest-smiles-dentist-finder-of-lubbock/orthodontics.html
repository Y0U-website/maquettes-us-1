Who Needs Orthodontic Treatment?

Orthodontic treatment is suitable for both children and adults. Early evaluation allows providers to monitor jaw growth and tooth development, while adults often seek treatment to improve alignment or correct long-standing bite concerns.

Parents exploring care for children may also consider coordination with a family dentist to ensure comprehensive monitoring during growth.

Types of Orthodontic Treatments

Treatment plans vary based on age, alignment severity, and patient preference.

Traditional Braces

Metal or ceramic braces use brackets and wires to gradually move teeth into proper alignment. They are often recommended for more complex cases.

Clear Aligners

Clear aligners provide a removable, less visible option for many alignment concerns. These custom trays gradually shift teeth through a series of planned movements.

Early Interceptive Treatment

For younger patients, early treatment may guide jaw development and create space for permanent teeth.

Your provider will evaluate your bite, spacing, and jaw alignment before recommending the most appropriate method.

Orthodontics and Oral Health

While many patients pursue orthodontics for aesthetic reasons, proper alignment also supports oral health.

Routine maintenance, including professional teeth cleaning , remains important during treatment.

  • Be easier to clean
  • Reduce uneven enamel wear
  • Improve bite balance
  • Lower the risk of gum inflammation

How Long Does Orthodontic Treatment Take?

Treatment timelines vary based on complexity. Some cases may require several months, while others extend longer depending on movement goals and bite correction.

Regular follow-up appointments ensure that teeth move safely and predictably.

Adult Orthodontics

Many adults pursue orthodontic care later in life. Modern options such as clear aligners make treatment more discreet than in the past.

A personalized plan accounts for existing restorations and long-term oral stability.

  • Improve smile appearance
  • Correct shifting after previous braces
  • Address bite discomfort
  • Prepare for restorative treatment such as dental implants

Frequently Asked Questions

At what age should orthodontic treatment begin?

Children are often evaluated around age seven, though treatment timing varies depending on growth and alignment.

Can adults get braces?

Yes. Orthodontic treatment is effective for many adults, depending on oral health and alignment needs.

Do clear aligners work as well as braces?

Clear aligners can be effective for many cases, though complex bite corrections may require traditional braces.

Will orthodontics be painful?

Mild soreness is common after adjustments, but discomfort typically subsides within a few days.

How often are orthodontic visits required?

Follow-up visits are usually scheduled every few weeks to monitor progress and make adjustments.
