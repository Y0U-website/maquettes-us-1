Can a Broken Tooth Wait Until Monday?

A broken tooth may range from a minor chip to a deep fracture exposing inner tissue. While small chips may wait briefly, severe pain, swelling, or exposed pulp requires urgent evaluation. Recognizing warning signs helps determine whether immediate care is necessary.

Temporary Tooth Pain Relief vs Dangerous Delay

Temporary tooth pain relief methods such as pain relievers or cold compresses may reduce discomfort but do not treat the underlying cause. Delaying professional evaluation can allow infection or structural damage to worsen. Recognizing when relief measures are insufficient helps prevent complications.

Dental Abscess: Early vs Advanced Symptoms

A dental abscess is a pocket of infection that can begin with localized gum swelling and progress to facial swelling, fever, and severe pain. Recognizing early symptoms helps prevent complications. Advanced signs require urgent dental evaluation to reduce infection spread and protect overall health.

Signs a Tooth Infection Is Spreading

A tooth infection may spread beyond the tooth when bacteria move into surrounding tissues. Warning signs include facial swelling, fever, difficulty swallowing, and increasing pain. Recognizing these symptoms early helps ensure timely treatment and reduces the risk of more serious complications.

Tooth Pain With No Visible Cavity: What Could Be Causing It?

Tooth pain without a visible cavity can result from cracks, bite pressure imbalance, gum inflammation, or nerve irritation. Even when no decay is obvious, structural stress or pulp inflammation may be present. Proper evaluation helps identify the underlying cause and guide appropriate treatment.

When Is a Cracked Tooth an Emergency?

A cracked tooth becomes an emergency when pain is severe, swelling develops, or infection signs appear. While minor cracks may cause mild sensitivity, deeper fractures can expose inner tissue and lead to complications. Recognizing warning signs helps determine when urgent dental care is necessary.
