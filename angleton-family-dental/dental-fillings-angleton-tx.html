A dental filling is a restorative treatment used to repair a tooth after a small area of decay or damage has been removed. At Angleton Family Dental in Angleton, TX, fillings are shaped to rebuild the affected part of the tooth with care that helps the visit feel clear and manageable.

When Do You Need a Dental Filling?

A tooth may need attention when small changes start showing up during meals, brushing, or daily routines.

Signs of Tooth Sensitivity

Sensitivity can show up when a tooth reacts to sweets, cold drinks, hot foods, or light pressure. For a child, that might look like avoiding a favorite snack, pointing to one tooth, or suddenly chewing on the other side.

For adults and teens, sensitivity may feel like a quick zing or an uncomfortable twinge that keeps returning in the same spot. When that happens, Angleton Family Dental can take a closer look and help families in Angleton, TX understand what the tooth may need next.

Signs of Cavity Pain

Cavity pain can feel like an ache, soreness, or discomfort that does not go away as quickly as expected. It may become more noticeable during the day, after eating, or when a tooth has been bothered for a while.

Pain is one of those signs families should not have to guess through at home. A visit to Angleton Family Dental can help identify what is causing the discomfort and give parents, kids, and adults a clearer path forward.

Signs of Filling Damage

An older filling may need attention if it feels loose, looks cracked, seems worn down, or has fallen out. Some patients may also notice a rough edge, a changed surface, or a spot that feels different when the tongue passes over it.

These little changes can be easy to brush off, especially during busy family weeks. Angleton Family Dental can check the existing filling and determine whether the tooth needs fresh support.

Dental Inlays for Added Support

A dental inlay may be recommended when the damaged area is larger than what a small filling can properly support, but the tooth does not need wider surface coverage. It is designed to fit within the prepared area of the tooth and add support where the structure needs more help.

This option gives Angleton families another level of restorative care between a smaller filling and a broader repair. At Angleton Family Dental, inlays are considered based on the size, location, and condition of the damaged area.

Dental Onlays for Wider Coverage

A dental onlay may be used when more of the tooth’s biting surface or cusp area needs coverage. This type of repair can help address damage that extends beyond a smaller area while still focusing on the part of the tooth that needs care.

For patients in Angleton, TX, onlays can offer a more supportive option when the tooth needs broader protection across the chewing surface. Angleton Family Dental evaluates the tooth carefully to choose the repair that fits the level of damage.

FAQs About Dental Fillings

What are dental fillings used for?

Dental fillings are used to repair small areas of tooth decay or minor damage. After the damaged area is cleaned away, the filling restores the space so the tooth can continue supporting everyday use.

How do you know if you need a dental filling?

A dental filling may be needed when a tooth has sensitivity, cavity pain, visible dark spots, or damage to an older filling. Angleton Family Dental can check the tooth and explain whether a filling, inlay, onlay, or another restoration may be the better fit.

Do dental fillings hurt?

Dental fillings are usually completed with local anesthetic to help the tooth stay numb during treatment. Patients may feel pressure or movement, but the goal is to keep the visit manageable and easy to understand.

How long do dental fillings last?

The lifespan of a dental filling depends on the material, the size of the repair, the tooth’s location, and daily habits. Regular dental visits help the dentist check whether a filling is still sealed, smooth, and supporting the tooth properly.

What is the difference between a filling, inlay, and onlay?

A filling usually repairs a smaller area of tooth damage. An inlay can support a larger area within the tooth, while an onlay can cover more of the biting surface when the tooth needs broader support.
