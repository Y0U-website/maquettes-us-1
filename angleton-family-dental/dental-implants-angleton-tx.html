What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are small replacement tooth roots that can support a restored tooth after tooth loss. The implant acts as the anchor, while the implant crown or restoration is the visible tooth-shaped part that completes the smile.

An implant is not the same as the crown people see when they smile. The implant provides the hidden support, and the crown or restoration gives the smile its tooth-shaped finish.

FAQs About Dental Implants

What are dental implants used for?

Dental implants are used to support replacement teeth after tooth loss. They act as anchors for restorations such as implant crowns, implant bridges, or implant-supported dentures.

What is the difference between a dental implant and an implant crown?

A dental implant is the anchor placed below the visible smile. An implant crown is the tooth-shaped restoration attached above the implant to complete the replacement tooth.

Who may be a candidate for dental implants?

A patient may be considered for dental implants when they have missing teeth, healthy enough gums, and enough bone support for implant treatment. A dental evaluation is needed to determine whether dental implants or implant restoration may be appropriate.

How long do dental implants last?

Dental implants are designed for long-term tooth replacement support, but their longevity depends on oral health, bite forces, home care, and routine dental visits. The restoration attached to the implant, such as a crown, may need maintenance or replacement over time.

Are dental implants painful?

Patients may have soreness after implant-related treatment, but dental teams use comfort measures to help make care manageable. Angleton Family Dental can explain the restoration side of implant care and help patients understand what to expect for the visible replacement tooth.
