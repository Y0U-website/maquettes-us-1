Flossing with Braces: Navigating the Wires

Flossing with braces can be challenging, but it’s an essential step to remove plaque and food particles that a toothbrush can’t reach.

Proper care of your braces is crucial for achieving the best results and maintaining the health of your teeth and gums. By incorporating these brushing and flossing techniques into your daily routine, you can ensure that your smile remains bright and healthy throughout your orthodontic journey.

  • Rinse First: Rinsing with water before brushing can help loosen any food particles stuck in and around the braces.
  • Apply Fluoride Toothpaste: Use a pea-sized amount of fluoride toothpaste to help strengthen the tooth enamel and protect against cavities.
  • Brush Properly: Angle the brush at 45 degrees to clean around the wires and brackets. Brush each tooth individually, spending about 10 seconds per tooth. Be sure to clean all surfaces of the teeth and braces, including the fronts, sides, and chewing surfaces, as well as the gums.
  • Rinse Again: After brushing, rinse thoroughly with water to remove any dislodged particles and toothpaste residue.
  • Use the Right Tools: Wax-coated floss or a floss threader can make navigating floss through braces easier, reducing the risk of snapping the floss on the brackets or wires.
  • Thread Carefully: If using a floss threader, thread a segment of floss through the eye of the threader. Then, use the threader to guide the floss behind the wire. Gently move the floss up and down the sides of each tooth and under the gums, being careful not to snap the floss into the gums forcefully.
  • Consider a Water Flosser: For those who find traditional flossing too cumbersome, a water flosser can be an effective alternative. It uses a stream of pulsating water to remove plaque and food particles around the braces and between the teeth.
  • Avoid Sticky and Hard Foods: These can damage your braces or get stuck and be difficult to remove.
  • Use an Antiseptic Mouthwash: This can help reduce inflammation of the gums and rinse away particles that brushing and flossing might miss.
  • Visit Your Dentist Regularly: Regular check-ups and cleanings are vital to maintaining oral health and ensuring your orthodontic treatment is progressing as planned.
