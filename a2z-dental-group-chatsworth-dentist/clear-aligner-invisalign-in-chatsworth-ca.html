Types of Clear Aligners

There are several types of clear aligners available, depending on your specific clinical needs and the recommendations of your dentist or orthodontist.

Clear aligners are a series of custom-made, virtually invisible aligners that gradually straighten your teeth. During your treatment, you will wear a set of aligners for about one to two weeks before moving on to the next set in the series. This progressive approach ensures that your teeth are gently and steadily moved into their desired positions.

In some cases, your dentist may place temporary attachments, known as engagers, on your teeth. These tiny, tooth-colored bumps help the aligners exert the correct amount of pressure on specific areas of your teeth, enhancing the effectiveness of the treatment.

How Do Clear Aligners Work?

The clear aligner treatment process begins with a comprehensive consultation and assessment. During this initial visit, your dentist will take detailed digital scans or impressions of your teeth to create a precise treatment plan. This plan will outline the gradual movements your teeth will make, and you’ll receive a series of custom-made aligners to wear throughout the treatment.

You will need to wear your aligners for 20-22 hours each day, only removing them for meals and oral hygiene. Each set of aligners will be worn for one to two weeks before progressing to the next set, gradually guiding your teeth into their ideal positions.

Clear Braces vs. Traditional Braces

Clear aligners offer several advantages over traditional metal braces:

Aesthetics

Clear aligners are nearly invisible, making them an ideal choice for patients who want a discreet orthodontic solution.

Comfort

The smooth plastic of clear aligners reduces the risk of irritation to the gums and cheeks, which is common with metal braces.

Convenience

Being removable, clear aligners allow for easier eating, drinking, and maintaining oral hygiene compared to fixed braces.

Flexibility

Clear aligners are suitable for treating a variety of mild to moderate orthodontic issues without the need for complex adjustments often required by metal braces.
