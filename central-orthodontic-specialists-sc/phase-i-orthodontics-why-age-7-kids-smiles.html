Why age 7 is the magic number for Phase I

At about age 7, kids usually have a mix of baby teeth and adult teeth, which gives specialists a clear window into how the jaw is growing and how teeth are lining up. That is why orthodontics for kids often starts with an evaluation at this age, not because every child needs treatment right away, but because the timing is clinically powerful.

What Phase I orthodontics can accomplish

Phase I, also called early interceptive treatment, guides growth while your child is still developing. The goal is to set up a healthier bite, better airway support, and a more predictable path for future alignment.

FAQ

Q: Does every child need Phase I orthodontics at age 7? A: No. Many kids simply need monitoring, but orthodontics for kids at age 7 gives us the best timing to spot growth issues early and act only if it helps.

Q: What appliances are common in Phase I? A: Expanders and limited braces for kids are common, along with habit guidance when needed. Our goal is targeted, efficient change, not lengthy treatment.

Q: Will Phase I prevent the need for braces later? A: Sometimes, but not always. More often it makes Phase II shorter and smoother, especially for crowding and bite correction in orthodontics for kids.

Q: How long does Phase I treatment usually take? A: Many plans run about 9 to 18 months, followed by a rest period for growth and adult teeth to come in.
