What Are Dental Implants?

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

What steps will need to be taken to replace your missing teeth with trustworthy dental implant posts? Knowing what to expect is critical before you commit to the process of getting dental implants; after all, it can take several months (or even a year in some cases), and the results are intended to last for a long time. Here’s a look at the four main stages of getting dental implants.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

First, you will need to schedule a consultation with our team. We’ll need to figure out whether you can have dental implants placed; to that end, we’ll use advanced dental technology to gather information about your mouth and jaw.

After carefully considering your situation, we’ll discuss your candidacy for dental implants. Sometimes we may find that certain preliminary procedures are required. For instance, if signs of gum disease are found, the issue will need to be treated before you can receive dental implants. Other preliminary services that you might need include bone grafting and tooth extractions.

Dental Implant Surgery

Dr. Carroll works in order to allow our patients to have the entire dental implant process completed in one location. As such, the surgery to place your dental implants can be carried out right here at our office.

Each dental implant post needs to be inserted into carefully chosen spots in your jawbone. Your gums will be opened for the procedure, and they will be closed afterward via sutures. A cap will be attached to each implant post to help the gums maintain their shape while your mouth is healing.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

After the surgery comes osseointegration. This is where the implant posts gradually fuse with the surrounding bone tissue, forming a lasting bond. The timeline for osseointegration isn’t set in stone, but it should typically be completed in a matter of months.

When the dental implants are firmly attached to the bone, they’ll be ready to receive metal connector pieces called abutments. This is an important step; without the abutments, we wouldn’t be able to secure your final restoration in place.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s)

A dental implant restoration could be a crown, a bridge, or a denture; it all depends on the extent of your tooth loss. Each type of prosthesis can be made out of natural-looking materials, so you can rest assured that you’ll have a beautiful new smile at the end of the process. The prosthesis will be customized just for you by using impressions of your grin.

During your last dental implant appointment, we will carefully attach the completed restoration to the posts. A last-minute check will be performed to confirm that your new teeth aren’t causing any problems with your bite. If everything looks good, you can start using your completed dental implants to eat, speak, and smile.

Benefits of Dental Implants

There are countless benefits of dental implants that patients can enjoy, which is why they’re a highly recommended procedure for those who have lost one or more natural teeth. When tooth roots are no longer there to stimulate the jawbone, it deteriorates over time. Unfortunately, traditional bridges and dentures do nothing to stop this process, but dental implants go deeper underneath the gums and replace the roots as well as the crowns. This lifelike structure enables implants to keep the jawbone strong and paves the way for unique benefits you won’t find with other tooth replacements.

Day-to-Day Benefits

Regardless of which replacement option you choose, you’ll need to take care of your prosthetic teeth. However, compared to traditional dentures, dental implants are simple to maintain. They require the same care and attention you should give to natural teeth, meaning you just need to brush and floss them every day. Additionally, the implant posts, which act like tooth roots, keep the jawbone stimulated and strong. As a result, you retain your youthful, natural-looking facial shape instead of suffering from the shrinking, sunken look that normally comes with tooth loss.

Health Benefits

Did you know that dental implants can help you stay healthy? In fact, studies have shown that those who have lost their natural teeth have a shorter life expectancy, but dental implants can help! Keeping a healthy diet is an essential part of your quality of life, but if you’ve ever had traditional dentures, you know how difficult it can be to eat nutritious foods with replacement teeth. Dental implants are comfortable, stable, and secure, allowing you to eat basically whatever you choose, including nutrient-rich vegetables and fruits.

Long-Term Benefits

Unlike traditional restorations that last between seven and 10 years, dental implants have a lifespan of over 35 years with the proper maintenance. That means that you’ll spend less time in the dentist’s office and more time enjoying your new smile! In addition to saving you time, these long-lasting tooth replacements may also help you save money. Although implants come with a larger price tag upfront, you don’t have to pay to replace your teeth over and over again. After 30 or more years, you may very likely find that your investment in implants was the smarter financial choice.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Dental Implant Post-Op Instructions

A crucial step in the dental implant process is the surgical placement of the implant posts into your jawbone. Following this procedure, it’s incredibly important to take proper care to ensure a smooth and problem-free recovery. Being well-prepared can make a big difference, so here’s a brief overview of some key steps to follow after your surgery.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

Preventing a dry socket should be one of your main priorities after dental implant surgery. This occurs when the blood clot at the implant site becomes dislodged, leading to significant pain and a slower recovery. To protect the clot, avoid actions like drinking through a straw, smoking, or touching the implant site directly.

Common Side-Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

It’s completely normal to experience some side effects following oral surgery, including dental implant placement. Over the next several days, you may notice the following:

These side effects should gradually subside within about a week. If they persist or worsen, don’t hesitate to contact us.

  • Occasional Bleeding Light bleeding is common and can be managed by gently applying pressure to the treatment area with a gauze pad.
  • Facial Swelling Swelling typically peaks within the first 72 hours but may persist for up to a week. Using cold compresses can help reduce swelling and provide relief.
  • Mild Discomfort Over the counter or prescribed pain medication can be used to ease any discomfort during recovery.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

To protect your treatment site, it’s important to avoid tough, crunchy, chewy, spicy, or acidic foods for the next several days. Instead, stick to soft, gentle foods that are easy to eat as you heal. Here are some great options that will help keep you comfortable and support a smooth recovery process:

  • Yogurt
  • Ice cream
  • Applesauce
  • Smoothies (without straws)
  • Scrambled eggs
  • Cold or room-temperature soup
  • Soft-cooked pasta (avoid al dente)
  • Mashed potatoes
  • Boiled vegetables

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

You should continue your oral care routine after surgery. While you can brush and floss as usual, be careful around the treatment site. Additionally, rinse your mouth gently using either a prescribed oral rinse from our office or a saltwater solution. Avoid mouthwashes containing alcohol, as they can cause dry mouth.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

Once your implant has fully fused with your jawbone and the abutment is in place, your permanent restoration can be attached. It’s normal to experience slight sensitivity in the gum tissue around your implant, which can typically be managed with pain medication. However, there shouldn’t be any bleeding, swelling, or significant recovery needed after placing your crown, bridge, or denture. If these symptoms do occur, contact us right away.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

You may think that once you get implants all your dental concerns are behind you. Well, think again! Your implants require just as much care and maintenance as natural teeth if you want them to continue functioning year after year. By making wise decisions in your oral healthcare, you can enjoy your replacement teeth for many years to come!

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

If you don’t already brush and floss your teeth daily, you need to start now! These small habits in your routine play a major role in the success of your implants. Plaque can still accumulate on your restorations and cause infection. Without frequent cleanings, the bacteria in plaque can irritate and infect the gum tissue, potentially compromising your implants in the process. By taking just a few minutes every day for oral hygiene, you can extend the life of your dental implants.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Choosing healthier foods doesn’t just improve your overall health it can help your replacement teeth last longer. While they will not become decayed, too much sugary or starchy food or drinks can encourage the growth of bacteria that cause gum disease. As a result, your gums can pull away from your implants, causing failure. However, making smarter decisions in your diet can help keep these bacteria under control.

Break Bad Habits

You probably already know that smoking or using tobacco is a harmful habit, but there are plenty of others that can damage your new teeth. For instance, if you chew on ice, pencils, or your fingernails, you put them at risk of failure. Opening packaging with your teeth is another bad habit that can potentially cause your dental implants to fail. Try to stop these habits to reduce the wear and tear exerted on your dental implants and prolong their lifespan.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Your dental implants, as well as the restorations on top, are designed to be pretty durable, but they aren’t completely indestructible. Unconscious habits, like grinding or clenching your teeth while you sleep, can weaken the solid foundation your implants are supposed to provide. In this case, we can create a custom night guard to protect your replacement teeth from long-term damage. Similarly, when you participate in sports or activities where you could get hit in the face, it’s always a good idea to wear a mouthguard, just in case.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Even if your dental implants replace every natural tooth in your smile, you still need to come to our dental office on a regular basis for checkups and cleanings. These visits help us keep tabs on your implants, catch problems as early as possible, and remove plaque buildup. This essential preventive appointment allows you to keep your smile on track for a long, long time.
