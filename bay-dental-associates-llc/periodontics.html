How We Care for Your Gums

Every patient’s gums tell a slightly different story, so our team takes the time to understand exactly where you are before recommending next steps. For gingivitis and earlier stages of gum disease, a gentle deep cleaning called scaling and root planing is often all it takes to clear away buildup below the gumline and let your gums heal and reattach. We monitor your progress closely and adjust your care plan as needed, so early gum concerns rarely need to become bigger problems.

For more advanced cases, treatment may involve options like periodontal surgery, soft tissue grafting, crown lengthening, or bone grafting to help rebuild bone that has been affected by more advanced gum disease. We will always walk you through what a diagnosis means, what your options look like, and what to expect at each step, so you feel informed no matter what stage of treatment you are at.

Gum Health and Dental Implants

Healthy gums and a solid foundation of bone are an important part of a successful dental implant, which is why we like to think of gum health and restorative planning as two parts of the same story. If you are considering dental implants but currently have gum disease, we will usually want to help your gums heal first before moving forward, so your treatment plan is built for lasting results.

A Few Simple Ways to Protect Your Gums

The best news about gum disease is that it is largely preventable. Brushing twice a day, flossing daily, and keeping up with your regular cleanings go a long way toward keeping your gums healthy for years to come. Seeing us for a checkup at least once a year also gives us the chance to catch any early changes long before you would ever notice them yourself, which usually means simpler, gentler care for you.
