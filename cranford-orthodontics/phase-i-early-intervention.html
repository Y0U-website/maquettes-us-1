Signs Your Child May Need Early Orthodontic Care

Not every child needs Phase I treatment, but certain warning signs may indicate that early evaluation is beneficial.

Parents should consider an orthodontic consultation if a child has:

Our orthodontists evaluate both teeth alignment and jaw development to determine the best course of action.

  • Difficulty biting or chewing
  • Early or late loss of baby teeth
  • Crowded or blocked teeth
  • Thumb sucking beyond early childhood
  • Teeth that stick out significantly
  • Crossbite, underbite, or open bite
  • Jaw shifting or clicking
  • Mouth breathing or speech concerns

What to Expect During Phase I Treatment

Early orthodontic treatment typically occurs between ages 7 and 10 , while the mouth and jaw are still developing.

Comprehensive Evaluation

Your child’s first visit includes a full orthodontic exam, digital scans, and growth assessment. Our doctors will determine whether treatment should begin now or if monitoring is recommended.

Customized Treatment Plan

If Phase I treatment is beneficial, we create a personalized treatment plan designed to guide development and address specific bite concerns.

Active Treatment

Treatment may involve orthodontic appliances that help guide tooth positioning and jaw growth while permanent teeth continue to develop.

Monitoring & Growth Guidance

Once Phase I treatment is complete, we monitor your child’s development until the remaining permanent teeth erupt. If necessary, a second phase of orthodontic treatment may be recommended later.

Phase I vs. Comprehensive Orthodontic Treatment

Phase I treatment addresses developmental concerns early , but it does not always eliminate the need for orthodontic treatment later.

Many children will still receive Phase II treatment during adolescence once all permanent teeth have erupted. However, early intervention can often make future treatment:

In some cases, early treatment may help reduce the need for tooth extractions or more complex orthodontic procedures later.

  • Shorter
  • Simpler
  • More predictable

Who Is a Good Candidate for Phase I Treatment?

Early orthodontic treatment may benefit children who:

Our orthodontists carefully evaluate each child’s development before recommending early intervention.

  • Are between ages 7–10
  • Have developing bite problems
  • Show signs of jaw growth imbalance
  • Have severe crowding or blocked teeth
  • Have crossbite or underbite issues
  • Have protruding front teeth at risk of injury

When Early Treatment May Not Be Necessary

Many children do not require immediate orthodontic treatment even if their teeth appear slightly crowded or uneven.

In these cases, our orthodontists may recommend growth monitoring until the appropriate time for comprehensive treatment. Monitoring allows us to track changes while avoiding unnecessary early appliances.

This approach ensures every child receives treatment only when it provides the greatest benefit .
