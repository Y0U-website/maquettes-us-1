Frequently Asked Questions About Immediate Dental Implants

Am I a candidate for immediate dental implants?

Most patients who need a single tooth extracted are good candidates for immediate dental implants. Ideal candidates have sufficient jawbone density, good overall health, and no active gum disease or infection. Dr. Yuabov will evaluate your oral health with a comprehensive exam and 3D imaging at Bedford Dental to determine if immediate implant placement is right for you. Even patients who have been told they are not candidates may qualify — advances in implant technology have expanded eligibility significantly.

Is the immediate implant procedure painful?

The immediate dental implant procedure is performed under local anesthesia, so you won’t feel pain during the surgery. Most patients are surprised by how comfortable the process is. After the procedure, you can expect mild soreness and swelling for a few days, which is easily managed with over-the-counter pain relievers. Dr. Yuabov will provide detailed post-operative care instructions to ensure a smooth and comfortable recovery.

How do immediate implants compare to traditional dental implants?

Traditional dental implants require multiple appointments spread over several months — first the implant is placed, then a healing period follows before the crown is attached. Immediate implants allow the implant post and a temporary crown to be placed in a single visit, the same day as your extraction. The final result is the same long-lasting, natural-looking tooth replacement, but with far fewer appointments and a faster path to a complete smile. Both options offer excellent outcomes; immediate implants simply make the process more convenient for qualifying patients.

How long do immediate dental implants last?

Immediate dental implants are designed to be a permanent tooth replacement solution. With proper care — regular brushing, flossing, and routine dental checkups — implants can last 20 to 30 years or even a lifetime. The implant post fuses with the jawbone through a process called osseointegration, creating a stable, long-lasting foundation. The crown placed on top may need to be replaced after 10 to 15 years due to normal wear.
