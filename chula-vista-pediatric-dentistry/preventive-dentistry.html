Digital X-Rays

Digital x-rays are fast, high-resolution images that capture your child’s jaw and tooth health. X-rays will only be recommended if your child’s dentist thinks they are necessary. For most cases, x-rays are taken every few years, however, your child’s dentist may recommend more if your child is at high risk for oral disease.

Pediatric Dentistry

Silver Diamine Fluoride

Cavities and toothaches are never fun, especially when it&#x27;s your child suffering. And when it comes to treating decay in baby teeth, you may be wondering if there are alternatives to dental fillings. Silver Diamine Fluoride (SDF) is a non-invasive procedure that doesn&#x27;t require any drills or shots, and can be completed in minutes. Painting on a layer of SDF to the affected tooth not only kills the bacteria that causes decay, but also seals and remineralizes the tooth for added strength. If your little one has a cavity, fillings may not be the only option!

Preventive Dentistry

Dental Sealants

Dental sealants use a transparent liquid resin, which is applied directly to the teeth and is hardened using a UV light. This creates an impenetrable seal on your child’s tooth, which stops bacteria, acid, and food particles from causing cavities. This treatment is usually recommended specifically for cavity-prone rear teeth.

Preventive Dentistry

Oral Cancer Screenings

Even children need oral cancer screenings! This is an essential part of preventive care that can be performed quickly, safely, and usually without your child noticing.

During the screening, the dentist will examine your child’s mouth, gums, and oral tissues. They’ll look for discolored patches, lumps, growths, and other similar abnormalities. In the rare case that an issue is found, a biopsy may be taken and sent to a specialist for further analysis, or the dentist will refer you to a specialist directly.

Scheduling a dental visit every 6 months allows your child’s dentist to address any issues before they become larger problems.

Frequently Asked Questions

At what age should my child start seeing a pediatric dentist?

The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry recommends that a child should visit the dentist by their first birthday, or when their first tooth appears, whichever comes first. Regular dental visits should begin by age two. Starting dental visits early helps your child get comfortable with the dentist, allows for early detection of any potential issues, and sets the foundation for a lifetime of healthy oral hygiene habits. Why is pediatric preventative dentistry important?

Pediatric preventative dentistry is crucial for several reasons. First, it helps to establish good oral hygiene habits from an early age, which can last a lifetime. Second, regular check-ups can catch potential issues early, such as cavities or alignment problems, allowing for early intervention and often simpler, less invasive treatments. Lastly, preventative care can help to avoid more serious oral health issues down the line, such as gum disease or tooth loss.

What is pediatric preventative dentistry?

Pediatric preventative dentistry is a specialized area of dentistry that focuses on maintaining the oral health of children. This includes regular dental check-ups, cleanings, fluoride treatments, dental sealants, and education on proper oral hygiene habits.
