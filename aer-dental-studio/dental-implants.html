Dental Implants

Unlike traditional bridges that require altering adjacent healthy teeth, single-tooth implants stand independently, preserving your natural dental structure. This independent support system also helps maintain bone health in your jaw, preventing the bone loss that typically occurs after tooth loss.

  • Abutment — connects the implant post to the crown
  • Custom crown — the visible portion designed to match your natural teeth perfectly

2. Multiple Implants

Multiple dental implants work the same way as a single-tooth implant, but scaled to replace two or more missing teeth in different areas of your mouth. Each implant consists of:

Rather than relying on a single appliance to span several gaps, multiple implants give each missing tooth its own independent, permanent foundation.

  • Titanium post — Surgically placed into the jawbone to serve as an artificial tooth root
  • Abutment — A connector that anchors the visible portion of the tooth
  • Custom crown — A natural-looking restoration matched to the size, shape, and shade of your existing teeth

3. Teeth in a Day Full Arch Implants

Full Arch Dental Implants is a full-arch tooth replacement system that uses four to six strategically placed implants to support an entire row of teeth: upper, lower, or both. The four to six implants are angled to maximize contact with available bone, which means most patients don’t need bone grafting, even if they’ve experienced bone loss from periodontal disease or long-term tooth loss.

The result is a fixed, non-removable set of teeth that looks, feels, and functions like a natural smile. If you’ve been told in the past that you don’t have enough bone, we have options for patients with very little or no bone.

Are Implants Safe?

Dental implants have been used for decades and have an exceptional safety record. The materials used—primarily titanium and ceramic—are biocompatible, meaning they’re well-accepted by the human body. In fact, dental implants have one of the highest success rates of any dental procedure, with success rates of 95-98 % for healthy patients.

Are Implants Worth It?

When considering the value of dental implants, it’s important to take a long-term perspective. While the initial cost may be higher than alternatives like bridges or dentures, implants often prove more affordable over time. Traditional options typically require replacement every 5-10 years, while implants can last a lifetime with proper care.

Beyond financial considerations, our patients consistently tell us that the improvements in comfort, confidence, and quality of life are invaluable. Being able to eat, speak, and smile without concern is something many of our patients describe as priceless. When you factor in the long-term benefits of preserved bone health and facial structure, dental implants represent an investment in your overall well-being.
