What is a Dental Implant?

Dental implants consist of three main components. This includes the titanium post that integrates with your bone tissue, the restoration that completes your smile, and the abutment that reliably attaches the two components together. Each component plays an important role in mimicking your previous tooth. Everything from the esthetics to the function is accounted for, so you can smile and eat all the foods you enjoy with confidence.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

While dental implants offer an incredibly comprehensive solution for tooth loss, they do involve a lengthy process, which can take a few months to complete. Even so, the advantages you will have with these titanium posts will make your investment worthwhile. Because we can complete the entire procedure from start to finish, including the surgical portion, we can ensure that you receive the best care for restoring your smile. Initial Dental Implant Consultation

The beginning of your treatment will always start with your initial consultation. During this visit, our team will evaluate your oral health, the surrounding facial structures, and your smile. This will allow us to verify if you’re an eligible candidate for the procedure. This appointment will also be the perfect time to ask any questions you may have, as we’ll be more than happy to walk you through the entire process and discuss the overall cost.

Some people might require preliminary treatments before moving forward with getting dental implants. This may involve gum disease therapy, bone grafting, or even tooth extractions. Once we’ve ensured that your oral health is in good condition for the titanium posts, we can develop the remainder of your treatment plan.

Dental Implant Surgery

Your oral surgeon will begin your procedure by completely numbing the areas in your mouth with a local anesthetic. They may even provide you with sedation dentistry to keep you relaxed and stress-free throughout your treatment. They’ll then make a tiny incision in your gums to reach the jawbone. Your implants will be properly placed in their pre-planned locations and angles before your gums are sutured around them. You’ll receive a protective dental cap above the implant post to keep it safe while you recover.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

Once you’ve undergone your placement procedure, you’ll have to allow your implants to complete osseointegration for the next several months. This is the process where the metal posts fuse with your jawbone, becoming one with your facial structure and creating the stable foundation necessary for successful restorations that can last a lifetime. When this process is finished, we’ll place your abutments atop the implants and give your gums some time to heal.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s)

After you’ve recovered from getting your abutments, you’ll come back to our office to receive your restoration(s). During this visit, we’ll place your new dental crown, bridge, or denture atop the metal connectors, ensuring that they’re well-fitted and feel natural for you. Once we’ve confirmed that your bite is comfortable and you’re satisfied with the results, you’ll be on your way to enjoying your brand-new pearly whites!

Benefits of Dental Implants

When you replace a tooth with a dental implant, it becomes part of your existing oral structures. This includes your bone tissue, just like your natural tooth roots. After the implant has finished the process known as osseointegration, you’ll be able to chew even the toughest of foods without worry. Steak, apples, nuts, and even chewing gum can all be enjoyed once you’ve completed treatment. Furthermore, success rates are typically as high as 95 percent, even after ten years of use! They often last a lifetime after placement, making them one of the best long-term solutions for tooth loss.

Dental Implants Post-Op Instructions

Once your dental implants have been placed, your mouth will need time to fully recover. To make sure that everything goes as planned during the healing process, you’ll be given some instructions to follow. It always helps to be prepared ahead of time, so here is a brief overview of what you can typically expect after dental implant surgery and what you’ll need to do to take care of your mouth.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

As part of the healing process, a blood clot will form at the surgical site. It’s important to make sure that this blood clot stays in place; otherwise, you could experience a painful dry socket that slows down the healing process. To protect your blood clot, you need to avoid doing any of the following immediately after dental implant placement:

  • Drinking through a straw.
  • Using cigarettes or any other tobacco products.
  • Directly touching the implant site with your tongue.

Common Side Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

In the days following your dental implant surgery, you are likely to experience some mild discomfort in your mouth. Additionally, there will likely be swelling in your gums, and intermittent bleeding may occur. As your mouth recovers, these symptoms should go away on their own. In the meantime, here are some tips for managing them:

  • Take ibuprofen or another type of over-the-counter pain medication to reduce your discomfort.
  • Hold a cold compress over any swollen areas for 15-minute intervals.
  • If there’s any bleeding, you can stop it by applying gentle pressure to the area with a piece of gauze.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

For at least a few days after your surgery, you’ll need to maintain a diet of soft and liquid foods. Mashed potatoes, scrambled eggs, yogurt, pasta, smoothies, and soups that aren’t too hot are all examples of foods that you should be able to safely enjoy while your mouth is recovering. When you’re ready to start eating more solid foods again, be sure to keep them away from your implant posts while you chew them.

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

You should be able to brush and floss your teeth like you normally would during the recovery process, but you will need to be extra gentle when cleaning around the implant site. If you want to minimize the risk of an infection occurring while you’re healing, you should make a point of rinsing your mouth with salt water two or three times daily.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

A fully customized crown, bridge, or denture will eventually be attached to your implant posts in order to complete the process of rebuilding your smile. At this point, your mouth will have fully healed from your dental implant surgery, so there shouldn’t be any swelling or bleeding; at most, there may be some mild sensitivity in your gums. If any unexpected symptoms occur after your new teeth have been attached, please call our office right away.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Getting dental implants is exciting! By caring for them properly, they can last upwards of three decades or even a lifetime! This makes them the longest-lasting tooth replacement option on the market. However, they will only last when you maintain them correctly. Here are some steps that you should be taking.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Dental implants can’t get cavities like normal teeth can, but you still need to practice excellent oral hygiene. In addition to keeping your surrounding teeth in good shape, sticking to an excellent oral hygiene routine can prevent other dental problems, like gum disease, that can cause your implants to fail. Remember to brush, floss, and rinse with mouthwash every day.

Eat a Healthy Diet

One of the many benefits of getting dental implants is the fact that you don’t need to worry about any eating restrictions. You can easily enjoy nutritious meals without struggling to chew. Just remember to brush, floss and rinse with mouthwash every day.

Break Bad Habits

Refrain from smoking or using other tobacco products immediately after your dental implant placement. This can slow down healing and increase your risk of developing an infection. Don’t use your teeth as tools either. You could end up accidentally damaging your dental implants or surrounding teeth. Instead, try to find some scissors, pliers, a bottle opener, or another tooth that can help you complete your task safely.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Your dental implants may be strong, but they aren’t completely indestructible. You should always wear a mouthguard to protect your dental implants and natural teeth when participating in sports. If you grind your teeth at night, ask about a nightguard to protect your implants from additional wear and tear.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

You should always attend regular cleanings and checkups twice a year. This way, we can monitor your dental implant and spot any issues as early as possible. By catching potential problems now, you can keep them from becoming more serious and leading to greater complications.
