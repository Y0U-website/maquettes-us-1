Types of Dental Implants

Single-Tooth Implants

When you’re missing just one tooth, a single implant provides the most natural-looking and functioning replacement. The titanium post is surgically placed in your jawbone, topped with an abutment, and finished with a custom-crafted crown that blends seamlessly with your natural teeth.

All-on-4 Implants This innovative solution allows us to replace an entire arch of teeth using just four strategically placed implants. The All-on-4 technique offers tremendous benefits:

  • Full arch restoration with minimal implants
  • Often possible even with reduced bone density
  • Many patients qualify for same-day teeth with this approach
  • More affordable than replacing each tooth individually
  • Prevents the sunken facial appearance often associated with dentures

Dental Implants Pros and Cons

Advantages

  • Longevity: With proper care, implants can last a lifetime
  • Bone Preservation: Implants stimulate bone growth, preventing deterioration
  • Natural Function: Eat, speak, and smile with confidence
  • Independence: No need for adhesives or removal for cleaning
  • Oral Health: No need to alter adjacent healthy teeth (unlike bridges)
  • Aesthetics: Look and feel like your natural teeth

Considerations

  • Initial Investment: Higher upfront cost than temporary solutions
  • Treatment Time: The standard implant process may take several months
  • Surgical Procedure: While minor, implant placement is a surgical process
  • Health Requirements: Best results in patients with good overall health

Are Implants Worth It?

The investment in dental implants delivers returns in quality of life that continue year after year.

  • Elimination of ongoing costs for adhesives, special cleaners, and frequent replacements
  • Prevention of bone loss that leads to costly reconstructive procedures
  • Avoiding damage to adjacent teeth that often requires additional treatment
  • Improved nutrition and health from being able to eat a wider variety of foods
  • Enhanced self-confidence in professional and social situations
  • Superior comfort without the slipping or irritation common with removable options

Dental Implant FAQ

Q. How long do dental implants last?

A. With proper oral hygiene and regular dental care, implants can last a lifetime, while the crowns may need replacement after 15-20 years.

Q. Is the implant procedure painful?

A. Most patients report minimal discomfort during and after the procedure. We offer various sedation options to ensure your comfort.

Q. Can I get implants if I’ve been missing teeth for years?

A. Yes! Even with long-term tooth loss, techniques like bone grafting can prepare your jaw for successful implant placement.

Q. How do I care for my implants?

A. Implants require the same care as natural teeth: brushing, flossing, and regular dental check-ups.
