What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are small posts placed in the jawbone to support replacement teeth. Depending on your needs, implants can support a single crown, a removable snap-in denture, or a fixed full-arch restoration.

The goal is tooth replacement in a way that restores function, feels stable, and fits your long-term oral health plan.

A Quick Look at Each Option

These descriptions are meant to inform you, not to determine your treatment. Your anatomy, bone density, health history, and other factors all influence what’s actually possible. That’s what the exam is for.

Implant Supported Teeth (Hybridge)

A permanent, full-arch solution for patients missing most or all of their teeth. Anchored directly to the jawbone using a series of implants. Burns uses the Hybridge system, a leading full-arch implant solution. Functions and feels like natural teeth. Not removable.

Snap-in Dentures

For patients who currently wear traditional dentures and want more stability. Anchored to implants but still removable by the patient for cleaning. A step above traditional dentures.

Single Implants

For patients missing one or two individual teeth with healthy surrounding teeth and adequate bone. The implant replaces the root, and a crown is placed on top. The closest thing to a natural tooth replacement available.

Dental Implants FAQs

What are dental implants?

Dental implants are small posts placed in the jawbone to support replacement teeth. They can support a single dental crown, snap-in denture, bridge, or fixed full-arch restoration depending on your needs.

What dental implant options does Burns Dentistry offer?

Burns Dentistry offers several implant solutions, including implant-supported teeth through Hybridge, snap-in dentures, and single dental implants. The right option depends on your anatomy, bone density, health history, number of missing teeth, and long-term goals.

Are dental implants permanent?

Dental implant restorations are fixed and permanent and cannot be removed by the patient, including single tooth implants and many full-arch implant-supported teeth. Snap-in dentures attach to implants for stability but the denture is removable by the patient for cleaning.

What is the difference between single implants, snap-in dentures, and implant-supported teeth?

Single implants replace one or two individual missing teeth. Snap-in dentures attach to implants but remain removable. Implant-supported teeth replace all teeth in an arch with a fixed restoration supported by multiple implants.

How do I know if I am a candidate for dental implants?

Dental implant candidacy is determined through an exam and imaging. We look at your jawbone, oral health, medical history, number of missing teeth, and the type of restoration you want before recommending a treatment path.

Do dental implants help with bone loss?

Dental implants can help preserve jawbone by replacing the missing tooth root and providing support for a restoration. The amount of bone support depends on your anatomy, the number of implants, and the type of restoration used.

Is the dental implant process handled in-house?

Yes. Burns Dentistry handles consultation, imaging, surgery, and final restoration in-house, so you are not coordinating between multiple providers.

What happens at the first implant consultation?

The first step is an exam and imaging. Dr. Burns gets the full picture of your bone structure, oral health, and goals, then explains which implant options are realistic for your situation.
