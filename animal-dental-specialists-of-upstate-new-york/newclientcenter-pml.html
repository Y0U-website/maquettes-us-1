Cardiopulmonary resuscitation, or CPR, is an emergency procedure performed in an attempt to restore the blood circulation and breathing to a patient who has suffered from cardiac arrest. DNR, which stands for do not resuscitate, tells the medical team NOT to perform CPR on a patient. This means that if a patient stops breathing or the heartbeat stops, we will NOT attempt emergency CPR. Statistics indicate that despite an initial 58 % survival rate after CPR, the discharge survival rate is only 5-7 % in dogs and 1-19 % in cats.
