When Is Sinus Lift Surgery Necessary?

  • The maxillary sinuses are air-filled cavities located above the upper back teeth. Some individuals naturally have large sinuses or thin bones in this area. Without sufficient bone height, implants cannot be securely anchored, increasing the risk of implant failure or complications.
  • Advanced gum disease can lead to bone loss in the jaw, affecting the bone’s ability to support dental implants. In such cases, sinus lift surgery helps rebuild the lost bone structure, providing a stable foundation for implants.
  • When teeth are extracted, especially molars and premolars in the upper jaw, the bone surrounding the extraction site may resorb over time. Sinus lift surgery may be necessary to restore the bone volume needed for future implant placement.
  • Dental implants require a minimum amount of healthy bone for successful integration and support. Sinus lift surgery ensures adequate bone height and volume to accommodate implants of appropriate size and length.
  • Without sufficient bone height, there is a risk that the implants could protrude into the sinus cavity during placement. Sinus lift surgery creates a barrier between the sinus membrane and the implant site, reducing this risk and ensuring the safety of the sinus cavity. Call us to learn more.

The Procedure for Sinus Lift Surgery

Sinus lift surgery in Alabaster, AL, Carrollton, GA, Anniston, AL, and Gadsden, AL, also known as sinus augmentation, is a specialized procedure designed to increase bone height in the upper jaw, specifically in the posterior maxilla where the molars and premolars are located. This surgical intervention becomes necessary when there is insufficient bone volume to support dental implants due to bone loss following tooth extractions, periodontal disease, or the natural anatomy of the sinus cavities.

The procedure begins with thorough pre-operative planning, including diagnostic imaging such as X-rays and CT scans, to assess the bone structure and sinus anatomy. On the day of surgery, patients are typically administered local anesthesia with sedation to ensure comfort throughout the procedure. General anesthesia may be considered for more complex cases or anxious patients.

The oral surgeon makes a small incision in the gum tissue to expose the jawbone beneath during the surgery. A precise window is then created in the bone to access the sinus membrane. Moving this membrane upward creates a space between the sinus floor and the jawbone. This space is filled with bone graft material, which may be sourced from the patient’s own body (autogenous), a donor (allogenic), an animal (xenogenic), or synthetic material. The bone graft is a scaffold designed to stimulate new bone growth over time.

After the bone graft is placed, the incision in the gum tissue is closed with stitches. Post-operatively, patients are closely monitored to manage any discomfort, swelling, or minor bleeding. Antibiotics and pain medications are often prescribed to aid in healing and prevent infection. Strict adherence to post-operative care instructions, such as avoiding strenuous activities and maintaining good oral hygiene, is crucial for successful recovery.

Following sinus lift surgery, a healing period of several months is necessary to allow the bone graft to integrate with the natural bone. During this time, regular follow-up appointments are scheduled to monitor progress with imaging studies and assess readiness for dental implant placement.

Sinus lift surgery provides a reliable solution for patients with deficient bone in the upper jaw, enabling them to achieve stable and long-lasting results with dental implants and restoring both function and aesthetics to their smile.
