The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

As one of the most comprehensive and reliable methods for replacing missing teeth, dental implants require a multistep process that can span several months. Though this might seem lengthy at first, your lifelike and long-lasting results will make them worthwhile. Better yet, you’ll be able to undergo the entire treatment at our practice, meaning you can receive care from a team you already know and trust! Until then, here are the four main steps of getting dental implants so you know what to expect.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

Dental Implant Surgery

We’ll begin your dental implant procedure by completely numbing your mouth with a local anesthetic. A small incision will then be made in your gum tissue, allowing us to reach your jawbone. We’ll strategically place the metal posts in their preplanned positions and then suture your gums around them. You’ll be given protective dental caps to keep the implants safe while you go home and recover from the treatment.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

Your recovery should take a few months, especially due to osseointegration. This is the process where your dental implants fuse to your jawbone, providing the stable foundation necessary to support your restorations for many years to come. Once osseointegration is successful, you’ll come back to our office to receive your abutments small metal connectors that will link your restorations to your implants. You’ll then be sent home so your gums can recover.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s)

Once you’re healed, you’ll return to get your new dental crown, bridge, or denture. Our team will be sure to verify that your bite is comfortable, your restoration(s) fit properly, and that you’re satisfied with your results. We’ll also be happy to answer any questions you might have before sending you on your way to enjoy your rebuilt pearly whites!

Benefits of Dental Implants

What makes dental implants different from other treatments is that a patient’s new teeth are connected directly to the jawbone using new titanium tooth roots instead of just sitting on top of the gums. This more closely mimics the natural dental structure and offers outstanding durability and functionality when eating and speaking. The benefits of dental implants in Parma Heights far outweigh other, more traditional tooth replacement options (i.e., dentures, fixed bridges) and make it possible for individuals like yourself to enjoy a better quality of life.

Some of the most common advantages of dental implants include:

Lifestyle Benefits

  • You won’t have to worry about eliminating certain foods from your diet because your implants will be firmly in place, allowing you to enjoy even hard, crunchy foods, which can be harder for dentures or fixed bridges to cut through.
  • Your implants will blend into your smile beautifully, looking completely natural and fitting comfortably. You’ll never be able to tell the difference between your real and artificial teeth.
  • Consuming your favorite hot beverage will never be a problem with dental implants. Unlike dentures that can become warped, your implants will remain fully intact and undeterred.
  • Eating and speaking are much easier because your implants are surgically implanted into your jawbone. This means you’ll never have to worry about accidental slippage or having them fall out like dentures.
  • According to a study, Biting Into Denture Care, nearly 10 % of people with fixed bridges and dentures say they avoid social situations. With dental implants, you can expect to enjoy social gatherings and fun with family and friends without worrying about your smile.

Health Benefits

  • Unlike dentures that can cause your jaw and gums to be sore, dental implants will not irritate your soft tissues, allowing for a more enjoyable experience.
  • You’ll save money on denture adhesives, cleaning solutions, and frequent repairs and replacements because dental implants act just like regular teeth. Not only can they last a lifetime with proper care, but you don’t need to spend extra money on additional products to keep them clean. Your implant dentist in Parma Heights can explain what you need to do to ensure the health and longevity of your new smile.
  • Dentures and other traditional tooth replacements only sit on top of the gum line. As a result, they do not work to improve your oral health. Dental implants in Parma Heights, however, mimic the natural tooth structure, ensuring your jawbone remains stimulated each time you chew or bite down. This helps to prevent facial collapse and offers a more youthful, vibrant appearance.

Long-Term Benefits

  • You can enjoy a superior method of tooth replacement that is designed to last a lifetime, unlike traditional dentures and fixed bridges that require replacing after several years.
  • According to Dentistry Today, individuals who lost 5 or more teeth by the age of 65 were at a greater risk for cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and osteoporosis. However, you can reduce your risk with dental implants.
  • Because it is believed that your lifespan is connected to how many teeth you have, it is in your best interest to pursue dental implants to replace your missing teeth because of their ability to mimic the natural tooth structure.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Just about anyone who’s lost a tooth can potentially benefit from these artificial teeth. That said, you must meet certain requirements to determine whether they’re a good fit for you, including:

If our team determines that you don’t meet the criteria, we’ll work with you to address any areas of concern so that you can move forward. For example, you might need to have tooth decay filled or have a bone graft to bulk up your jawbone so it can sustain your restoration.

  • Good overall health: This method involves surgically placing titanium support rods into your jawbone, so you need to be strong enough to undergo the procedure.
  • A clean mouth: Cavities and gum disease can impact your results, so we need to ensure that you don’t have any oral issues, first.
  • Sufficient jawbone density: When you lose teeth, your body absorbs bone mass that’s no longer needed to support them which weakens your jaw. For dental implants to be successful, we need to determine that your jawbone can support them.

Missing One Tooth

Dental implants are ideal if you only have to replace one tooth because they function as a single, standalone structure. You won’t have to worry about it becoming loose or dislodged like with dentures, and your dentist won’t have to remove enamel from healthy teeth like they do for dental crowns to line up properly.

The durable, tooth-colored material can match your natural hue so no one will notice it’s there. It’ll blend in seamlessly for a perfectly replenished smile.

Missing Multiple Teeth

If you have lost several pearly whites in a row, then you might do better with an implant bridge. This prosthetic consists of the right number of pontics (artificial teeth) needed to fill in the gap in your grin and is completed by a dental crown on either side. Traditionally, these crowns fit over your natural teeth but it’s also possible to attach them to dental implants. This keeps your jaw healthier than standard dental bridges because the titanium rods stimulate your jaw whenever you bite down or chew to keep it active.

Missing All Teeth

If you’ve lost all your teeth or if you’re frustrated by wearing traditional dentures, then our team might recommend implant dentures. This method involves strategically placing 4 to 6 titanium poles into your jawbone. Then, we’ll build customized dentures that attach to them for a more permanent solution. This allows us to recreate an entire row all at one time, and as a bonus, this restoration won’t slip out of place. Plus, you won’t have to worry about removing it for regular cleanings since it remains inside your mouth.

If your jawbone doesn’t have the required density, you may do better with All-On-4 dental implants. This procedure consists of planting 4 support rods into your jawbone to securely hold a full set of dentures in place.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

The blood clot that must form around your implant sites will be one of the most important parts of the treatment. That’s why, right after you’ve left your appointment and returned home, make sure to avoid touching or disturbing the area. You should also get plenty of rest for smoother recovery, but make sure to keep your head elevated to prevent blood from rushing to your mouth.

  • Refrain from smoking or using tobacco
  • Avoid touching the implant with your fingers or tongue
  • Do not drink from a straw

Common Side Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

One of the most common symptoms after oral surgery is soreness. Fortunately, this can easily be managed with over-the-counter and prescribed pain relievers. You might also experience intermittent bleeding, and some swelling in the area, which should also be short-lived. If you notice any of these symptoms persisting or worsening over time, give us a call right away for help. In the meantime, try managing your side effects as such:

  • Apply gentle pressure to any bleeding area with a clean washcloth or gauze.
  • Take pain medications to minimize discomfort or soreness.
  • Use a cold compress on the outside of your cheek to reduce swelling.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

Even if you can’t floss or brush as thoroughly as before, keeping your mouth clean is essential for safe recovery. Otherwise, you can end up leaving your smile vulnerable to bacteria, plaque, and infection, which can become problematic and costly. Instead, try practicing these oral health tips:

  • Brush carefully and gently when cleaning around the implant site.
  • Avoid using mouthwash containing alcohol, which can be uncomfortable.
  • Rinse with salt water 2-3 times daily, especially after eating, but don’t spit the water out; let it pour from your mouth.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

After you’ve received your new crown, bridge, or denture onto your implants, you’ll be finished with the entire process. Your gums might feel some sensitivity, but it’s nothing a little pain medication can’t handle. Inform our team of any bleeding or swelling that you experience afterward so that we can address the issue. Other than that, you’ll be free to show off your brand-new pearly whites!

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Your smile needs to be well taken care of, regardless of whether it’s natural or prosthetic; otherwise, damage and infection can take away your confidence and function. If you’re going to go through the process of getting dental implants, you should do everything you can to make them last as long as possible. With basic maintenance, your dental implants can go 25 years or even longer. Needless to say, the little things you need to do to keep them in good condition are well worthwhile!

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Dental implants cannot become decayed, but your smile depends on more than just your pearly whites. Brushing and flossing clear away inflammation-causing bacteria that could infect your gums. Even if all your teeth are prosthetic, daily oral hygiene is essential if you want to hold onto your new smile. These simple tasks don’t require much time or effort, but they are powerful in keeping your dental implants long term.

Eat a Healthy Diet

A sweet treat here and there isn’t harmful, but if you consume a lot of sugar in your daily diet, you are putting your implants at risk of failure. Sugar encourages the growth and spread of inflammation-causing bacteria in the mouth and facilitates infection. Limiting your sugar intake can help keep these bacteria under control.

On the other hand, there are tons of delicious, beneficial foods that can help your implants stay strong and secure. Calcium-rich foods reinforce the jawbone, while anti-inflammatory and immunity-boosting foods like leafy greens, bell peppers, and others fight infection.

Break Bad Habits

If you have harmful habits, now is the perfect time to stop them. For instance, smoking or vaping encourages bacteria and gum infection, which means you’ll want to take necessary steps to quit. Other bad habits include chewing on ice, using your teeth to open plastic packaging, and more. Basically, if a habit can damage your teeth, it could damage your implants.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Are you active in sports? A hit to the mouth could cause dental implant failure, which means you should be wearing a mouthguard. This tiny piece of custom plastic absorbs the blow, protecting your smile; plus, it can even help reduce concussions!

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

To the untrained patient, the early signs of potential dental implant failure can be subtle and difficult to detect. By the time you realize there’s a problem, it could already be too late! Fortunately, if you continue to come to our office regularly, we can keep a close eye on your smile, identify any symptoms indicating trouble, and take care of the issue before it becomes detrimental to your implants.
