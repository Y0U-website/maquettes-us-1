Composite Fillings in Blue Island, Illinois

While silver amalgam fillings contain elemental mercury, these types of fillings are generally regarded as safe. But, if you are like most patients, you would rather avoid the risk completely.

Composite fillings are an excellent alternative to silver amalgams and are suitable for small-to-medium-sized fillings. The tooth-colored resin material is fairly durable against friction and pressure, which makes them great for anterior (front) and posterior (back) teeth.

What is in Composite Fillings? Composite fillings are made from a mixture of silica, quartz, and plasticizers. The unique formula of these fillings allows them to be tinted to match the shade of your natural teeth.

The Real Reason to Avoid Silver Amalgams

If you’re wondering if composite fillings are safer than silver amalgam fillings, the answer is yes, but probably not for the reasons you assume. Beyond the cosmetic benefits, the other reason patients choose tooth-colored composite fillings is they do not contain mercury. Many countries have banned the use of silver amalgam fillings as the ingredients (mainly mercury) are considered environmental toxins and probable human toxins. While it is true some mercury does leach from these fillings, the health effects of this kind of exposure are largely negligible.

The real concern with amalgam fillings is that the metal inside the filling expands and contracts when exposed to hot and cold foods, which can lead to tooth fractures over time.

Composite Fillings on Front Teeth

Having composite fillings on the front teeth is a popular option, as the anterior teeth are highly visible whenever you speak or laugh. If you have a cavity in the esthetic zone of your mouth, we will most likely recommend tooth-colored composite fillings.

How Much Do Composite Fillings Cost? Composite fillings and amalgam fillings are now very similar in terms of overall cost. So, if you are wondering how much composite fillings cost, you will be pleased to learn the average out-of-pocket cost is right around $175. Since insurance companies’ rates are typically based on standard treatments, they may ask you to cover the small difference between amalgam and composite fillings.

How Long Do Composite Fillings Last?

The one downside to composite fillings is that they commonly last a few years less than amalgam fillings. On average, composite fillings last around 12 years before requiring replacement. Factors that influence the longevity of composite fillings can include the size and location of the filling as well as your own oral health habits.
