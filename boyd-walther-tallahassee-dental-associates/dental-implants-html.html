What Are Dental Implants?

The Dental Implant Process

The process of replacing missing teeth with dental implants will take several months because there are various steps to ensure the long-term success of your smile. While every patient’s journey towards a complete smile will be unique, typically you can expect these steps:

  • Initial Consultation We’ll need to perform a comprehensive examination to create your personalized treatment plan. Many patients need additional treatments before their placement surgery, like tooth extractions, bone grafting, or a sinus lift.
  • Placement Surgery We partner with leading dental implant specialists for this part of the process to ensure you’re able to enjoy the best results possible.
  • Osseointegration The following three to six months after your placement surgery, your dental implant and jawbone will fuse in a process called osteointegration.
  • Restorations Finally, you’ll return to our office to have your lifelike, custom-made dental crown, bridge, or denture secured to your dental implant(s).

Who Dental Implants Can Help

Dental implants can be used to replace any number of teeth anywhere in the mouth. We can use one or more dental implants to support all manner of custom-made restorations, including dental crowns, dental bridges, partial dentures, and full dentures. The type of restoration that will be right for you will depend on how many teeth you will be replacing.

Missing One Tooth

When replacing a single tooth, a single dental implant can be placed where the missing tooth once was. A porcelain crown will be attached to the post using a connector called an abutment. It looks and feels so natural that you may even forget which tooth is artificial.

Missing Multiple Teeth

Just like their traditional counterparts, implant bridges and partial dentures can be used to restore your lost teeth. Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

As amazing as dental implants are, they don t just work on their own. These posts require regular maintenance and care to last, as they ll fail due to damage or infection otherwise. With that said, don t worry - our practice is here to assist! Listed below are some great tips on caring for dental implants in Tallahassee, designed to protect your treatment results. Just keep reading to learn about them in detail, or call our office.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Though they aren’t real teeth, dental implants depend on a healthy mouth. The posts become unstable when placed near cavities and infected gums, often resulting in failure. Gum disease can even loosen a patient’s implant posts until they fall out.

Given these points, you should make oral hygiene a big priority. Daily brushing and flossing will help you avoid tooth decay, gum disease, and similar dental problems. In doing so, the habits will keep your mouth strong and stable for your dental implants.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Dental implants don’t react well to junk food, all things considered. Sticky and sugary items such as candy and caramel tend to get wedged between the posts. From there, such leftovers can cause cavities and gum disease that make implants fail.

Luckily, sticking to a mouth-healthy diet solves this problem. Meals rich in calcium, phosphorus, and vitamin C strengthen your jaw, keep your gums healthy, and more. Even better, they don’t contain sticky sugar that could get caught near your implants.

Break Bad Habits

While they have high durability, even dental implants can fail from poor oral habits. That means you should avoid or quit these practices.

Indeed, several common oral habits put implants at risk. One example is tobacco use something that delays treatment recovery, infects gum tissues, and worse. Another is chewing or biting hard objects, as doing so can cause dental implants to break. The chips and fractures that result usually lead to treatment failure.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Dental implants can break under extreme force, despite their use of titanium and porcelain. Therefore, ensure their safety by protecting them from accidents.

The ideal protection for dental implants is a custom mouthguard. After all, using one for sports can shield your posts from collisions, tackles, and various mouth injuries. A nightguard could also keep you from grinding your teeth while asleep.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

No patient can maintain dental implants alone, even if they remember to brush and floss. That means you should get regular dental checkups for assistance.

Remember, dentists offer the best preventive oral care today. They can diagnose and treat smile issues before they threaten your implant posts. That being the case, please see them at least every six months or so.

Are Dental Implants Right for Me?

How Long Do Dental Implants Last?

With proper care and use, your dental implants can last 30+ years, but their lifespan greatly depends on your health and lifestyle.

To ensure that they last as long as possible, make sure you brush, floss, and rinse with mouthwash every day. Furthermore, avoid chewing on anything too sticky or hard, or opening packaging with your teeth. Smoking and using tobacco products have also been linked to dental implant failure.

Can I Take Dental Implants Out?

No, you cannot remove dental implants because they are fixed in the bone. The only way to take them out is by a trained dental professional. Never attempt to remove your dental implants yourself.

There are some types of implant dentures that are removable for cleaning, however only the prosthetic teeth and gums are removable, not the actual implant posts.

Does Getting Dental Implants Hurt?

Rest assured that your mouth will be numbed with local anesthetic before the dental implant procedure begins, ensuring that you feel no outright pain. You will likely be sedated, which will also lower your body’s ability to register pain.

Thankfully, your jawbone does not have many nerve endings to begin with, however while the surgery itself shouldn’t hurt, your mouth will likely be sore for a few days afterward.

Be sure to take any prescribed or OTC pain relievers as directed by your dentist. You may also use cold compresses to ease swelling and discomfort. Please call the office if your discomfort worsens instead of improving after about two or three days.

What Can Cause Dental Implants to Fail?

Though it is very rare, dental implant failure typically occurs for one of two reasons: peri-implantitis or failed osseointegration.

Peri-implantitis is a form of gum disease that develops as a result of poor oral hygiene and can damage your bone and the gums supporting your dental implant, causing it to loosen.
