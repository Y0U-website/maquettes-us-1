What Are Full-Arch Dental Implants?

Full-arch dental implants replace every tooth on your upper or lower jaw with a single fixed bridge anchored to four to six implant posts. If you have been living with loose Dentures , teeth too damaged to save, or gaps that make chewing painful, this approach gives you a permanent set of teeth that stay in your mouth around the clock.

Your dentist places titanium posts into areas of your jaw where bone density is strongest. Once those posts heal and lock into the bone, a custom prosthetic arch snaps securely into place. The result feels closer to natural teeth than anything removable. You bite, chew, and speak without worrying about slipping or adhesive.

At the first surgical appointment, your dentist places four to six implant posts into the jawbone while you’re comfortably numb. Many patients at Advanced Dental & Implant Center also choose Sedation Dentistry to stay relaxed throughout. You’ll leave that day with a temporary set of teeth attached to the posts.

Over the next three to six months, the posts fuse with your jawbone. During that time, you’ll eat softer foods and come in for periodic checkups. Once healing is complete, your dentist swaps the temporaries for a final, custom-made arch. Most people say the recovery is easier than they expected.

Before Appointment

  • Arrange for someone to drive you home, since full-arch implant surgery typically involves sedation that makes driving unsafe.
  • Write down every medication and supplement you take so your dentist can flag anything that affects bleeding or healing around implant sites.
  • Eat a solid, protein-rich meal about two hours before your appointment because you won&#x27;t be able to chew normally for several days after surgery.

After Appointment

  • Stick to soft foods like smoothies, mashed potatoes, and scrambled eggs for the first two weeks while your implants begin to integrate.
  • Rinse gently with prescribed antimicrobial mouthwash after meals, but avoid swishing forcefully around the surgical sites for the first week.
  • Hold off on brushing directly over the implant sites for the first few days. Use a soft-bristle brush on surrounding teeth only.

(719) 445-0490 Book Appointment How long do full-arch dental implants typically last? Most full-arch dental implants last 20 years or longer. The titanium posts fuse permanently with your jawbone, and the prosthetic arch can last 15 to 20 years before it may need refurbishing or replacement.

  • Can I eat steak and crunchy foods with full-arch dental implants? Yes. Once fully healed, you can bite into apples, chew steak, and eat raw vegetables without worry. The implants anchor directly into bone, so your bite force is close to what natural teeth provide.
  • How are full-arch dental implants different from Dentures? Dentures rest on your gums and can shift or slip. Full-arch dental implants are fixed to four to six posts in your jawbone, so they stay locked in place. You brush them like natural teeth and never remove them.
  • Will full-arch dental implants stop my jawbone from shrinking? They do. Implant posts stimulate the jawbone the same way tooth roots do, which prevents the bone loss that typically follows tooth loss. Most patients see their facial structure stabilize within the first year.
  • Am I too old to get full-arch dental implants? Age alone is not a disqualifier. Patients in their 70s and 80s routinely receive full-arch implants. Your dentist evaluates bone density and overall health, not your birthday, to determine if you are a good candidate.
