What Is Soft Tissue Grafting?

Soft tissue grafting can correct recession, rebuild thickness, or improve symmetry in the gumline.

Types of Bone Grafting Procedures We Offer

Apex Oral Surgery provides a comprehensive range of grafting techniques tailored to each patient’s needs.

1. Socket Preservation (Extraction Site Grafting)

When a tooth is removed, the empty socket begins to shrink almost immediately. Socket preservation places bone graft material into the extraction site to: Maintain bone height and width

  • Prevent jawbone collapse
  • Protect surrounding teeth
  • Prepare the site for a future dental implant

2. Ridge Augmentation

When significant bone has been lost over time, ridge augmentation rebuilds the shape and volume of the jawbone. Benefits include: Restoring proper bone width and height

  • Improving facial support and structure
  • Creating the necessary foundation for implants
  • Preventing further deterioration

3. Sinus Lift (Sinus Augmentation)

For upper back teeth, the sinus cavity may be too close to the jawbone for safe implant placement. A sinus lift gently raises the sinus floor and adds bone beneath it to create room for implants.A sinus lift is used when: Bone height is insufficient

  • The sinus has naturally enlarged
  • Upper molars or premolars are missing

4. Major Bone Grafting / Reconstructive Grafting

For advanced cases of bone loss due to trauma, congenital defects, or severe infections, Dr. Jimenez performs reconstructive grafting to restore larger sections of the jawbone.These procedures are highly specialized and require the expertise of an oral surgeon trained in complex reconstruction.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is bone grafting, and why might I need it?

Bone grafting is a surgical procedure that rebuilds lost or weakened bone in the jaw, most often to prepare the site for a dental implant. It may be needed after tooth loss, gum disease, trauma, or long-term denture wear, all of which can cause the jawbone to deteriorate over time.

What’s the difference between bone grafting and soft tissue grafting?

Bone grafting restores volume and density to the jawbone, while soft tissue grafting rebuilds gum tissue. Bone grafting supports implants and overall jaw structure; soft tissue grafting addresses gum recession, improves aesthetics, and protects tooth roots.

Where does the bone graft material come from?

At Apex Oral Surgery, Dr. Christian Jimenez uses biocompatible materials that may come from your own body, a tissue bank, animal sources, or synthetic options. He’ll review the safest, most effective choice for your specific case during your consultation.

Is bone grafting painful?

Most patients report minimal discomfort. The procedure is performed under local anesthesia or IV sedation, and any post-surgical soreness is typically well-controlled with prescribed or over-the-counter medication.

How long does it take to heal from a bone graft?

Initial healing usually takes one to two weeks, but the graft itself needs four to six months to fully integrate with your existing bone before a dental implant can be placed. Healing times vary based on the type and size of the graft.

What is a sinus lift, and why is it needed?

A sinus lift is a specialized bone graft performed in the upper back jaw, where the sinus cavity can limit available bone for implants. The procedure lifts the sinus membrane and adds bone, creating enough density to support implants safely.

What is PRF (Platelet-Rich Fibrin), and how does it help healing?

PRF is a regenerative therapy made from a small sample of your own blood. It releases natural growth factors that accelerate healing, reduce swelling, and improve graft success — all without using foreign materials.
