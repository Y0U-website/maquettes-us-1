Dental Hygiene — Frequently Asked Questions

Click Each Box To Learn More

▶︎ 1. What is dental hygiene?

Dental hygiene is the ongoing practice of keeping the teeth, gums, and mouth clean and healthy. It combines professional dental care with daily habits such as brushing, flossing, using fluoride toothpaste, staying hydrated, and maintaining a balanced diet. At Arizona Dental, dental hygiene services may include cleanings, plaque and tartar removal, gum evaluations, fluoride treatments, oral cancer screenings, diagnostic imaging, and personalized preventive education.

▶︎ 2. Why are professional dental cleanings necessary?

Brushing and flossing remove much of the plaque that forms on the teeth, but they cannot always reach every area of the mouth. When plaque is not removed, it can harden into tartar, which cannot be removed safely with a toothbrush. Professional cleanings use specialized instruments to remove tartar, reduce bacteria, polish the teeth, and identify early signs of decay or gum disease before they become more serious.

▶︎ 3. How often should I have my teeth cleaned?

Most patients benefit from professional dental cleanings approximately every six months. However, some people may need more frequent visits based on their oral health, medical history, gum condition, smoking history, dry mouth, diabetes, orthodontic appliances, or tendency to develop heavy tartar buildup. Your dental team will recommend a schedule based on your individual needs.

▶︎ 4. What happens during a dental hygiene appointment?

During a dental hygiene appointment, the hygienist evaluates your teeth and gums, checks for inflammation or bleeding, and removes plaque and tartar buildup. The teeth are then polished to remove surface stains and smooth the enamel. Depending on your needs, the visit may also include X-rays, gum measurements, fluoride treatment, an oral cancer screening, and recommendations for improving your at-home hygiene routine.

▶︎ 5. Is flossing really necessary?

Yes. Brushing cleans the front, back, and chewing surfaces of the teeth, but it does not fully clean the narrow spaces between them. Plaque and food debris often collect in these areas, increasing the risk of cavities and gum inflammation. Floss, interdental brushes, floss picks, or water flossers can help clean between the teeth and below the gumline where a toothbrush cannot reach.

▶︎ 6. Why do my gums bleed when I brush or floss?

Bleeding gums are often caused by inflammation from plaque buildup along the gumline. This may be an early sign of gingivitis, which can often be reversed with professional cleaning and improved daily care. Bleeding may also occur when brushing too aggressively or using improper flossing technique. Persistent bleeding should be evaluated by a dental professional because it may indicate gum disease or another oral health concern.

▶︎ 7. Can dental hygiene help prevent gum disease?

Consistent dental hygiene is one of the most effective ways to prevent gum disease. Removing plaque and tartar reduces the bacteria that irritate the gums and damage the structures supporting the teeth. Early-stage gum disease, known as gingivitis, can often be reversed. More advanced periodontal disease requires professional treatment and ongoing maintenance to control infection and protect the gums and supporting bone.

▶︎ 8. Why do I still get cavities if I brush every day?

Cavity risk depends on more than brushing alone. Diet, frequent snacking, acidic drinks, genetics, dry mouth, medications, enamel strength, gum recession, flossing habits, and brushing technique can all affect the likelihood of decay. A dental examination can help identify the factors contributing to cavities and determine whether fluoride treatment, dietary changes, prescription toothpaste, or more frequent preventive care may help.

▶︎ 9. What is the difference between a regular cleaning and a deep cleaning?

A regular cleaning is preventive care for patients whose gums and supporting bone are generally healthy. It removes plaque, tartar, and surface stains from above and around the gumline. A deep cleaning, also called scaling and root planing, is used to treat periodontal disease. It removes bacteria and hardened deposits from deeper beneath the gums and smooths the tooth roots to help the gum tissue heal.

▶︎ 10. How can I improve my dental hygiene at home?

Consistent daily habits combined with professional preventive care can help protect your natural teeth, reduce dental expenses, and maintain a healthier smile for years to come.

  • Brushing twice daily for approximately two minutes
  • Using a soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste
  • Flossing or cleaning between the teeth every day
  • Cleaning your tongue to reduce bacteria
  • Limiting frequent sugary or acidic foods and drinks
  • Drinking water throughout the day
  • Avoiding tobacco products
  • Attending regular dental examinations and cleanings
