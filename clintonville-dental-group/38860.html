Do dental implants hurt?

Most patients report minimal discomfort during and after the procedure. Here’s what you can expect:

During the Procedure

We typically perform the implant process under local anesthesia so the area will be completely numb. You won’t feel pain during the placement of the implant, though you may experience some slight pressure or vibrations. For those feeling particularly anxious, we provide additional sedation options that can help you relax.

After the Procedure

It’s normal to experience mild soreness or swelling for a few days following the procedure. This is similar to the discomfort you might feel after a tooth extraction. Over-the-counter pain relievers, such as ibuprofen, are usually enough to manage any discomfort. Many patients are pleasantly surprised at how manageable the recovery is.

Long-Term Comfort

Once the implant site heals and we place the restoration, dental implants feel and function like your natural teeth. There’s no ongoing pain or discomfort, and they integrate seamlessly into your daily life.

Do I need special tools to clean around my implants?

No, you can clean your dental implants the same way you clean your natural teeth . You can use a soft-bristled toothbrush, regular floss, and mouthwash, though there are other options for a deeper clean.

How to Clean Your Dental Implants

You should always follow your dentist s instructions regarding cleaning your dental implants. However, in most cases, you should brush twice a day with a soft-bristle toothbrush. Apply gentle pressure! Don’t scrub your teeth as hard as you can. Angle the bristles toward your gumline and clean all of the surfaces of your implant crown. When you floss, you can use implant-specific floss that slides around the abutment without shredding.

Water flossers are another helpful option for people with implants. They are great for flushing out food particles and bacteria from hard-to-reach areas near the implant base. Interdental brushes also work well for cleaning areas between your implant and neighboring teeth. These small brushes come in a variety of sizes, so try a few out until you find one that works for you.

Consistency is key when cleaning your implant. Daily cleaning helps prevent plaque buildup and keeps your gums healthy around the implant site.
