How we know it worked

About three months after your last session — at your first recall visit — we measure your gum pockets again and compare them to where you started. Healing gums show it plainly: shallower pockets, far less bleeding, firmer tissue that sits tight against the teeth. Those numbers — not how it feels day to day — are how progress is judged, and we go over them with you honestly.

That visit also begins periodontal maintenance — the every-three-to-four-month rhythm that protects the ground that&#x27;s been regained. For the deepest pockets, or gums that don&#x27;t respond as hoped, we&#x27;ll say so plainly and involve a trusted local specialist.

Deep cleaning questions, answered

What is scaling and root planing?

Scaling and root planing — often called a deep cleaning — is the standard first treatment for gum disease that has moved below the gumline. Scaling removes plaque and hardened tartar from below the gums; root planing smooths the tooth roots so gum tissue can reattach and bacteria have a harder time coming back.

Is a deep cleaning really necessary?

When the measurements call for it, yes. Gum pockets are measured in millimeters at your exam; healthy gums generally measure 1 to 3. When pockets reach 4 millimeters or more, bleed easily, or show tartar and bone changes on X-rays, the infection sits where a regular cleaning cannot reach — and left alone, it tends to progress quietly. The recommendation should always come with your numbers. Ask to see yours; we will walk through them together.

Does scaling and root planing hurt?

We numb the area being treated before starting. Afterward, gums can be tender for a few days and teeth may be temporarily sensitive to temperature — most patients manage easily with over-the-counter pain relief and gentle care.

How long does scaling and root planing take?

We usually treat one side of the mouth at each visit — two visits in all for most patients — with each visit scheduled for about 90 minutes. Treating half the mouth at a time keeps each appointment manageable and lets us be thorough.

What should I do after a deep cleaning?

Wait until the numbness wears off before chewing, and plan a soft meal for the rest of the day. For the first few days, gums may be tender and may bleed a little when you brush — keep brushing gently anyway, because clean teeth heal best. Warm salt-water rinses can soothe the area. Cold sensitivity is common at first and usually fades over the following weeks. We review all of this with you before you leave.

Do I go back to regular cleanings after a deep cleaning?

Usually not right away. After scaling and root planing, most patients move to periodontal maintenance — a deeper kind of cleaning every three to four months, timed to how quickly the bacteria that cause gum disease return. Dr. Patel re-checks your gum measurements at each visit and adjusts the plan as your gums respond.

Can a general dentist do scaling and root planing?

Yes. Scaling and root planing is well within the scope of general dentistry, and it&#x27;s a particular focus of our office — Dr. Patel is a general dentist with a dedicated periodontal program and 18 years of experience treating gum disease. For advanced cases that need surgical care, we work with trusted local specialists.
