Extractions

Tooth extractions at American Urgent Dental may be recommended when a tooth is causing pain, infection, or damage that cannot be addressed with other dental care. In urgent situations, an extraction may be advised when preserving the tooth is no longer in the patient’s best interest, as it compromises their oral health.

Our team focuses on careful evaluation, patient comfort, and clear communication, so you understand why an extraction is recommended and what to expect throughout the process.

What Is a Tooth Extraction?

A tooth extraction involves removing a tooth from its position in the jaw. This may be necessary when a tooth is severely damaged, infected, or contributing to ongoing discomfort. Extractions can also be recommended when a tooth interferes with surrounding teeth or overall oral health.

Before proceeding, your dentist will assess the tooth and surrounding structures to determine whether extraction is the most appropriate option.

When Is an Extraction Recommended?

An extraction may be recommended if a tooth is causing significant pain, has extensive decay, is fractured beyond repair, or is infected. Dental injuries and certain urgent conditions may also require extraction to prevent further complications.

Each situation is evaluated individually, and your dentist will explain the reasoning behind the recommendation, tailored to your specific needs.

What Happens During a Tooth Extraction?

During an extraction, the area around the tooth is numbed to support comfort. The dentist carefully removes the tooth while protecting the surrounding tissue. The procedure is completed with attention to patient comfort and efficiency, especially during urgent visits.

After the extraction, you will receive clear instructions on caring for the area and supporting proper healing.

What Can Patients Expect After an Extraction?

Following an extraction, it is normal to experience mild soreness as the area begins to heal. Your dentist will provide guidance on managing discomfort, protecting the extraction site, and recognizing signs that may require follow-up care. Healing timelines vary, and your team will explain what to expect based on your situation.
