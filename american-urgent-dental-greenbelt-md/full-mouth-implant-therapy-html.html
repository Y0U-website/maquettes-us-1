Full Mouth Implant Therapy

Full mouth implant therapy at American Urgent Dental is a comprehensive approach to replacing most or all teeth using dental implants. This type of care is designed for patients whose oral health, comfort, or function has been affected by extensive tooth loss, damage, or ongoing dental concerns.

Rather than relying on a single technique, full-mouth implant therapy focuses on careful evaluation and personalized planning to determine the most suitable implant-supported solution for each patient.

What Is Full Mouth Implant Therapy?

Full mouth implant therapy refers to a customized treatment plan that replaces multiple missing or failing teeth with implant-supported restorations. Depending on individual needs, this may involve several implants placed throughout the jaw to support fixed bridges, dentures, or full-arch restorations.

This approach enables dentists to tailor care to individual oral anatomy, bone structure, and functional goals, rather than using a one-size-fits-all method.

How Is Full Mouth Implant Therapy Different from All-On-X?

All-On-X is a specific technique that can be used within a comprehensive full-mouth implant therapy plan. It relies on a set number of implants to support a full arch of teeth. Full-mouth implant therapy, however, is broader and may involve different implant numbers, placement strategies, and restoration types based on the patient&#x27;s needs.

In other words, All-On-X is one possible option, while full mouth implant therapy represents the overall planning process.

Who May Be a Candidate for Full Mouth Implant Therapy?

This type of therapy may be considered for patients missing most or all of their teeth, those with advanced dental damage, or individuals who struggle with traditional dentures. A detailed evaluation, including advanced imaging, is used to assess candidacy and guide treatment planning.

What Can Patients Expect During the Process?

The process begins with diagnostic evaluation and treatment planning. Implants are placed strategically and allowed time to integrate with the jawbone before restorations are secured. Each phase is explained clearly, so patients understand timing, expectations, and next steps.
