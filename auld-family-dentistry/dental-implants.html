The Dental Implant Process: What to Expect

A thorough evaluation begins with X-rays and impressions to assess bone density and gum health. The dentist discusses goals, answers questions, and creates a customized treatment plan.

During the procedure, a small titanium post is surgically placed into the jawbone. This serves as the foundation for the new tooth. Patients receive local anesthesia for comfort, and most experience minimal discomfort afterward.

The healing period allows the implant to fuse with the bone, creating a stable base. This phase, known as osseointegration, typically takes a few months, but temporary solutions are available to maintain appearance and function.

Once the implant is secure, an abutment is attached to connect the post to the final crown. The custom crown is then placed, carefully shaped, and shaded to match surrounding teeth for a natural look.

Each step is planned with precision to ensure the best outcome. The result is a strong, secure replacement tooth that looks and functions just like the real thing.

Are Dental Implants Right for You?

Dental implants are an excellent option for many patients, but a few key factors determine if they are the right choice. During a consultation, the dentist evaluates oral health, bone structure, and overall suitability for the procedure.

Implants can replace a single tooth, or multiple teeth, or even support a full arch of dentures. They provide a long-lasting alternative to traditional bridges and removable dentures.

Since implants integrate with the jawbone, having enough bone is essential for long-term stability. If bone loss has occurred, grafting may be an option to strengthen the area before placement.

Gum health plays a critical role in implant success. Patients with untreated gum disease may need additional care before moving forward with the procedure.

Unlike dentures that require adjustments or replacements over time, implants are designed to last for decades. They offer a permanent option for those seeking a secure, natural-looking restoration.
