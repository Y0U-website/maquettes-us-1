Dental implants that last decades.

  • Preserves jawbone. The bone resorbs when there’s no tooth root stimulating it. An implant keeps that signal going.
  • Lasts. A well-placed implant with good hygiene routinely lasts decades. Crowns and bridges over time will need replacement; the implant itself usually doesn’t.
  • Eats like a tooth. Patients describe forgetting which one is the implant after a few months.

The treatment, end to end

Cone-beam 3D imaging before anything else, so we see bone density, nerve position, and sinus anatomy in detail. Implant placement is a single surgical visit, typically under local anesthesia and oral sedation. The site heals for three to six months while the bone integrates with the post. A custom crown is then designed and placed.

Single tooth, multiple teeth, or full arch

One implant for one tooth. Two implants supporting a small bridge for two or three missing teeth in a row. Four to six implants supporting a full-arch fixed bridge (what’s often called "All-on-4" or similar) when most or all of the teeth on an arch are gone or need to be removed. For full-arch cases we work with a surgeon who places the implants, then restore them here once the sites have healed, a division of labor that gives full-arch patients a team that does this work in volume.

Implant-supported dentures

A middle option that’s been transformative for denture-wearing patients. Two to four implants per arch give the denture something to snap onto. The denture stops slipping. No more adhesives. Chewing capacity comes back to something close to natural.

Bone grafting, when needed

Bone grafting places material in the jaw so the bone can heal in a shape and volume that will support an implant. When we remove a tooth, we’ll often graft the socket at the same visit to preserve the bone for a future implant, routine socket-preservation grafting we do in-house. Cases that call for extensive grafting or a sinus lift we refer to an oral surgeon.
