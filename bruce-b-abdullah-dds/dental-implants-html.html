What is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a small, screwlike post that is surgically inserted into the jaw. It’s made out of biocompatible material that can fuse with the bone, giving it equal stability and security to natural tooth roots. A customized restoration can be attached to an implant via a connector piece called an abutment.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

Each patient will have a different dental implant experience, as no two plans are the same. However, at Grand Dental Lake Zurich, we follow the same four-step process that is required of all implant procedures: initial consultation, oral surgery, abutment placement, and delivery of the final restoration. This ensures that each phase of treatment is fully completed, increasing the chances of successful placement and long-lasting results. To learn how dental implants work, keep reading.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

When a person inquires about dental implants, a scheduled consultation must first occur. This allows for a thorough examination of their oral and overall health as well as their jawbone. Digital imaging and diagnostics are required to provide an in-depth view of the entire oral and facial structures areas that are necessary to understand if dental implants are to be properly positioned during surgery. If problems are found during this stage, such as gum disease, dental decay, or jawbone shrinkage, we will recommend additional treatment to create a healthier environment for future implants.

Dental Implant Surgery

At Grand Dental Lake Zurich, we place our implants in-house so that patients can remain in one convenient location throughout their treatment. We will begin by administering local anesthesia before making one or more incisions in the gums. This allows us to carefully place each implant post into its specific location, positioning it for maximum support. When finished, we will close the gum tissue and place protective healing caps over each post.

Dental Implant Osseointegration/Abutment

The third step involves a person spending much of their time recovering. While individuals can return to work a few days or even a week after surgery, the process of healing continues and can take 4-6 months. This is necessary to ensure that osseointegration occurs, which is the fusing of the dental implants with jawbone tissues. When fully healed, the patient will return to our office to receive metal abutments, which are placed on top of implants to ensure adequate attachment to the restoration.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

During the consultation, our team will review the severity of tooth loss and determine which type of restoration is needed. Because of dental implants’ versatility, they can support a customized dental crown, bridge, or denture with ease. This means that the final step will involve placing the completed restoration on top of the implant(s), creating a full, complete smile that the patient can begin to enjoy right away.

Dental implants are known for helping patients suffering from tooth loss. Who is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

When meeting with an implant dentist, patients who are interested in permanent prosthetics can expect to spend ample time learning more about their oral and overall health as well as their jawbone. Not only will we evaluate the current state of one’s teeth and gums to determine if tooth decay or periodontal disease exists, but we’ll also identify if any overall health issues put a person at risk for implant failure (i.e., diabetes, heart disease, etc.). It’s also essential that we look at the density of a person’s jawbone to determine if it can support customized restorations.

Missing One Tooth

When only a single tooth is missing, our team will use one dental implant post, along with a metal abutment that serves to connect the restoration with the existing implant. After a customized dental crown is placed on top to complete the root-to-crown structure, a patient can feel more confident in their appearance.

Missing Multiple Teeth

A traditional fixed bridge requires altering healthy abutment teeth; however, an implant bridge only focuses on the vacant sockets. There is no need to touch other areas of a person’s smile when two dental implant posts can be put into place, within the jawbone, and a customized bridge can be secured on top. Not only does this eliminate the need to use nearby teeth as anchors, but it also ensures a more permanent tooth replacement.

Missing All Teeth

Requiring between four and six dental implant posts, an implant denture is a preferred method of treatment for many tooth loss sufferers. Not only does this type of prosthetic work just like regular teeth, but it also looks natural and feels comfortable, allowing for a permanent, beautiful, and longer-lasting smile.

Am I Too Young to Get Dental Implants?

Generally speaking, it is inadvisable to place dental implants in patients who are in their early 20s or younger. The reason is that most of these individuals’ jawbones are not yet completely developed. Placing dental implants too soon can lead to serious complications. It is recommended that young people who have lost their teeth seek alternative treatments until it is safe to commit to dental implant placement. Once a person reaches their mid to late 20s, it is usually safe and recommended to place implants.

Dental Implant Post-Op Instructions

Once a patient’s dental implants are in place, the next step is essential to the long-term success of these permanent prosthetics, but it is also something individuals should not worry about. There are often many questions about what is to be expected during the healing process, which is why our team at Grand Dental Lake Zurich is here to provide additional insight. Below, we explain what patients can expect, which symptoms are normal, the types of food that can be enjoyed, and what individuals can do to encourage proper healing.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

Following the placement of one’s dental implants, it is necessary for blood clots to form so that proper healing can occur. Should one or more of these clots become dislodged, it can cause a painful dry socket. Patients should avoid the following:

  • Spitting
  • Using a straw
  • Smoking or using tobacco of any kind
  • Touching the surgical site with one’s fingers or tongue

Common Side Effects

Mild side effects are normal after implant placement, especially in the days following surgery. Some of the most common issues a patient might experience include:

  • General discomfort, as the mouth will need time to heal, specifically the gums and bones. Taking over-the-counter medication can help alleviate any pain.
  • Slight swelling, which can last a little more than a week. Using a cold compress can help reduce inflammation and discomfort.
  • Occasional bleeding, which can last for several days but be reduced by using clean gauze and gentle pressure.

Diet

In the days following dental implant placement, patients will need to avoid eating hard, crunchy, or sticky foods, as these can irritate the surgical wounds. Instead, our team suggests a soft food diet that doesn’t require much chewing or biting down. Typical foods that are recommended include:

Once an individual’s mouth begins to feel less uncomfortable, they can begin to slowly incorporate denser foods.

  • Pudding
  • Oatmeal
  • Warm soup
  • Pasta
  • Scrambled eggs
  • Yogurt
  • Mashed potatoes
  • Ice cream

Health & Oral Hygiene

Maintaining good oral hygiene after dental implant surgery is necessary to avoid the accumulation of bacterial build-up. The process of cleaning one’s teeth and gums requires a gentle touch and close attention.

At Grand Dental Lake Zurich, we suggest patients begin to brush their teeth as usual the day after surgery. When cleaning near implants, though, they must be careful to avoid irritating the area. Flossing should continue as well. When rinsing, it is best to use salt water two or three times a day, as this clears out unhealthy bacteria and promotes healing.

Patients should avoid any kind of mouthwash that contains alcohol, such as Scope or Listerine.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

The final stage of the dental implant process is to receive a customized restoration. Patients will have their crown, bridge, or denture secured in place. At this time, some slight gum sensitivity might occur, but it will dissipate quickly and can be managed with an over-the-counter pain reliever. Individuals should not expect any bleeding or swelling at this point.
