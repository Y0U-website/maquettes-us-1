Emergency Pediatric Dental Care

If there is significant head or jaw trauma causing loss of consciousness, dizziness, or vomiting, contact emergency medical services immediately before calling our office.

  • Knocked-out (avulsed) permanent teeth
  • Chipped or broken teeth that cause sharp edges or bleeding
  • Loose teeth due to trauma
  • Injury to the jaw or mouth causing difficulty opening or closing, bleeding, or bruising
  • Dental abscesses or signs of infection (fever, swelling, redness)

Quick Tips for Dental Emergencies

When a dental emergency happens, quick action can make a big difference in protecting your child’s smile. Stay calm, assess the situation, and follow these simple steps while contacting our office for further guidance.

  • Avulsed (knocked-out) baby teeth: Do not replant. This can damage developing permanent teeth.
  • Avulsed permanent teeth: Rinse gently, place the tooth in milk or saliva (avoid water), and call us immediately.
  • Chipped or broken teeth: Save any broken pieces and rinse your child’s mouth with warm water. Apply a cold compress for swelling.
