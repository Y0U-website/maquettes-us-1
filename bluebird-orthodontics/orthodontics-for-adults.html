FAQs About Adult Orthodontics

Yes. Healthy teeth can be moved at any age. Orthodontic treatment works just as well for adults as it does for children.

Many options are available now to minimize the appearance of braces. Today’s braces are smaller and sleeker, and some are next-to-invisible.

Dr. Frank can help you find a treatment that will fit your needs.

You can do almost anything during orthodontic treatment – sing, play a musical instrument, dine out, kiss, and even have your picture taken. Some adjustments may be necessary. But, if you’re like the vast majority of adult patients, you’ll adjust quickly.

New techniques and materials have made wearing braces more comfortable and convenient than ever.

During your consultation, Dr. Frank can help you figure out if you need treatment. After carefully considering your needs, Dr. Frank will recommend the best course of treatment for improving your smile, including the different types of braces that are best for you.

Orthodontic treatment can create or hold space to replace missing teeth. Dr. Frank will work with your dentist or dental specialist. They will provide bridges or implants so you get the best results.

Dr. Frank will consult with your dentist and/or periodontist about the health of your gums. If you are a candidate for orthodontic treatment, you will continue regular supervision with your dentist and periodontist. In many cases, tooth alignment can improve gum health.

The cost of your treatment will depend on many factors, including the severity of the problem, its complexity, and length of treatment. Dr. Frank will be glad to discuss the cost with you before treatment begins.

You will find that braces are more affordable today than ever. At Bluebird Orthodontics, we offer a variety of convenient payment plans and accept many dental insurance plans that include orthodontic benefits.

Orthodontists are dental specialists who diagnose, prevent and treat dental and facial irregularities. Dr. Frank received an additional three full years of specialized education beyond dental school to learn the proper way to align and straighten teeth.

Only those with this formal education may call themselves “orthodontists,” and only orthodontists may be members of the American Association of Orthodontists (AAO).
