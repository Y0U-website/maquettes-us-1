Types of Dental Implants We Offer

Single Tooth Implants

If you are missing one tooth , a single dental implant is the best option. The implant replaces the missing tooth without affecting adjacent teeth , unlike traditional bridges.

Multiple Tooth Implants

For patients missing several teeth , implant-supported bridges or individual implants provide a stable, long-term solution without the need for removable dentures.

Full Arch Implants (All-On-X)

For those missing all teeth on the upper or lower jaw , All-On-X implants provide a full, permanent set of teeth using just four or more implants .

The Dental Implant Process

At Chips Dental Associates , we use advanced digital imaging and guided implant placement to ensure the best results. Here’s what to expect:

1. Consultation and 3D Imaging

We begin with a comprehensive evaluation using 3D CT scanning to assess bone density and plan the precise placement of your implants.

2. Implant Placement

The titanium implant post is surgically inserted into the jawbone under local anesthesia or IV sedation for comfort.

3. Healing and Osseointegration

Over the next three to six months , the implant fuses with the jawbone in a process called osseointegration , creating a strong foundation for your new tooth.

4. Abutment and Crown Placement

Once healing is complete, a custom porcelain crown is attached, restoring the look and function of your natural tooth.

If additional bone support is needed before implant placement, we also offer bone grafting to strengthen the jaw.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants

1. How long do dental implants last?

With proper care, dental implants can last a lifetime . The implant itself is permanent, while the crown may need replacement after 10-15 years.

4. How long does the implant process take?

From implant placement to the final crown, the full process takes three to six months , allowing time for the implant to integrate with the jawbone.

5. Can smokers get dental implants?

Yes, but smoking increases the risk of implant failure . We recommend quitting or reducing smoking for better healing and long-term success.
