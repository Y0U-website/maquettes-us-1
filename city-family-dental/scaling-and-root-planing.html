What is scaling and root planing (SRP)?

Think of SRP as a deep cleaning for your teeth and gums—more intensive than the regular professional cleaning you receive every six months. While a typical cleaning involves our hygienists removing bacteria, plaque, tartar, and surface stains from your teeth and gumline, SRP goes further. Scaling removes harmful, disease-causing buildup beneath the gumline, and root planing smooths the roots’ surfaces to promote healing and prevent further bacterial growth.

Why is planing necessary?

Plaque and bacteria tend to stick to rough surfaces. By planing or smoothing these rough surfaces, we promote a healthier mouth and help encourage the reattachment of the gums to the teeth.

Is SRP for all types of gum disease?

Care recommendations depend on the stage or severity of the disease. For some individuals with more mild gum disease, all that may be necessary to restore gingival health is modifications to home care, antibacterial rinses, and the standard prophy cleaning. For others with more advanced gum disease, it may be suggested to halt the disease’s progression and promote gum healing.

It is important to know that SRP is a nonsurgical alternative to more extensive, traumatic, and aggressive periodontal therapies. Alternatively, surgical treatments are generally for severe gum disease and can help regenerate tissues that have been lost due to this destructive condition.

Scaling and Root Planing FAQs

How can I tell if scaling and root planing may be needed?

Certain changes in gum health may indicate the need for deeper care. Ongoing gum inflammation, bleeding during brushing, tenderness, or persistent bad breath can signal that bacteria have moved below the gumline. Because early stages are not always painful, routine dental visits are important for identifying concerns early. A dental professional evaluates gum measurements and tissue health to determine whether this treatment is appropriate.

What happens during a scaling and root planing procedure?

The procedure involves thoroughly cleaning areas above and below the gumline to remove plaque and hardened deposits. Root surfaces are smoothed to discourage bacteria from reattaching and to support healing. Local anesthesia is commonly used to keep patients comfortable. Specialized instruments allow the dental team to work precisely while protecting surrounding tissues.

How should I prepare for my scaling and root planing appointment?

Preparation is usually minimal. Sharing your medical history, medications, and any concerns with your dentist helps ensure safe care. Maintaining good oral hygiene before the appointment supports better outcomes. If sedation or extended treatment time is planned, your dental team may provide additional instructions ahead of your visit.

What is recovery like after scaling and root planing?

Recovery typically involves mild tenderness or sensitivity for a short period. Some patients notice gum soreness or temperature sensitivity while tissues heal. Eating softer foods and using recommended oral care products can help manage discomfort. Following post-treatment instructions closely supports healing and reduces irritation.

Are there risks or complications associated with this treatment?

Side effects are generally mild and temporary. Increased sensitivity or slight gum irritation may occur as tissues adjust. Complications such as infection are uncommon and can usually be prevented by following aftercare instructions. The benefits of controlling gum inflammation and reducing bacteria generally outweigh these temporary effects.

How often is scaling and root planing needed?

The frequency depends on individual gum health and treatment response. Some patients require it only once, while others benefit from periodic maintenance if gum disease is ongoing. Regular dental evaluations help determine whether additional care is needed. Consistent home care can reduce the likelihood of repeat treatment.

How does scaling and root planing support long-term oral health?

By removing bacteria beneath the gumline, this treatment helps slow or stop the progression of gum disease. Healthier gums provide better support for teeth and reduce the risk of future complications. Maintaining clean root surfaces also improves the effectiveness of daily brushing and flossing.

Will scaling and root planing improve bad breath?

Yes, persistent bad breath is often linked to bacteria trapped below the gums. Removing these deposits can significantly improve breath freshness. Continued oral hygiene and follow-up care help maintain these improvements over time.

Can this treatment help prevent tooth loss?

Controlling gum disease early helps protect the bone and tissues that support teeth. By reducing inflammation and bacterial buildup, scaling and root planing lower the risk of advanced damage that can lead to tooth instability. Early intervention plays a key role in preserving natural teeth.

What happens after treatment is completed?

Follow-up visits allow the dental team to evaluate healing and determine next steps. Some patients transition into a maintenance schedule focused on preventing recurrence. Ongoing monitoring, combined with good daily habits, supports long-term gum health and stability.
