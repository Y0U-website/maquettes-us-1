Dental Implants

A missing tooth changes how you eat, how you speak, and sometimes how you hold your jaw. Dental implants address the root of the problem, literally, by replacing the tooth and the structure underneath it.

What You Should Know About Dental Implants Before Your Consultation

Dental implants are the closest thing dentistry has to replacing a tooth the way it was built: a small titanium post set into the jawbone, a connecting piece called an abutment, and a crown on top that matches your other teeth. Most people asking about dental implants want to know how long they take, what they cost, and whether their bone can support one. Those are the right questions, and we can answer all three once we see your x-rays.

Anytime Dental offers implant placement in-house at all three of our Florida locations: Gainesville, Merritt Island, and Sun City Center. You don’t get a referral to a specialist across town. The same office that does your exam, places the implant, and delivers the final crown. Your records stay in one place, and the team who reviews your scans is the same team who sees you at every visit.

This matters more than it sounds. Implant treatment takes several months from start to finish, and a lot can go wrong when your care is split between offices that don’t share records in real time. Keeping everything under one roof cuts down on repeated x-rays, phone tag between offices, and appointments where someone has to catch up before doing anything useful.

How an Implant Is Placed and Why the Timeline Is What It Is

The placement appointment itself is a surgical procedure, but a contained one. The implant post is placed directly into the jawbone during a single visit, under local anesthesia. Most patients are surprised by how manageable the recovery is. Some swelling and tenderness for a few days, then things settle down. We use sedation options for patients who want them, and we’ll talk through what’s right for you before anything is scheduled.

After placement, there’s a healing period, typically three to six months, where the bone fuses around the post in a process called osseointegration. This is the part patients find frustrating because there’s nothing to do but wait. It’s also the part that determines whether the implant holds for decades. Skipping or shortening this phase to move faster is how implants fail. We won’t do that.

Once the implant has integrated, the abutment and crown go on. At that point, you have a functional tooth. It doesn’t need special cleaning products. It doesn’t come out at night. You brush and floss around it the same way you would a natural tooth, and you come back for your regular dental exams to keep an eye on the surrounding tissue.

If the tooth was lost recently, or if there’s been significant bone loss in the area, a bone graft may be needed before the implant can be placed. Bone Grafting is something we handle in-house as well, which means you’re not managing a separate surgical referral on top of your implant treatment.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants

Most adults who are missing one or more teeth are worth evaluating for implants. The key factors are bone density, gum health, and overall medical history. Patients with uncontrolled diabetes, active periodontal disease, or significant bone loss may need additional treatment before implant placement is feasible. That’s not a no, it’s a sequence. We figure out what needs to happen first and build the plan from there.

Gum disease is probably the most common reason someone needs to address something before an implant can be placed. If there’s active infection in the surrounding tissue, it won’t let an implant heal properly. Our periodontics team handles scaling and root planing and other gum treatments in-house, so if that’s the step that needs to come first, you’re not starting over at a new office.

For patients replacing a single tooth, Single Tooth Replacement is a straightforward application of the implant process. For patients who are missing most or all of their teeth and are currently using dentures, implant-supported options change the picture significantly. Implant-supported dentures don’t slip, don’t require adhesive, and don’t accelerate the bone loss that conventional dentures often cause over time. Ask about this specifically if you’ve been wearing dentures for several years.

Age is less of a barrier than most people expect. We see patients in their 70s and 80s at our Sun City Center office who are excellent candidates. Our team works with patients across the Del Webb community and the surrounding Hillsborough County area, and straightforward conversations about what’s realistic given a patient’s bone health and medical history are a normal part of those consultations.

Insurance Coverage and Financing for Dental Implants

Dental insurance coverage for implants varies widely. Some PPO plans cover a portion of the crown, the surgical placement, or both. Many cover neither, or apply a waiting period before benefits kick in. We work with most PPO dental plans at our Gainesville and Merritt Island locations. At Sun City Center, our team helps patients file their own claims and will walk you through exactly what your plan covers before treatment begins.

Because implant treatment has multiple stages, the costs also come at different points in your care. The written estimate you receive at your consultation will break down each stage separately so you can see what hits when and plan accordingly. We discuss financing options for patients who want to spread the cost over time. Ask the front desk about current options when you call.

One thing worth noting: implants typically cost more up front than alternatives like a bridge or a partial denture. Over ten or twenty years, the math often looks different. Bridges involve preparing the adjacent teeth, and those teeth may eventually need crowns or root canal therapy on their own. Implants don’t touch the neighboring teeth at all. That’s a real difference, and it’s worth factoring in when you’re comparing estimates.

Why Replacing a Missing Tooth Sooner Changes What’s Possible Later

Bone loss starts within months of a tooth being lost. The jawbone in that area no longer has a root stimulating it, and it begins to resorb. The longer that goes on, the less bone is available for an implant, and the more likely a bone graft becomes a required first step rather than an optional one. Acting earlier, when bone volume is still good, keeps the process simpler and the cost lower.

A missing tooth also shifts the load on surrounding teeth. Adjacent teeth drift toward the gap. The opposing tooth has nothing to meet, which changes how it wears. These are gradual changes, and they’re easy to ignore until they become restorative problems on teeth that were fine before. An implant stops that drift because it fills the space and maintains the bone around it.
