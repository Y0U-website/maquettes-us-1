How Orthodontic Treatment Actually Moves Teeth

Teeth move because of sustained, low-level pressure applied over time. That pressure causes the bone around the tooth root to remodel: it breaks down on the side the tooth is moving toward and rebuilds on the side it’s moving away from. The tooth shifts gradually into the new position. Both braces and clear aligners work on this same principle. The difference is how the force is applied and how often it’s adjusted.

Braces use brackets bonded to the tooth surface, connected by a wire that the doctor adjusts at each visit. The wire’s tension does the work between appointments. Traditional braces handle complex cases well because the doctor controls the force precisely and continuously.

Invisalign uses a series of custom-fitted clear trays. Each tray in the series is slightly different from the last, and the cumulative change over the full series is what produces the final result. Patients swap to a new tray on a set schedule, typically every one to two weeks. The trays are removable, which makes eating, brushing, and flossing simpler. They’re also harder to see on the teeth, which matters to a lot of adults.

Which option is right depends on how much movement is needed, how crowded or spaced the teeth are, and how a patient’s bite looks overall. Some cases are genuinely suited to either option. Others have factors that make one clearly the better choice. That’s what the consultation is for.

Who Gets Orthodontic Treatment and When It Makes Sense

The short answer: a lot of different people, for a lot of different reasons. Orthodontic treatment isn’t just for teenagers with braces in school photos. It’s also for adults who had braces years ago, stopped wearing their retainer, and watched the results shift back. It’s for patients whose crowding makes it hard to clean between teeth properly, which leads to decay and gum problems over time. And it’s for people who simply want their teeth to look more even and haven’t had a good moment to deal with it until now.

For kids and teens, timing matters. Early evaluation, usually around age seven, lets the dentist identify crowding, spacing, or jaw issues while the bones are still developing. Some problems are easier to correct during growth than after. That said, not every child needs early treatment. A lot of kids do fine waiting until the permanent teeth are in and starting treatment in the early teen years.

Adults are a growing share of orthodontic patients nationwide, and that tracks with what we see at our offices. Sun City Center patients sometimes come in for orthodontic consultations alongside other restorative work they’ve been putting off for years. Patients in Gainesville often include university-age adults starting treatment for the first time. On Merritt Island, we see working parents who finally have scheduling flexibility for themselves after managing their kids’ dental care for years.

If you’ve been told your teeth are crowded, that your bite is off, or that you’re a candidate for aligners, an orthodontic consultation is the next step. If you’ve never had it looked at and you’re curious, that’s also a fine reason to come in.

What the Orthodontic Process Looks Like from First Visit to Retainer

The first appointment is a consultation, not a commitment. The doctor examines your teeth, reviews x-rays (which may already be on file if you’ve been a patient here), and talks through what movement is needed, how long it would take, and which treatment options apply to your case. You get a written estimate before any treatment starts. No surprises at checkout.

If you move forward, the next step depends on the treatment type. For Invisalign, digital scans map your teeth precisely. That data is used to design the full series of trays and to show you a projected treatment plan. For braces, the process involves bonding the brackets and placing the initial wire at the start appointment. Either way, the office handles it in-house rather than sending you to a separate facility.

Active treatment typically runs anywhere from several months to two or more years, depending on the complexity of the case and the type of treatment. You’ll have regular check-in appointments throughout. For Invisalign patients, those visits tend to be shorter and spaced further apart. For braces, adjustments happen roughly every four to six weeks.

When the teeth are in their final position, active treatment ends and retention begins. Retainers hold the teeth in place while the surrounding bone fully stabilizes. Skipping retainers is the most common reason for relapse, so we talk through the retention plan from the start, not as an afterthought at the end. If you have questions about what comes after active treatment, the retainers page covers how they work and why the timing matters.

Insurance Coverage and Payment for Orthodontic Treatment

Orthodontic coverage varies significantly from one PPO plan to the next. Some plans include a lifetime orthodontic benefit that applies to both children and adults. Others cover only patients under 18, or have a waiting period before the benefit activates. The dollar amount varies too: a plan might cover a flat $1,500 or a percentage up to a cap. The consultation is a good time to ask what your specific plan covers so you know the actual out-of-pocket number before treatment starts.

At Anytime Dental’s Gainesville and Merritt Island locations, the team works directly with most PPO dental insurance plans. At the Sun City Center office, many patients are on retiree plans or Medicare supplement policies that don’t include dental. The team there helps patients understand what their plan covers and assists with filing claims so patients aren’t navigating that paperwork alone. If you have questions about how your specific plan applies, the dental insurance page has more information on how we handle coverage across all three locations.

Financing options are available for patients who want to spread the cost of treatment over time. Ask about current financing plans when you come in for your consultation. Orthodontic treatment is one of the larger investments in dental care, so it’s worth having a complete cost picture, including what insurance pays and what financing terms look like, before you make a decision.

Most general dental practices don’t offer orthodontics at all.
