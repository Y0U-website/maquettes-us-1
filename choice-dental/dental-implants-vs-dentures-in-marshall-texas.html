What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are small titanium posts that a dentist places into the jawbone to act as artificial tooth roots. Once a post fuses with the bone, it holds a custom crown, bridge, or full set of replacement teeth firmly in place. Because the implant sits in the bone rather than on the gums, it does not slip or move when you eat or speak.

Implants can replace a single missing tooth, several teeth, or a full arch. They look and feel very close to natural teeth, which is why many patients in Marshall consider them first when comparing dental implants vs dentures. What Are Dentures?

Dentures are removable replacement teeth that rest on the gums and can be taken out for cleaning. A full denture replaces all of the teeth in the upper or lower jaw, while a partial denture fills the gaps when some natural teeth remain.

Modern dentures are lighter and more natural looking than older versions. Because they sit on the gums rather than in the bone, dentures rely on a good fit, and sometimes a mild adhesive, to stay comfortable and secure.

For many people, dentures are a good way to restore a full smile and much of their chewing ability, especially when several teeth are missing or the jawbone cannot support implants without extra treatment.

Dental Implants vs Dentures: The Key Differences

The main difference between dental implants and dentures is that implants are fixed in the jaw and do not come out, while dentures are removable and rest on the gums.   • Stability and chewing: implants are anchored in the bone and stay put, so you can bite into firm foods with confidence, while dentures restore good chewing but can take practice with very hard or sticky foods.
  • Comfort and fit: implants feel like part of your mouth, while dentures rest on the gums and may need occasional adjustment as the gums change shape.
  • Jaw and bone health: implants help preserve the jawbone because they replace the tooth root, while traditional dentures do not stimulate the bone underneath.
  • Daily care: implants are brushed and flossed like natural teeth, while dentures are removed for daily cleaning and soaking.
  • Longevity: with good care, implants can last many years and often a lifetime, while dentures usually need to be relined or replaced periodically.

Which Option Is Right for You?

The right choice between dental implants and dentures depends on your jawbone, your overall health, your budget, and how you want your replacement teeth to feel. Dental implants are often preferred by people who want a fixed solution that closely mimics natural teeth and protects the jawbone. Dentures can be a good fit for people who want a faster, removable option, or who may not be candidates for surgery.

Some patients combine the two, using a few implants to hold a denture securely in place. Because factors like bone density and gum health matter, the only way to know which option suits you best is an examination with your Choice Dental dentist in Marshall.
