Dr. Kumar will first examine your teeth and gums to determine if you are a suitable candidate for veneers. He will also discuss with you the goals and expectations for your treatment.

Dr. Kumar will remove a thin layer of enamel from the front surface of the teeth that will receive the veneers. This is typically done using local anesthesia to ensure your comfort.

At one of the best dental laboratories selected by Dr. Kumar, a skilled technician (master ceramist) will use the impression to create custom-made veneers that match the shape, size, and color of the patient’s natural teeth. In the lab that is used by Avon Family and Cosmetic Dentistry, the technicians use a microscope to fabricate their dental restorations which results in better overall quality restoration including better fitting margins, shade, character, shape, and size of the restoration.

Once the veneers are ready, Dr. Kumar and his team will check their fit and color and make any necessary adjustments. A try-in paste may also be utilized to check the fit of the veneers. Dr. Kumar will then use special dental bonding cement to bond the veneers to the prepared teeth.

Our office will schedule a follow-up appointment to check your bite and ensure that the veneers are functioning properly.

FAQs about Dental Veneers

General FAQs

While dental veneers can address a variety of cosmetic dental issues, not everyone is a suitable candidate. Your dentist will evaluate your oral health, the condition of your teeth, and your overall dental history to determine if veneers are the right option for you. Additionally, patients with significant tooth decay, gum disease, or inadequate enamel may not be good candidates for veneers.

While dental veneers do not require any special care beyond regular brushing, flossing, and dental check-ups, it is important to avoid biting down on hard objects, such as ice or your fingernails, which can damage the veneers. Additionally, if you grind or clench your teeth at night, your dentist may recommend a nightguard to protect your veneers.

Porcelain veneers are resistant to staining and discoloration, so they are a good option if you are concerned about maintaining a bright, white smile. However, composite resin veneers may be more prone to staining over time, particularly if you consume dark-colored foods and drinks like coffee or red wine.

Dental veneers can last anywhere from 7 to 15 years on average, depending on the material they are made from and how well they are cared for. Porcelain veneers are generally more durable than composite resin veneers, but they can be more expensive.

Porcelain veneers are made from a durable, stain-resistant material that closely resembles natural tooth enamel. They are typically more expensive than composite veneers but are known for their longevity and aesthetic appeal. Composite veneers are made from a tooth-colored resin material and are generally more affordable. However, they may not last as long as porcelain veneers and are more prone to staining.
