Here’s What Happens When You Lose a Tooth

Even if you think you can live with a gap in your smile, it’s not optimal for your oral health. Teeth adjacent to the gap can start to tilt and shift, leading to tooth and bite misalignment. Eventually, the loss of one tooth can lead to the loss of more teeth due to wear and jawbone shrinkage. This is why tooth replacement is crucial.

If a tooth and its roots are lost, the bone in your jaw will start to break down without a tooth root to promote healthy bone regeneration. As bone loss continues, your facial features lose support, and you will start to take on a drawn or aged appearance as well.

Taking Care of Your New Smile

Dental implants are simple to care for because you treat them like natural teeth. This includes following best practices like visiting your dentist regularly for dental exams and cleanings in Bellevue, NE . This lowers your gum disease risks, which can cause your implants to fail and lead to further tooth loss.

You should also be brushing and flossing your teeth at least twice each day, especially before bed. It is important not to sleep with debris on your teeth and gums. A friendly staff member, including our dentist or hygienist, can provide tips to help you achieve the best results from your at-home routine.

As with natural teeth, you do not want to use your dental implant as a tool to open a bottle or as a device to crush ice. This can lead to tooth or implant crown fractures. You should also talk to us if you grind and clench your teeth (bruxism). Because this happens during sleep, you may not know you’re doing it, but your dentist can spot the signs during your routine dental checkup and create a custom appliance for night use. This appliance prevents nocturnal bruxism, which can damage your teeth and your implant.

If you work around unpredictable machinery or play on a sports team, consider a mouthguard to keep your teeth and implants safe.

How long does dental implant surgery take?

For a single implant, the surgery generally involves several steps. First, we administer local anesthesia to ensure you are comfortable. Then, we make a small incision in the gum to expose the bone and place the implant into the jawbone. After securing the implant, we close the gum with stitches. This process usually takes about an hour, but it can be longer if multiple implants are being placed or if additional procedures, such as bone grafting, are required.

Recovery time also plays a crucial role in the overall timeline. After the surgery, the implant needs to integrate with the jawbone in a process called osseointegration, which can take several months. Once this healing period is complete, you will return to have the abutment and crown placed, finalizing your dental implant.

What is the best option for replacing missing teeth?

If you have a missing tooth (or teeth), you may have difficulty chewing your food or speaking clearly. You might also feel embarrassed to smile or laugh with friends and colleagues. Your Bellevue, NE dentist offers tooth replacement options to restore your bite and your confidence.

Tooth Replacement Options for Missing Adult Teeth

We offer dental implants and dental bridges in our Bellevue dentist office because these are the best options for replacing teeth.

Both are fixed restorations, but there are key differences.

A dental implant is an artificial tooth root (or roots) that we cap with a dental crown or a prosthetic retained denture or bridge. Dental implants are most like natural teeth; they are easy to care for and are known for their longevity.

A dental bridge is one or two replacement teeth situated between dental crowns. The tooth crowns cap healthy teeth, allowing the bridge teeth to close the gap in your smile. A bridge requires a special cleaning brush for maintenance and is not typically as long-lasting as the dental implant.

The option that is best for you depends on your smile goals. Your restorative dentist will help you decide following your tooth replacement consultation.

Can you get dental implants if your gums are infected?

Patients shouldn’t have issues with their dental implants if there’s no active oral infection. Even patients who have suffered from advanced periodontal (gum) disease with bone loss may be able to get dental implants. However, this would require bone augmentation with a bone graft before the implant placement.

Patients who have had gum disease or are prone to gum inflammation should take special care with their oral health and hygiene to maintain their gums and dental implants. Severe gum inflammation while wearing dental implants can result in implant failure and require another tooth replacement treatment.

Why are dental implants necessary?

Dental implants are needed when you have teeth missing from your smile. Missing teeth can make you self-conscious about your smile and cause you to develop oral health issues. With a gap in your smile, your remaining teeth may begin to shift, leading to bite misalignment and difficulty chewing.

Additionally, without your natural tooth and root, your jawbone can begin to atrophy, which can cause your face to change shape as your jawbone breaks down.

Basically, tooth replacement is critical when you have gaps in your smile. As far as teeth replacement options are concerned, dental implants are the closest choice to natural teeth. For that reason, our dentists at Kennedy Dental often recommend dental implants over other tooth replacement options.
