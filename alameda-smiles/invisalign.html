Invisalign vs. Traditional Braces — Which Is Right for You?

This is the question we hear most often. Here’s an honest comparison to help you decide:

Visibility: Invisalign trays are virtually invisible. Traditional braces have metal brackets and wires that are obvious when you smile or talk. For adults in professional settings and teens who are self-conscious, this alone is often the deciding factor.

Comfort: Invisalign trays are smooth plastic. No sharp brackets cutting the inside of your cheeks, no wires poking your gums. Braces can cause irritation, especially after adjustments.

Eating: Remove your Invisalign trays and eat whatever you want. With braces, you’ll need to avoid hard, sticky, and crunchy foods for the entire treatment.

Cleaning: Pop out Invisalign, brush and floss normally. Braces require special brushes, floss threaders, and extra time to clean around brackets.

Treatment time: Both typically take 12 to 18 months for moderate cases. Simple Invisalign cases can finish in 6 months.

Cost: Comparable. Insurance covers both the same way in most plans.

Best for: Invisalign handles mild to moderate crowding, spacing, overbites, underbites, and crossbites effectively. Severe jaw misalignment or very complex cases may still benefit from traditional braces. Dr. Chan will tell you honestly which option gives you the best result.

For most adults and teens, Invisalign is the clear choice. You get the same results without the metal mouth look, the food restrictions, or the monthly tightening appointments.

Invisalign Treatment Timeline at Alameda Smiles

Knowing what to expect takes the uncertainty out of treatment. Here’s your Invisalign timeline with Dr. Chan:

Week 1 — Consultation and digital scan. Dr. Chan examines your teeth, takes a 3D digital scan (no goopy impressions), and shows you a simulation of your finished smile. You’ll see the projected result before committing.

Week 2 — Custom trays arrive. Invisalign’s lab fabricates your entire set of aligners based on Dr. Chan’s treatment plan. You receive your first set and start wearing them.

Weeks 2 through 52 — Wear and swap. Wear your trays 20 to 22 hours per day. Swap to the next set every two weeks. Each tray makes small, precise movements.

Every 6 to 8 weeks — Check-in with Dr. Chan. Quick appointments to verify your teeth are tracking correctly. Most check-ins take 15 to 20 minutes.

Month 12 to 18 — Final tray and retainer fitting. Once your teeth reach their target positions, Dr. Chan fits you for a retainer to lock in your results.

Ongoing — Night retainer. Wear your retainer at night to maintain your new smile long-term. This is the part most people skip. Don’t skip it.
