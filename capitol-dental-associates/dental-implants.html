Dental Implants

  • How do dental implants work? Dental implants fuse to your jawbone and provide stable support for teeth.
  • What are some types of dental implants? Endosteal implants these are surgically implanted directly into the jawbone. Once the surrounding gum tissue has healed, a second surgery is needed to connect a post to the original implant. Finally, an artificial tooth (or teeth) is attached to the post individually or grouped on a bridge or denture.
  • Subperiosteal implants these consist of a metal frame that is fitted onto the jawbone just below the gum tissue. As the gums heal, the frame becomes fixed to the jawbone. Posts, which are attached to the frame, protrude through the gums. As with endosteal implants, artificial teeth are then mounted to the posts.
  • What are some types of dental implant treatments? Bone augmentation This can involve restoring bone in your jaw so that it can support dental implants.
  • Sinus lift This can involve adding bone under the sinus in order to fight deterioration and support missing upper back teeth.
  • Ridge expansion If your jaw isn’t wide enough support dental implants, bone grafts can be added to add space along the top of your jaw.

Dental Implants Common Questions

  • What is Dental Implant Surgery? Implants are surgically placed into the jaw for a more permanent solution to tooth loss. They are a longer-term option than, say, a bridge.
  • Should I get Dental Implants? You might be a great candidate for a dental implant if you are missing a tooth or multiple teeth; if you can’t wear dentures and if you don’t want to worry about coming out.
  • How long does it take to get dental implants? The length of time it takes for a dental implant to be placed depends upon a number of factors including physiological ones. It is possible that your implant will have to be placed in more than one visit.
  • What are the benefits of dental implants? Implants often outstrip the benefits of dentures in that you don’t have to take them out to clean them, they look more like real teeth, they feel like your regular teeth, and you don’t have to worry about them coming out.
  • Are dental implants safe? Yes. Before undergoing the implant surgery you’ll have to go through an evaluation to make sure you’re a good candidate, but if approved the materials and tools used in the procedure are safe.
  • How should I start? Schedule an appointment for an evaluation and we will help you get on your way to great oral health.
