Dental Implants

Missing a tooth changes more than just your appearance. It affects how you chew, how clearly you speak, and what happens to your jawbone over time. At Belgrade Family Dental, Dr. Jennifer Lehnhardt and our team offer dental implants in Belgrade that replace missing teeth from the root up, giving you a solution that functions as your natural teeth did.

Implants are the only tooth replacement option that actually prevents bone loss and doesn’t require altering healthy neighboring teeth. That combination makes them the gold standard for permanent tooth replacement.

How Dental Implants Work

An implant consists of three parts: a titanium post that acts as the tooth root, an abutment that connects to the post, and a crown that looks and functions like a natural tooth. The post gets surgically placed into your jawbone where the missing tooth used to be.

Over the next few months, something remarkable happens. Your bone grows around the titanium post and fuses with it through a process called osseointegration. This creates an incredibly strong foundation—strong enough to handle the same biting and chewing forces that natural teeth endure.

Once the post has fully integrated with the bone, the abutment and crown are attached. The result is a stable replacement tooth that looks natural and can last for decades.

Why Bone Health Matters

Your jawbone stays healthy because your tooth roots stimulate it every time you bite or chew. When you lose a tooth, that stimulation disappears. The bone in that area starts to deteriorate because your body figures it’s not needed anymore.

This bone loss doesn’t just affect that one spot. It can change the shape of your face over time, creating a sunken appearance around your mouth. It also makes future tooth replacement more complicated because there’s less bone to work with.

Dental implants in Belgrade solve this problem by replacing the root along with the visible tooth. The titanium post stimulates the bone just like a natural root does, maintaining bone density and preserving your facial structure.

Bridges and dentures sit on top of the gums without addressing the bone issue at all. That’s why people who’ve worn dentures for years often develop that collapsed look around their mouth—the bone has been slowly disappearing underneath.

The Implant Process Step by Step

Getting an implant isn’t a quick procedure, but the timeline exists for good reasons. Rushing the process leads to failures.

First comes a comprehensive exam, including X-rays or a CT scan to assess your bone structure. The dentist needs to confirm there’s enough healthy bone to support an implant. If there isn’t, bone grafting might be necessary first, which adds several months to the timeline.

During the surgical appointment, the titanium post is placed into the jawbone. This is done under local anesthesia, and most people report that the procedure itself is less uncomfortable than they expected. Some practices offer sedation options if you’re particularly anxious.

The healing period lasts three to six months. During this time, you’ll have a temporary restoration so you’re not walking around with a visible gap. The bone gradually fuses with the implant post, creating the stable foundation needed for the final crown.

Once integration is complete, the abutment gets attached to the post, and impressions are taken for your custom crown. A couple of weeks later, the permanent crown is placed and adjusted to match your bite perfectly.

Who Makes a Good Candidate

Most healthy adults can get implants, but certain factors affect success rates. You need adequate bone density and height in the area where the implant will go. Heavy smokers face higher failure rates because smoking interferes with healing and bone integration.

Uncontrolled diabetes or gum disease can also complicate the process. These conditions need to be managed before implant surgery. People taking certain medications, particularly those that affect bone metabolism, might need to adjust their treatment plan.

Age isn’t usually a barrier. Older adults can be excellent candidates as long as they’re healthy enough for the procedure. The main requirement is that your jawbone has finished growing, which is why implants aren’t typically recommended for teenagers.

Caring for Your Implant

Once your implant is in place and healed, you care for it exactly like a natural tooth. Brush twice daily, floss around it carefully, and see your dentist for regular checkups and cleanings.

The crown can still develop plaque buildup, and the gums around the implant can become infected if you don’t keep them clean. This condition, called peri-implantitis, can threaten the implant if left untreated.

Most implant failures happen in the first year when integration is still developing. After that, the success rate is extremely high. The crown may eventually need replacement—crowns typically last fifteen years or so—but the implant post itself can last a lifetime.
