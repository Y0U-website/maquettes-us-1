Scaling and Root Planing

This treatment helps stop the progression of gum disease, promotes healing, and reduces the depth of periodontal pockets.

  • Root Planing smooths root surfaces, helping the gums reattach to the teeth and making it harder for bacteria to accumulate again.

Why Would You Need Scaling and Root Planing?

This procedure is typically recommended for patients diagnosed with gingivitis or early-to-moderate periodontitis, especially when routine cleanings are no longer sufficient.

Ignoring these symptoms allows gum disease to worsen, leading to significant oral health challenges.

  • Gums that bleed easily
  • Persistent bad breath (halitosis)
  • Red, swollen, or tender gums
  • Receding gums or teeth that appear longer
  • Loose teeth or a change in bite alignment
  • Deep pockets that form between your teeth and gums

The Benefits of Scaling and Root Planing

Getting a deep cleaning can be a turning point in your oral health journey. Here’s what SRP can do for you:

Stops Disease Progression SRP removes the root cause of inflammation—plaque and bacteria—helping to stop gum disease in its tracks .

Prevents Tooth Loss By healing the gums and reducing pocket depth, SRP stabilizes teeth and reduces the risk of them becoming loose or falling out.

Improves Breath and Appearance Removing hidden bacteria eliminates odor and helps your gums return to a healthy pink color , revitalizing your smile.

Protects Your General Health Gum disease has been linked to heart disease, diabetes, and respiratory issues. Managing oral inflammation may help reduce your risk for these conditions.

Minimally Invasive Unlike surgery, scaling and root planing is a conservative treatment that doesn’t require cutting or stitches.

What Happens If You Don’t Get Treatment?

Avoiding or delaying scaling and root planing can lead to serious consequences:

In short, what starts as a simple inflammation can cascade into complex and costly oral health problems.

  • Worsening Gum Disease : Gingivitis can progress into periodontitis, a condition that leads to irreversible bone loss.
  • Tooth Mobility and Loss : As the infection spreads, teeth can become loose and may eventually fall out or need extraction.
  • Systemic Health Impact : Chronic oral infections may contribute to inflammation throughout the body, affecting your heart, lungs, and metabolic health.
  • Costlier Treatments Later : Advanced periodontitis often requires surgical procedures and restorative work like implants or dentures.
