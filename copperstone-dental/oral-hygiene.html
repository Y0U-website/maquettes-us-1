Digital Dental X-Rays

Your dental cleaning and prevention plan at Copperstone Dental includes regularly having x-rays taken of your teeth. Our office is equipped with the latest x-ray technology, digital radiography, which uses an electronic sensor to capture more accurate, digital images of your teeth.

The digital x-ray process is much faster and more comfortable for our patients than traditional x-rays, and the radiation emitted from the digital x-ray machine is less than what is emitted from a microwave oven, a common household appliance. And, because the digital x-ray images can be instantly viewed, our dental team will be able to detect and diagnose any potential problems much faster than ever before.

Along with the low radiation and instant viewing, this x-ray machine allows your dentist, Dr. Matthew Tuft, the opportunity to closely zoom in on the picture to evaluate small characteristics that would not typically be seen with the naked eye.

Digital x-rays in Meridian, ID, also have the ability to compare new x-rays to old x-rays and identify any changes that have been made. This is called subtraction radiography. When the machine is commanded to compare the two images, it will subtract any section of the images that are the exact same. This will then leave the changes apparent on the screen, big or small. This allows us to completely evaluate your overall health and take the necessary steps to proceed. To learn more about digital x-rays, call us now!

Periodontal Care

At Copperstone Dental, we aim to not only take care of your teeth, but to also take care of the tissues, gums, and bones that support your teeth. We take periodontal disease very seriously at our practice, and we strive prevent it, diagnose it, and treat it in every way we can.

There are many signs that can be associated with periodontal disease, which include excessive bleeding in the gums, persistent bad breath, receding gums, and loose teeth. If you’re suffering from these symptoms, we invite you to come into our office as soon as possible.

Periodontal disease occurs when the plaque on your teeth remains untreated. It will eventually release toxins into your oral cavity and irritate your gums, making them bleed and shrivel away from the teeth. If this continues, the toxins attack the bone that holds your teeth in place. Once the bone begins to deteriorate, your teeth become loose and eventually fall out.

To help maintain your overall oral health, we offer a variety of periodontal and deep cleaning services, including scaling and root planing. Together, we can work to help you achieve optimal oral health. Teeth Cleanings

Annual teeth cleanings are very important to your oral health. Along with routine oral care, a professional teeth cleaning helps to remove and prevent harmful bacteria from lurking in your teeth and gums. When you practice good oral care with daily brushing and flossing, you prevent plaque from colonizing on your teeth that damage your tooth enamel.

Teeth Cleaning Procedure

At Copperstone Dental, our professional teeth cleaning consists of four simple steps your hygienist will take to thoroughly clean your teeth and gums:

  • Dental Exam: Before a scheduled cleaning, your dental hygienist will perform an exam to check your teeth for signs of gingivitis or other potential issues. This is done with a specialized mirror that allows the hygienist to check hard to see areas, such as behind the front teeth and near the back molars. If no issues are detected, your hygienist will continue with the cleaning.
  • Plaque Removal: Your hygienist will use a specialized tool to gently remove plaque and tartar in between your teeth and around the gum line.
  • Teeth Cleaning: Once your teeth have been cleared, your teeth will be brushed with a gritty toothpaste and high-powered toothbrush to remove any lurking bacteria. The grittiness of the toothpaste provides a gentle scrub to thoroughly clean your teeth, while providing a quality tooth polish. If done correctly, this helps to restore the natural color of your teeth without damaging the enamel.
  • Flossing: After the brushing is complete, your hygienist will use a thick floss to remove any remaining bacteria left in the gums.

Fluoride Treatments

Fluoride treatment is a type of gel or paste used as a protective shield to help prevent cavities. After your cleaning, your hygienist will coat the gel on your teeth and leave for about one minute, then rinse out with a diluted mouth rinse.

After Dental Care Steps

It is inevitable that plaque causing bacteria will begin to form within 48 hours after a teeth cleaning, which is why it’s important to practice good oral health care. Brushing twice a day and flossing once per day, or after each meal, will help to keep your teeth healthy and tartar-free!

Helpful Oral Hygiene and Fluoride Treatments Information

By incorporating these oral hygiene practices into your daily routine and maintaining regular dental visits, you can help ensure the health and longevity of your teeth and gums. Healthy teeth and gums contribute to overall well-being and quality of life.

While fluoride offers significant benefits for oral health, it’s essential to use it appropriately and in moderation. Excessive fluoride consumption can lead to dental fluorosis, a condition characterized by white spots or streaks on the teeth. Talk to your dentist about the appropriate use of fluoride products and any concerns you may have about fluoride intake. With proper use, fluoride can be a valuable tool in maintaining a healthy smile and preventing dental problems.

Overall, when used as directed and in recommended amounts, fluoride is a safe and effective tool for preventing tooth decay and promoting oral health. It’s important to strike a balance between benefiting from fluoride’s protective effects and avoiding excessive intake to minimize the risk of dental fluorosis. If you have any questions or concerns about fluoride safety, don’t hesitate to discuss them with your dentist or healthcare provider.

  • What are Oral Hygiene and Fluoride Treatments? Oral hygiene refers to the practice of maintaining the cleanliness of the mouth, teeth, and gums to prevent dental problems such as cavities, gum disease, bad breath, and tooth loss. Good oral hygiene habits are essential for overall health and well-being, as oral health is closely linked to systemic health.
  • Key Aspects of Oral Hygiene Brushing: Brushing your teeth at least twice a day is crucial for removing plaque, bacteria, and food particles from the surfaces of your teeth. Use a fluoride toothpaste and a soft-bristled toothbrush, and brush in gentle circular motions to clean all tooth surfaces, including the front, back, and chewing surfaces. Be sure to brush for at least two minutes each time.
  • Flossing: Flossing helps remove plaque and food debris from between the teeth and along the gumline, where toothbrush bristles cannot reach. Floss at least once a day, using a gentle back-and-forth motion to clean between each tooth. You can also use interdental brushes or water flossers as alternatives to traditional dental floss.
  • Rinsing: Mouthwash can help reduce plaque and bacteria, freshen breath, and promote healthy gums. Use an antimicrobial mouthwash containing fluoride, and swish it around in your mouth for 30 to 60 seconds before spitting it out. However, mouthwash should not be used as a substitute for brushing and flossing.
  • Healthy Diet: A balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, and whole grains provides essential nutrients for healthy teeth and gums. Limit sugary and acidic foods and beverages, as they can contribute to tooth decay and erosion.
  • Regular Dental Check-ups: Routine dental visits are essential for maintaining oral health. Your dentist can detect early signs of dental problems, perform professional cleanings, and provide personalized oral hygiene advice. Schedule dental check-ups at least twice a year or as recommended by your dentist.
  • Quit Smoking: Smoking and tobacco use can increase the risk of gum disease, tooth decay, oral cancer, and other oral health problems. Quitting smoking can significantly improve your oral and overall health.
  • Protective Measures: Wear a mouthguard when participating in sports or recreational activities to protect your teeth from injury. Avoid using your teeth as tools to open bottles or packages, as this can cause damage to your teeth and gums.
  • Remineralization: Fluoride promotes remineralization, the process by which minerals such as calcium and phosphate are deposited back into the enamel, repairing early stages of tooth decay before cavities form.
  • Reduces Acid Production: Fluoride inhibits the growth of bacteria that produce acid in the mouth, which can erode tooth enamel and lead to cavities.
  • Some Ways Fluoride Can Be Incorporated Into Your Oral Hygiene Routine Fluoride Toothpaste: Use a fluoride toothpaste approved by dental professionals. Brush your teeth at least twice a day, preferably after meals, to ensure consistent exposure to fluoride.
  • Fluoride Mouthwash: Rinse with fluoride mouthwash after brushing to help strengthen enamel and protect against cavities. Mouthwash containing fluoride can reach areas of the mouth that may be difficult to clean with brushing and flossing alone.
  • Professional Fluoride Treatments: During dental check-ups, your dentist may apply a fluoride gel or varnish to your teeth to provide additional protection against cavities. This is particularly beneficial for individuals at higher risk of tooth decay, such as children, seniors, and those with certain medical conditions.
  • Fluoridated Water: Many community water supplies are fluoridated, meaning they contain fluoride at optimal levels to promote dental health. Drinking fluoridated water can help strengthen teeth and reduce the risk of cavities. If your water supply is not fluoridated, your dentist may recommend fluoride supplements or other sources of fluoride.
  • Monitor Fluoride Intake: Be aware of other potential sources of fluoride, such as fluoridated water and certain foods and beverages. If you live in an area with fluoridated water, ensure that your overall fluoride intake does not exceed recommended levels.
  • Consult with a Dentist: Talk to your dentist about fluoride and its role in oral health. They can provide personalized recommendations based on your dental needs and any concerns you may have about fluoride use.
  • Seek Professional Dental Care: Regular dental check-ups are essential for maintaining oral health and ensuring that fluoride is being used appropriately. Your dentist can assess your fluoride needs and provide professional fluoride treatments as needed.
