Why Someone Might Need Dentures (Full Dentures)

Full dentures are used when all or most teeth in an arch (upper or lower) are missing. They help with:

Missing many teeth makes eating difficult. Dentures allow people to chew a wider variety of foods again.

Teeth play a big role in forming sounds. Dentures help restore proper pronunciation.

Dentures replace missing teeth and support facial muscles, preventing the “sunken” look that happens after tooth loss.

A complete, natural-looking smile helps improve self-esteem.

While dentures don’t stop bone loss entirely, replacing missing teeth helps maintain facial structure better than leaving gums bare.

Partial dentures are used when a person still has some natural teeth remaining . They’re often recommended for:

1. Filling gaps caused by multiple missing teeth

They prevent shifting of the remaining natural teeth, which can otherwise move out of place.

With missing teeth, chewing can become difficult or uneven; partial dentures help share the workload.

They help avoid excessive wear on remaining teeth that may be compensating for missing ones.

Even a few missing teeth can change how someone smiles or speaks; partials restore a natural look.
