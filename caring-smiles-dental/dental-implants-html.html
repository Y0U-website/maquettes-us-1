What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are small posts, or cylinders, that are usually made of medical-grade titanium alloy. They get surgically placed beneath the gumline during a minimally invasive procedure. Once they are inserted into the jawbone, they fuse with the surrounding tissue, a process that allows them to act as prosthetic tooth roots. They can provide a remarkably stable base of support or various types of restorations, including crowns, bridges, and dentures.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

The dental implant process isn’t exactly the same for every patient, but there are four main steps that are generally followed. Since getting dental implants involves a significant investment of time and money, it is crucial to have an accurate idea of what to expect before you commit to the treatment. Below is a brief overview of how dental implants work in Beaverton.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

At your initial consultation, we’ll discuss whether dental implants are the right choice for replacing your missing teeth. We’ll perform a thorough evaluation of your mouth and jaw with the help of the latest diagnostic technology. If we find that you are a viable candidate for dental implants, we will walk you through what the rest of the process will look like. Note that in some cases, bone grafting and other preliminary treatments may be required before dental implants can be placed.

Dental Implant Surgery

Your dental implant surgery will be handled by a highly trained local specialist. Step one of the procedure is to apply a local anesthetic to numb your mouth. After that, the gums are opened so that the specialist can work with the jawbone. A predetermined number of implant posts will be carefully inserted into the jaw in specific locations at carefully chosen angles. After the gum tissue has been sutured shut, small caps will be placed on the implant posts. These caps help the gums maintain their proper shape while they heal.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

Osseointegration allows your dental implant posts to fuse with the jawbone, which is critical for their long-term success. Depending on a number of factors (such as the number of implant posts placed), osseointegration can take anywhere between three and six months.

After osseointegration comes abutment placement. An abutment is a small connector piece that is attached to the implant posts. Its purpose is to keep a crown, bridge, or denture anchored in place so that it can act as your replacement teeth. Once the abutments have been placed, we will wait until your mouth has fully healed before moving on to the last stage of the dental implant process.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s)

Based on careful impressions of your mouth, a lifelike prosthesis will be designed. Each dental implant prosthesis provided by our team is carefully personalized in order to ensure that it looks natural and fits comfortably.

The completed prosthesis is attached to your implant posts during a final appointment at our office. We’ll perform a last-minute check to make sure that everything looks good. Once that’s done, you’ll be ready to start enjoying the full benefits of your newly completed smile!

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

The dental implant procedure has an incredibly high success rate, but that doesn’t mean your new teeth are invincible. You must care for them properly to prevent complications such as infection, failed osseointegration, or other issues.

Fortunately, there are several ways you can help keep your restorations looking and feeling great. Continue reading to learn more about how you can help maintain your dental implants for years to come!

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Your prosthetics aren’t made of the same organic materials as your enamel, so thankfully, they cannot develop cavities. However, bacteria in your mouth can penetrate the site and cause disease, such as peri-implantitis, the leading cause of failure.

This can be avoided by brushing twice daily at home, then flossing around your teeth and implant cautiously so you don’t accidentally damage its protective seal.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Dental implants are the only replacement teeth that are placed in your jaw and fuse with the bone, which can return up to 80 % or more of your original bite force. As a result, they don’t require as many dietary restrictions as dentures or bridges.

That means you can enjoy a wider variety of wholesome foods to support your oral and overall health. For instance, many citrus fruits and leafy greens that require sufficient chewing are high in vitamin C to support your connective tissues. And different dairy products, like milk, cheese, and yogurt, are rich in calcium for strong teeth and bones.

Break Bad Habits

Smokers are significantly more likely to experience implant failure because tobacco products contain nicotine, which functions as a vasoconstrictor in the body. It reduces blood oxygen levels and flow, so your systems are unable to deliver essential nutrients or infection-fighting cells to your mouth during recovery from oral surgery.

You should also try not to use your teeth to open bottles, mail, or other packaging, as this can damage your restoration or cause it to become unstable or dislodged while the site heals.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Athletes and others who participate in close-contact activities are prone to sustaining injuries that can damage or dislodge their teeth, including their implant(s). Fortunately, this can be avoided by wearing a custom-fit mouthguard to help shield your smile if you take a blow to the face.

Our team in Beaverton can also provide nightguards to prevent bruxism, also known as teeth grinding, which sometimes occurs subconsciously at night and can place excessive pressure on your new tooth, causing it to fail.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Seeing Dr. Appelhans every six months for a routine examination and professional cleaning allows our team to monitor your dental condition, so we can catch and treat any areas of concern, such as a destabilized dental implant, before they become more problematic.
