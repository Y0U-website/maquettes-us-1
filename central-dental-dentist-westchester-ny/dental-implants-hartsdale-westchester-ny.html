What are the types of dental implants?

There are three types of dental implants. They include Endosteal, subperiosteal, and zygomatic.

  • Endosteal implants: this is the most common type of dental implant, and it is suited for most patients. However, the patient must have a good and healthy jawbone to qualify for this procedure. It involves surgically placing various cylinders or blades and screws into the jawbone.
  • Subperiosteal implants: this is the second most popular type of implant often used as an alternative to endosteal implants. Instead of fixing the implant into the jawbone, it is placed on top of the jaw with a metal frame post. The framework protrudes through the gum and holds the implant in place. Patients who don’t have sufficient jawbones or don’t want to go through intensive oral surgery or bone grafting are suitable for subperiosteal implants.
  • Zygomatic implants: the less popular zygomatic implant is the most complicated of the three types. It is placed on the cheekbone instead of the jawbone.

FAQs About Dental Implants

How much do dental implants cost?

No two dental procedures are the same. As a result, the cost varies depending on the complexity and parts involved. However, the overall cost per tooth varies between $2,500 $3,000, and the option for the entire teeth is $30,000 $40,000. Not to worry, some dental insurance covers a small portion of the total cost. Therefore, speak with your insurance provider to know the cost you will be responsible for.

How long does a dental implant last?

A dental implant can last between 20 – 30 years since they are designed as a permanent solution for tooth loss. However, factors like oral hygiene and maintenance ultimately determine the longevity of dental implants.

How painful are dental implants?

Dental implants are not usually painful since the mouth is numbed during the procedure. Any associated pain will last 2 -3 hours in the first 24 to 48 hours after surgery. Contrary to popular belief, the pain you feel after the surgery is not from the hole made for the implant. Instead, it comes from soft tissue manipulation that occurs during the process. If the procedure requires bone grafting, expect a high degree of pain that can last one to three days. Nonetheless, your dentist will give you a strong pain reliever to make you as comfortable as possible.

What is better: bridge or implant?

A bridge is better if you are missing more than one consecutive tooth. Also, it doesn’t require invasive surgery and takes little time to fix. On the downside, it loses its appearance with age, can damage healthy teeth around the missing tooth, and has a higher chance of causing tooth decay.

An implant is more durable and offers longer protection. It also retains its appearance longer than bridges and has a higher success rate. Although it won’t damage healthy teeth around the implant, the process is more intense. Also, it costs more.

How many teeth can one implant support?

The number of teeth the implant will support depends on the type of implant. For example, two dental implants can support two teeth, while an all-on-4 procedure can support a full set of upper and lower teeth.

Can all teeth be replaced with implants?

Yes, all your teeth can be replaced with multiple implants. As we explained above, four dental implants are suitable for patients who have lost all their teeth throughout an arch. In some cases, your dentist will recommend six or more implants.

When bone grafting is required?

Bone grafting is necessary for dental implant procedures when your jawbone is too soft or not thick enough. Since chewing and biting exert pressure on the bone, the surgery will fail without the necessary jawbone to support the implant. Therefore, your dentist will source bone grafts from another location in your body or use a synthetic bone graft to provide structures for new bone growth. Unlike the regular procedure, bone grafting extends treatment by more months since the transplanted bone needs to grow enough to support the implant.
