Pediatric Dentistry FAQs

  • When should my child see a dentist? The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry website(open in new window) recommends your child see a dentist when his or her first tooth arrives or no later than the first birthday.
  • Should I worry about my child’s baby teeth? Yes! It’s crucial to care for your children’s baby teeth. They help children speak clearly and chew comfortably. Plus, they act as a path for permanent teeth when they erupt so that they’re correctly aligned.
  • How often should my child see the dentist? The same recommendation for adults applies to children every six months. Biyearly visits to the dentist allow us to help prevent cavities and other dental diseases. We believe in the prevention, not just treatment, of dental disease in children. Children with great smiles under our care are not only healthier but have higher self-esteem. The greater the focus on preventative care, the better opportunity for kids to live a happy, healthy life.
  • How can my child prevent cavities? During your child’s consultation, we discuss cavity prevention strategies with you to ensure optimal care for your child. Something you can do right away get your child into the habit of brushing and flossing every day. Another step includes limiting your child’s exposure to sugar, which can wear away tooth enamel and create decay.
  • Should I worry about thumb sucking? Pacifiers and thumb sucking are perfectly normal for infants. Most children are ready to stop these habits around the age of three. However, if it continues past this point, it may alter your child’s bite, jaw form, and teeth. Don’t worry, though. We can go over your concerns during the pediatric dental consultation and figure out a strategy to limit these habits.
