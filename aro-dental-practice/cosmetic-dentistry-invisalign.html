How Cosmetic Dentistry Pricing Typically Works

People ask us about cost before they ask almost anything else. Fair enough. But cosmetic dentistry doesn’t work off one flat number. It works off the smile in front of us.

Every case starts with what you actually need. A single chipped tooth is a different job than a full set of veneers. Material matters too; porcelain behaves differently than composite bonding, and each has its own process. The number of teeth involved changes things, and so does whether we need imaging first to plan the work.

We use 3D imaging to plan cosmetic cases here, which helps us map out exactly what a treatment will involve before we start. That planning step is part of why costs vary case to case, not visit to visit.

It’s worth asking any provider what’s actually driving the cost of your specific plan. A good office should walk you through that breakdown without rushing you. If they can’t explain it in plain terms, that’s a sign to keep asking questions.

  • Type of treatment, from whitening to veneers to bonding
  • Material used and how it’s fabricated
  • How many teeth are being treated
  • Whether 3D imaging or extra diagnostics are needed first
  • How much prep work the teeth require before the cosmetic step

What to Expect After Your Cosmetic Treatment

So you just walked out with a new smile. Now what? Most patients feel a little strange for the first day or two, and that’s normal. Your lips and cheeks need time to learn the new shape of your teeth, especially after veneers or bonding. Some mild sensitivity to hot or cold is common the first week, and it usually fades on its own.

We tell every patient the same thing before they leave the chair: give your mouth a few days before you judge the results. Your tongue will notice every little edge at first. That’s just your brain mapping the new terrain, not a sign something’s wrong.

Bite adjustment is the one thing we watch closest. If your teeth don’t meet evenly, even by a hair, you’ll feel it within a day or two, usually as jaw tightness or a spot that keeps getting hit first. That’s an easy fix at a quick follow-up, not something you should just live with.

Dr. Aro has spent over 20 years fine-tuning this kind of detail work in San Luis Obispo, and he’d rather see you back for a five-minute adjustment than have you quietly grinding through discomfort at home.

Give it real time before you decide how it looks and feels.

  • Stick to softer foods for a day or two if you had bonding or contouring work done
  • Avoid biting into hard foods like ice or hard candy that could chip new bonding material
  • Brush and floss like normal; cosmetic work doesn’t change your daily care routine
  • Call us if you notice a sharp edge, a rough spot, or your bite feels off when you close down
