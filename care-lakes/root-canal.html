Treating the Problem From Inside the Tooth

Inside every tooth is soft tissue called the dental pulp, which contains nerves and blood vessels. When this tissue becomes inflamed or infected because of deep decay, cracks, trauma, or other damage, it can cause significant pain and eventually threaten the tooth.

Root canal therapy involves removing the affected tissue from inside the tooth, cleaning and sealing the area, and restoring the tooth so it can continue functioning as part of your smile.

Rather than removing the entire tooth, the goal is to preserve as much of your natural smile as possible.

Dental pain should never be ignored. While these symptoms do not automatically mean you need root canal treatment, they are good reasons to schedule an evaluation.

Pain that continues, returns frequently, or interferes with everyday activities should be evaluated by a dentist.

Sensitivity that lingers after eating or drinking something hot or cold may indicate a problem inside the tooth.

Tenderness or discomfort when pressure is placed on a particular tooth can be a sign that something is wrong.

Inflammation near a painful tooth may be associated with an infection that requires treatment.

Damage can sometimes allow bacteria to reach the inner portion of a tooth.

Certain changes in the appearance of a tooth can occur following trauma or damage to the pulp.

It can be tempting to wait and see whether dental pain disappears on its own. But when discomfort is caused by infection or damage inside a tooth, delaying treatment can allow the problem to become more serious.

Our Laurel dental team can evaluate the affected tooth, determine what is causing your symptoms, and explain whether root canal therapy or another treatment is appropriate.

What Happens During Root Canal Treatment?

Dr. Vazir examines the affected tooth and surrounding area to determine the source and extent of the problem.

The treatment area is numbed so your comfort can remain a priority throughout the procedure.

The inflamed or infected tissue inside the tooth is carefully removed.

The inside of the tooth is cleaned, shaped, and sealed to help protect it from further infection.

Depending on how much tooth structure remains, a restoration such as a dental crown may be recommended to strengthen and protect the treated tooth.

Whenever Possible, We Want to Preserve Your Natural Tooth

When a tooth is significantly damaged or infected, patients sometimes assume it must be removed. That isn’t always the case.

Root canal therapy may allow us to preserve a tooth that would otherwise need to be extracted. Keeping your natural tooth can help maintain your bite, chewing function, and the alignment of surrounding teeth.

If the tooth cannot be predictably restored, Dr. Vazir can discuss extraction and potential tooth-replacement options with you.

Our goal is not simply to treat the immediate problem. We want to help you make the best decision for your long-term oral health.
