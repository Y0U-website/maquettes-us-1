Porcelain veneers

Veneers cover the front surface of the tooth, making them different from crowns, which surround more of the tooth structure.

Your smile is uniquely yours, and cosmetic dentistry should reflect that. Some patients want to address one noticeable tooth, while others are interested in creating a more consistent appearance across several visible teeth.

At Dental Care of Laurel Lakes, we start by listening to what you want to change. From there, Dr. Vazir can evaluate your teeth and oral health and discuss whether porcelain veneers or another cosmetic treatment is appropriate for your goals.

Veneers can cover certain stains or discoloration that may not respond the way you would like to traditional whitening.

A veneer can improve the visible appearance of a tooth that has been chipped or has an uneven edge.

In appropriate cases, veneers may be used to visually close small spaces between teeth.

Veneers can help create a more consistent appearance when certain teeth look smaller, differently shaped, or out of proportion with neighboring teeth.

Personalized From Consultation to Final Smile

1. Cosmetic Consultation: Your visit begins with a conversation about your smile, what you would like to improve, and what you hope to achieve. Your dentist will also evaluate your teeth and gums to determine whether veneers are an appropriate option.

2. Preparing Your Smile: Traditional porcelain veneers generally require removal of a small amount of enamel so the restoration can fit properly and blend naturally with the tooth.

3. Creating Your Veneers: Your veneers are customized for the teeth being treated, with attention given to their size, shape, and appearance.

4. Final Placement: Once ready, the veneers are bonded to the prepared teeth and evaluated for proper fit and appearance.

Veneers can be an excellent cosmetic option, but they are not appropriate for every smile. Existing concerns such as tooth decay or gum disease generally need to be treated first. Because enamel is removed during traditional veneer treatment, the process is considered irreversible. Patients who clench or grind their teeth may also need additional evaluation before choosing veneers.
