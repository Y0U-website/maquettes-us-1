The implant process: what to expect

One of the most common concerns patients have about implants is the timeline. This is not a one-appointment procedure, and it helps to know what you&#x27;re signing up for before you begin.

The process generally unfolds in several stages. First, any necessary preparatory work is done, such as extractions or bone grafting if needed. The implant post is then placed in the jaw during a minor surgical procedure. After placement, there&#x27;s a healing period that typically lasts three to six months while osseointegration occurs. Once the bone has fully integrated with the post, the abutment and crown are placed to complete the restoration.

The wait can feel long, but it&#x27;s what makes implants so durable. The bone-level foundation is exactly what separates them from other tooth replacement options.

Implants compared to other options

Patients considering tooth replacement often weigh implants against bridges or dentures. Each has its place, and the right choice depends on a patient&#x27;s specific situation, bone health, budget, and the number of missing teeth.

A traditional bridge can replace a missing tooth more quickly and without surgery, but it requires filing down the adjacent healthy teeth to serve as anchors. Over time, the bone beneath the bridge can also continue to shrink since there&#x27;s no root to stimulate it.

Dentures are a practical solution for patients missing many or all of their teeth, but they can shift, require adhesive, and don&#x27;t provide the same bite force as natural teeth or implants. Implant-supported dentures, which snap onto implant posts rather than resting on the gum, offer a significant improvement in stability for patients who need full-arch replacement.

Implants and your long-term oral health

Beyond the immediate benefit of replacing a missing tooth, implants do something no other restoration can: they preserve the jawbone. When a tooth root is gone, the bone in that area no longer receives the stimulation it needs and begins to resorb over time. This is what causes the sunken facial appearance that often accompanies long-term tooth loss. Because an implant functions like a root, it keeps that stimulation going and helps maintain bone volume for the long haul.
