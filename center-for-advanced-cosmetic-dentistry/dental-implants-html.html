What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that is placed into the jawbone to restore the root structure of a missing tooth. This is used to support a crown, bridge, or denture. Since the implant post is biocompatible, it integrates with the surrounding bone tissue to create a bond just as strong as that of the natural tooth roots. Thanks to this strong foundation, dental implant patients never need to worry about their new teeth shifting out of place.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

Since every step of the dental implant process can be handled right here at our practice, you will have the same caring, trustworthy team working on your smile from start to finish. We’ll take whatever steps are necessary to maximize your chances of a successful dental implant treatment, allowing you to confidently look forward to your new smile. Here’s what will happen when you choose dental implants as a tooth replacement solution.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

At your initial consultation, we’ll use the latest technology to take a detailed look at your mouth as well as the relevant facial structures. This is necessary for figuring out whether or not you are a candidate for the dental implant process. In some cases, you may be able to move on to the next step right away; however, there are situations where bone grafting and other types of preliminary work may need to be completed before your jaw is ready for dental implants.

Dental Implant Surgery

Unlike other forms of tooth replacement, dental implants are surgically inserted into your jaw. Your comfort matters to us, so we’ll make sure your mouth is numb before we move forward with the implant placement procedure.

To access your jawbone, we will need to make an incision in your gums. After that, we can insert the implant posts. Note that the ideal position and angle for your dental implants will be determined ahead of time. Once we’re done placing the posts, the gum tissue will be sutured closed.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

Dental implants can only succeed if they’re able to form a lasting bond with the bone in your jaw. As such, we will need to allow three to six months for osseointegration to take place. To ensure that the process is successful, you will need to avoid exposing your dental implants to an excessive amount of pressure.

After osseointegration, we can attach an abutment to each implant post. The purpose of an abutment is to keep your artificial teeth anchored to the implants. Bear in mind that another surgery will need to be performed in order to place the abutments.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s)

At this point, we can take impressions of your mouth so that we can design the final restoration that your dental implants will support. The type of prosthesis used can vary from patient to patient; some may only need a single crown while others might require a bridge or a denture. No matter what kind of restoration is needed, though, you can rest assured that it will be made out of high-quality, natural-looking materials.

The completed restoration will be attached to your implant posts during one final appointment. We’ll perform a quick last-minute check to ensure that there aren’t any problems with your bite; once we do, you can start enjoying all of the benefits that your new grin has to offer.

Benefits of Dental Implants

Dental implants are one of the most popular dental procedures with estimates for annual placements ranging from 500,000 to 5 million! They are outpacing many other treatments by a large margin, and it’s easy to see why! Dental implants offer numerous benefits that traditional dentures and bridges just can’t match. If you’re curious how dental implants from Center For Advanced Cosmetic Dentistry can help you improve your life, read more below!

Day-to-Day Benefits

Many patients want to know what life is like with dental implants. They wonder how they look, feel, and function on a daily basis. Here are some of the ways your implants will immediately go to work for you:

  • Boosted Confidence: As soon as your smile is restored, you’ll feel a difference in the frequency of your smiles. Dental implants look just like natural teeth, creating a seamless and aesthetically pleasing grin you can be proud of.
  • Eat Your Favorite Foods: Traditional dentures limit your diet because of their lack of stability. However, dental implants are very sturdy and restore up to 70 percent of your biting and chewing capacity. This lets you eat all your favorite foods without worry!
  • Prevent Speech Impediments: Missing teeth can change the air pressure in your mouth and your tongue placement when you pronounce certain words. This often leads to speech impediments like slurring or lisps. By keeping your smile intact, dental implants prevent these speech issues so you can communicate with confidence.

Health Benefits

Beyond day-to-day use, there are also many ways that dental implants can improve your mental, physical, and oral health, including:

  • Improved Digestion: Chewing, the first step in the digestive process, is more difficult after tooth loss. Without proper processing in your mouth, large chunks of food can enter your digestive system. This can lead to issues like indigestion and acid reflux. Dental implants make chewing easier, preventing these issues.
  • Easier Oral Hygiene: Crooked teeth and large gaps are an invitation for bacterial plaque and tartar buildup. Dental implants help keep your mouth tidy and easier to clean.
  • Preserve Jawbone Health: After tooth loss, your jawbone begins the process of resorption, deteriorating over time. Dental implants are the only tooth replacement option that prevents this, helping you keep your smile intact.

Long-Term Benefits

Dental implants are one of the most successful treatments in dentistry, boasting a success rate of over 95 percent. This means you can expect to enjoy many long-term benefits, such as:

  • Keeping Your Youthful Appearance: As your jawbone deteriorates over time from aging, your jawline and facial features will begin to collapse. This leads to a shrunken and hollow look that adds many years to the appearance of your face. Dental implants stop this process, helping you stay looking youthful longer.
  • Preserving Tooth Alignment: After losing a tooth, the adjacent ones begin shifting to try to fill the gap. This leads to bite problems and temporomandibular joint (TMJ) pain. A dental implant will fill the gap, keeping your teeth in alignment.
  • Saving Money: Dental implants can last for decades with proper care and maintenance. Over time, this will save you the money you might have otherwise spent on replacements for dentures and dental bridges that more easily wear out.

Who Dental Implants Can Help

Dental implants can be used to help patients missing any number of teeth. They just need to have three basic requirements: good dental health, stable overall health, and enough jawbone to support the new roots. Once all of these conditions are met (our team can help you with any that are lacking), we can go over your restorative options.

Missing One Tooth

A dental implant root can be placed into the jawbone and connected to a porcelain crown using something called an abutment. Unlike with a traditional bridge, single implants don’t require the adjacent teeth to be altered at all, better preserving someone’s natural dental structure.

Missing Multiple Teeth

For patients missing teeth throughout their mouth, implants offer plenty of options. Crowns can be placed anywhere, and a small number of roots can even be used to secure a partial denture. Just two implant roots can be used to give a patient a dental bridge that replaces up to three teeth in a row.

Missing All Teeth

It only takes four to six carefully placed implant roots to give a patient a rock-solid full denture. Compared to a traditional removable prosthetic, implant dentures are smaller, have a better hold, and can even help a patient recover much more of their natural bite strength.

The most important thing to know about the cost of dental implants is that there is no fixed price. The number of implants needed, the type of restoration used, and many other factors can make the price vary from person to person. While implants do tend to be more expensive than other options, they are also an investment that will last for decades , and a patient won’t have to periodically replace their restorations like with traditional bridges and dentures.

Dental Implants Post-Op Instructions

Once you’ve had your dental implants placed, you’ll need to recover for a while. You won’t be able to use your new teeth until your mouth is fully healed and rested. With that said, please don’t worry; the Center for Advanced Cosmetic Dentistry can help with your recovery! Listed below are the suggested dental implant post-op instructions from our Hammonton team. To review them in greater detail, just keep reading or call us soon!

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

Right after surgery, leave the forming blood clot at your treatment site alone; touching it will delay your recovery. You should instead do the following:

  • For at least a few days, keep your fingers and tongue away from your new implant.
  • Don’t use tobacco during the first 24 hours after surgery.
  • During recovery, avoid using drinking straws; their suction can dislodge blood clots.

Common Side Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

You’ll likely experience some mild side effects while you recover. The most common ones include the following:

Side effects like those above are temporary; they fade within a few days. However, please tell us if they seem to persist for you.

  • Oral Discomfort Your treatment site may ache as the anesthetic from your surgery wears off. Should that happen, use store-bought (or doctor-prescribed) pain relievers.
  • Facial or Gum Swelling In the first 72 hours after surgery, some patients can experience swelling in their face and gums. Apply a cold compress or ice pack if that happens to you.
  • Intermittent Bleeding Remember to apply gauze and gentle pressure if your implant site experiences bleeding.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

Eating tough meals too soon could dislodge your blood clot, so only have soft foods for the first few days after surgery. Doing otherwise would delay your recovery and harm your oral health.

As for specific soft foods, good ones to consider are:

You’re free to resume a normal diet once you feel up to it. Still, please limit your intake of crunchy and tough foods going forward. You’d also do well to avoid chewing directly on the implant site.

  • Yogurt
  • Scrambled Eggs
  • Mashed Potatoes
  • (Non-hot) Soup
  • Smoothies
  • Soft Cheese
  • Pasta
  • Pudding
  • Sweet Potatoes
  • Ice Cream

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

Even as you recover, you’ll still need to clean your mouth every day. Just make sure to use these guidelines when doing so:

  • Be careful of the surgical site when brushing your teeth twice daily.
  • At least twice or thrice daily, rinse your mouth with salt water twice. You could also substitute a prescription mouthwash for the salt water.
  • Avoid mouthwashes with a high level of alcohol (i.e., Scope, Listerine, etc.).

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

Once a dental crown, bridge, or denture is attached to your implant, your treatment’s hardest part is done. You should expect only minor gum sensitivity from that point onward. In other words, you won’t experience harsh swelling, bleeding, or similar symptoms during the rest of your recovery. You can instead enjoy your restored smile after the attachment is placed.

Maintaining & Caring For Your Dental Implants

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

One of the main reasons dental implants fail is peri-implantitis or an infection in the gum tissue surrounding your replacement teeth. This typically happens because of bacteria and plaque buildup around your smile due to poor oral hygiene. For this reason, you’ll want to stick to a good tooth cleaning routine every day. Simply by brushing twice a day, flossing daily, and rinsing frequently with ADA-approved mouthwash, you can ensure your dental implants remain healthy for many years to come.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Since you’ll regain around 80 % or more of your biting power with your new teeth, you won’t have to worry about holding back on your favorite foods. With the ability to munch on most types of meals, you’ll also be able to maintain a healthier diet, which will be essential for your dental implants. Be sure to stick to more nutritional options like fresh fruits and veggies, lean proteins, whole grains, and lean proteins to promote stronger gums and bone tissue. Avoid overindulging in sugary, acidic, or starchy treats that can increase the risk of developing harmful bacteria.

Break Bad Habits

Part of preserving your dental implants and natural teeth is to refrain from bad oral habits. These may include biting your fingernails, chewing or smoking tobacco products, and using your teeth as tools all of which can cost you an emergency trip to your dentist. Don’t hesitate to consult our dental team if you need help quitting any of these habits, as we’ll be more than happy to offer the support, guidance, and resources necessary to make this process easier for you.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Do you usually grind or clench your teeth while you sleep? If so, then you might consider talking to us about nightguards for bruxism. They will allow you to rest easily as well as prevent wearing down your dental implants. If you tend to play contact sports where you can potentially take a blow to the face, then let us know. We’ll be able to recommend the best products to preserve your specific smile!

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

We highly suggest that you schedule routine appointments with our office for checkups and cleanings at least twice a year. This allows us to keep an eye on the condition of your dental implants and monitor your oral health for any developing issues. With early treatment, you’ll be able to avoid more serious issues from becoming bigger problems, such as gum disease, cavities, and infections.
