The Ideal Age for a First Orthodontic Visit

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends scheduling your child’s first orthodontic evaluation by the age of seven . At this age, we can identify emerging alignment or bite issues and develop a proactive treatment plan if needed.

Signs your child may benefit from an early orthodontic examination include:

  • Early or late loss of baby teeth
  • Difficulty chewing or biting
  • Crowded or misaligned teeth
  • Breathing through the mouth
  • Thumb-sucking beyond age four

By addressing these concerns early, we help set the foundation for better sleep, improved energy, proper facial balance, and long-term wellness.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best age to start early orthodontic treatment?

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends children have their first orthodontic evaluation by age seven. At this age, we can detect early signs of alignment or bite issues, even if treatment isn’t immediately required.

What are the benefits of early orthodontic intervention?

Early orthodontics allows us to guide jaw development, create space for adult teeth, correct bite issues, and address harmful habits. This proactive approach often reduces the need for more invasive treatments later and can improve your child’s self-esteem.

How do I know if my child needs early orthodontic care?

Look for signs such as crowded or crooked teeth, difficulty chewing, early or late loss of baby teeth, thumb sucking, or mouth breathing. If you notice any of these, schedule an evaluation with our team.

What should we expect during the first visit?

During your child’s first appointment, we’ll review their dental and medical history, perform a gentle exam, and may take digital X-rays or photos if needed. We’ll discuss our findings and recommend a customized treatment plan or monitoring schedule.

Does early treatment mean my child will avoid braces later?

Early orthodontic care can reduce the length and complexity of future treatments, but some children may still need braces as teens. The goal is to set up a healthy foundation for future smile success.
