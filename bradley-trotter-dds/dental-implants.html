Why Dental Implants Are the Gold Standard for Tooth Replacement

A missing tooth does more than affect your smile. It can lead to bone loss in your jaw, shifting of nearby teeth, and changes to your bite. Dental implants are the only tooth replacement option that stimulates the jawbone like a natural root, preserving your facial structure and preventing future dental problems.

What Happens If You Delay Replacing a Missing Tooth

Waiting too long to replace a missing tooth allows the jawbone to shrink where the root once was. Neighboring teeth may tilt into the gap, leading to bite problems and making future restoration more complex. The bone loss can also limit your implant options without grafting. Addressing the gap sooner rather than later gives you the best chance for a smooth, long-lasting result.

Are You a Candidate for a Dental Implant?

Most adults with good general health and adequate bone density can receive a dental implant. During your consultation, we take 3D CT scans to evaluate your jawbone and plan the placement precisely. Even if bone loss has occurred, we can often rebuild the area first.

Bone Grafting Before Implants — When It s Needed

If the jawbone where the implant goes is too thin or soft, a bone graft may be recommended. This procedure adds volume to the bone and creates a solid foundation for your implant. We perform bone grafting in our office, often during the same visit as other preparatory work. Learn more about bone grafting and how it supports successful implant outcomes.

What to Expect: The Dental Implant Process Step by Step

Understanding the stages of implant treatment helps you feel prepared and confident.

Initial Consultation and 3D Imaging

Your first visit includes a thorough exam with our in-house 3D CT scanner. This imaging gives us a detailed view of your jawbone, nerves, and sinuses, allowing precise implant planning. We also discuss your goals, review your health history, and create a personalized treatment plan.

Implant Placement and Healing

On the day of surgery, we use local anesthesia and offer sedation options to keep you comfortable. Your surgeon makes a small incision in the gum, places the titanium implant into the jawbone, and closes the site. Over the next several months, the implant fuses with your bone in a process called osseointegration.

Final Restoration

Once healing is complete, we attach an abutment (a connector piece) and a custom-made crown that matches your natural teeth. The result is a permanent tooth that looks, feels, and functions like the real thing.

Can I get a dental implant the same day a tooth is removed?

In some cases, yes. If the extraction site has enough healthy bone and no infection, we may place the implant immediately. Your surgeon will determine the best timing for you.

Do dental implants hurt?

Implant surgery is performed with local anesthesia and sedation, so you feel no pain during the procedure. Afterward, discomfort is usually mild and controlled with over-the-counter pain relievers. Most patients return to normal activities within a day or two.
