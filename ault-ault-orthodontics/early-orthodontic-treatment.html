Common Early Orthodontic Issues

Many families seek early orthodontic treatment to address problems that appear while both baby and permanent teeth are present. Identifying these concerns early helps guide growth and simplifies future care. Some of the most common issues include:

Addressing these concerns through orthodontic treatment for children supports proper function, reduces long-term complications, and helps build a strong foundation for a healthy smile.

  • Crowding – Not enough space in the jaw causes teeth to overlap or twist as they erupt.
  • Spacing – Large gaps between teeth that may interfere with alignment once permanent teeth emerge.
  • Crossbite – Upper teeth sit inside the lower teeth, which can affect jaw development and bite function.
  • Overbite – Upper front teeth overlap too far over the lower front teeth, leading to uneven wear or discomfort.
  • Underbite – Lower teeth extend past the upper teeth, often linked to differences in jaw growth.
  • Open Bite – Front teeth do not touch when the back teeth are closed, sometimes caused by thumb sucking or tongue habits.
  • Jaw Discrepancies – Uneven growth of the upper or lower jaw can affect both bite and facial balance.
  • Early Loss of Baby Teeth – Losing baby teeth too soon may cause surrounding teeth to shift, reducing space for permanent teeth.

What Is Phase 1 Orthodontic Treatment for Children

What Is Phase 1 Orthodontic Treatment and How It Works

Many parents ask, what is Phase 1 orthodontic treatment, and the answer is simpler than it sounds. Phase 1 orthodontic treatment is an early orthodontic approach used while a child still has a mix of baby teeth and permanent teeth. Rather than focusing on straightening every tooth, this stage addresses jaw growth, spacing, and bite alignment.

Phase 1 orthodontic treatment may include expanders, limited braces, or habit appliances depending on your child’s needs. The goal is to guide development so permanent teeth erupt into better positions. This often prevents crowding, crossbites, or jaw imbalances that are harder to correct later.

Not every child needs Phase 1 treatment. Some benefit most from observation until the right moment. Dr Jeff can evaluate growth patterns and help families decide what timing makes sense.

Not sure if Phase 1 care is right for your child? An orthodontic evaluation can give you clarity and confidence.

Best Age for First Orthodontic Visit for Children

Best Age for First Orthodontic Visit and Why Timing Matters

The best age for first orthodontic visit is typically around age seven. At this stage, enough permanent teeth have emerged for an orthodontist to identify developing concerns. This visit doesn’t always lead to immediate treatment, but it provides valuable insight into your child’s growth.

Signs a Child Needs Orthodontic Care Early

Common Signs a Child Needs Orthodontic Care

Recognizing the signs a child needs orthodontic care can help parents act before issues worsen. Some signs are easy to spot, while others require a trained eye.

Common indicators include crowded or overlapping teeth, early or late loss of baby teeth, difficulty chewing, mouth breathing, thumb-sucking beyond early childhood, or jaws that shift when opening and closing. Bite issues such as overbites, underbites, or crossbites are also strong signals.

Even if your child isn’t experiencing discomfort, these signs may point to underlying growth concerns that benefit from early orthodontic care in San Marcos.
