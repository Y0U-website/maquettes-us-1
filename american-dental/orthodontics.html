Tips To Maintain Oral Hygiene During Orthodontic Treatment

Braces and aligners are just some of the most common forms of orthodontic treatment. If you’ve received any of these treatments from your orthodontist, it’s important to understand how to maintain your oral hygiene to prevent infection. Here are some helpful tips that will show you how to keep your oral hygiene as clean as possible both during, and after you’ve received orthodontic treatment near Chicago, IL.

Placing braces on your teeth causes little discomfort, but the procedure can take one or two hours to complete. Your teeth will be cleaned and dried before the procedure begins. An orthodontist in Chicago, IL attaches brackets to your teeth using bonding glue. Archwire is placed in the brackets that runs across your teeth. The final step of the procedure is securing the wire in place using elastics.

Braces require regular adjustments to ensure treatment is progressing normally. An orthodontist in Chicago, IL removes the wires and elastics. Patients brush and floss their teeth before the new wires and elastics are placed on the teeth. Adjustment might cause some pain, but the discomfort only lasts a few days.

The treatment time for braces in Chicago, IL can vary between one to three years depending on the condition of your teeth. Patients wear a retainer after the braces are removed to prevent the teeth from shifting. An orthodontist in Chicago, IL might suggest wearing the retainer all of the time for the first six months. Patients will eventually only need to wear a retainer when they are sleeping.

  • Even after your treatment, it’s important to get regular dental exams and cleanings. This includes getting X-rays and a professional cleaning of the teeth and gums, which is the best way to keep your oral health in top condition.
  • Always follow the instructions given by your orthodontist after your treatment. They’ll give you the tools and information you need to maintain good oral hygiene long after the treatment is complete.
  • If you have braces or clear aligners, use a water flosser to remove excess food particles, bacteria, and plaque. These powerful flossers get into every small crevice of the mouth to keep your braces, aligners, and teeth nice and clean.
  • Use an interproximal brush small enough to fit between your braces and teeth if you don’t have a water flosser. These special brushes help to remove stuck food and work as a great supplement to traditional dental floss.
  • Remove your clear aligners whenever you eat and drink and whenever you brush and floss your teeth. Make sure you also clean the aligners twice each day for the best oral hygiene possible.
