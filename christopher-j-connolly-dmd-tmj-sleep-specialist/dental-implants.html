What Is the Process for Dental Implants?

Dental implant procedures are completed in two phases: the initial implantation of each implant and their final restoration, or when the prosthetic tooth/teeth are affixed on top. At Connolly Family Dentistry, our team of doctors provides complete dental implant care from start to finish — so you will not have to worry about getting a referral to an outside office for the initial implantation procedure.

It all starts with a consultation. During this initial appointment, our team will evaluate the health of your jawbone and determine whether or not you are a good candidate for this comprehensive replacement. You may need to have an initial bone grafting procedure performed first. This surgery adds volume to the weak or depleted jawbone to create a perfect environment for dental implants.

Once your jaw is ready, the procedure can begin. Your dentist uses x-rays and models of your surrounding teeth and jaw to precisely place each implant exactly where it needs to go. After implantation, recovery begins. It can take anywhere from four to eight months for the implants to fully heal together with the jawbone.

After this process is complete, it’s time for the restoration. You return to our office once again, and each dental implant is exposed. A small connecting abutment is placed on top and your gums are given one more brief healing period. When that is complete about two weeks later, your prosthetic tooth/teeth can be placed — and you are ready to smile with your new dental implants!

Dental Implant Restoration Options

Whether you have lost a single tooth or you are missing an entire row of teeth in the upper or lower jaw, it doesn’t matter — dental implants can provide a highly effective replacement for any number of teeth.

The one that is right for you depends on how many teeth are missing, where in the mouth these gaps are located, as well as your budget and personal preference.

  • A single dental crown
  • A fixed or removable bridge for multiple missing teeth when some healthy teeth remain
  • A complete denture, fixed or removable, in the upper or lower jaw for when a full arch of teeth has been lost
