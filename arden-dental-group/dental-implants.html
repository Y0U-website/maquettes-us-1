Adequate jawbone density ensures implant stability. A bone graft is a way of adding new bone to your jawbone before an implant procedure.

Do you have existing medical conditions?

Good systemic health fosters predictable healing. Patients with most chronic diseases, such as high blood pressure and diabetes, are usually successful candidates for dental implant treatment.

Smoking lowers the success rate of dental implants.

Active periodontal disease must be treated first to protect the bone. However, implants placed in patents who have lost teeth to periodontal disease have a very high success rate over time.

Several variables influence what you’ll ultimately pay for dental implants, and a precise quote is possible only after a one-on-one consultation with one of our trusted doctors. However, consider the following:

  • Clear, Stable Speech: Because implants lock firmly into the jaw, they stay put while you talk which means no clicking plates or mid-sentence slips so words sound crisp and natural.
  • All-Day Comfort: The titanium post fuses with jawbone, eliminating the sore spots and shifting that removable dentures can sometimes cause, so you forget you’re even wearing a replacement tooth.
  • Long-Term Resilience : With daily brushing, flossing, and routine checkups, an implant can serve you for decades or permanently, making it one of the most durable restoration choices available.
  • Tooth-Friendly Solution: Unlike traditional bridges that require trimming neighboring teeth, an implant stands on its own root, keeping the surrounding enamel and gum tissue untouched.
  • Eat Without Issue: Implants deliver near-natural bite strength, letting you crunch apples, enjoy steaks, and savor your favorite chewy or crunchy foods without worry.
  • Consultation We review goals, finances, and sedation options during your consultation.
  • Site Preparation Tooth extraction may be needed if a failing tooth must be removed.
  • Implant Placement A titanium post is placed into the jawbone, acting as a root for the new tooth. The gum tissue is then sutured into place.
  • Healing Phase Bone fuses to titanium during a period called osseointegration which typically lasts several months.
  • We monitor via short progress checks to avoid lengthy chair time.
  • Custom Restoration We uncover the implant, scan digitally, and ensure your restoration locks in place. This occurs after the implant or implants have fully integrated and the restoration will match the shape, design and color of your natural teeth.
  • Smile Preview & Final Fit Shade and contour tweaked; bite polished; aftercare tips provided.
  • Maintain Oral Health Morning and night, ensure you’re brushing your teeth and flossing regularly as you would your natural teeth. Proper oral hygiene is essential for a successful healing process.
  • Hydrate & Nourish Sip water often and load up on vitamin-rich smoothies or broth. A soft diet is usually recommended during the early stages of healing to avoid putting unnecessary pressure and stress on the surgical site.
  • Sleep Smart Prop up on two pillows the first three nights to reduce swelling.
  • Protect the Clot Avoid suction (straws, vaping) and spitting. Think of the clot as a natural Band-Aid you don’t want to peel off.
  • Ice the Right Way Cold compress for 20 minutes, then give your skin 20 minutes off; continuous icing can damage tissue.
  • Move, but Don’t Strain Gentle walking improves circulation, yet heavy lifting can spike blood pressure and restart bleeding.
  • Watch for Red Flags Call us if you experience throbbing pain that worsens after Day 3, persistent bleeding, foul taste, or fever. Early attention keeps small issues small.
  • Plan For Follow- Up Visits Once the implant has fused with your jawbone and the gums have healed, we’ll add a custom crown, bridge, or denture on top. It’s shaped, shaded, and sized to blend with your natural teeth, giving you a complete, comfortable smile.
  • Practice Location: Each dental office sets its fees based on local operating expenses, staffing, and supply costs.
  • Surgical Factors: Pricing changes with the complexity of the procedure: extractions, imaging, and the level of sedation all add to the total.
  • Restorative Phase: The final crown (or bridge/denture) is priced per tooth and may be preceded by a temporary restoration while the implant heals.
  • Number of Teeth: Replacing several teeth in one visit extends chair time and increases the quantity of implants and materials which may raise the overall fee.
  • Insurance Variables: Only certain dental insurance plans contribute to implant costs. Our dental savings plan covers implants; ensure you ask our team about this plan!
