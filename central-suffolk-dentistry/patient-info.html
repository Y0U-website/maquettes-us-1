Patient Instructions

Please follow the care instructions provided by our office and maintain good oral hygiene at home. You’ll find post-treatment guidelines for various services—just click the one that applies to you. If you experience severe pain or dental issues, contact us right away. For any life-threatening emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest ER.

Fillings

  • Wait for numbness to fade: If you received anesthesia, avoid eating until full sensation returns to prevent accidentally biting your cheeks, lips, or tongue.
  • Resume chewing normally: Composite fillings harden immediately, so once numbness is gone, you may chew as usual.
  • Normal sensitivity: Mild sensitivity to hot, cold, or pressure is common and should improve within days to weeks.
  • Deep fillings: If the cavity was deep, sensitivity may last longer. If symptoms worsen, contact us.
  • Irritated gums: The gum area near the filling may feel sore for a few days. Warm salt-water rinses and OTC pain relievers help.
  • Jaw fatigue: After multiple fillings, your jaw may feel tired or sore from keeping your mouth open. This typically improves within 2–5 days.
  • New surface feel: Your filling may feel unusual at first, but you’ll adapt within a few days.
  • Uneven bite: If your bite feels high or uncomfortable once numbness wears off, call us for a quick adjustment.
  • Avoid smoking while numb: Wait until full sensation returns to avoid accidental burns.
  • Delay smoking: Smoking can affect healing. Avoid until you’re comfortable and healed.
  • Good oral hygiene: Brush and floss daily to prevent new decay around the filling and increase its longevity.

Crowns & Bridges

  • Purpose: Temporaries protect the tooth and maintain space for the permanent restoration.
  • Oral hygiene: Brush normally and floss carefully—slide floss out from the side to avoid dislodging the temporary.
  • If temporary falls off: Contact us immediately. You may temporarily place it with toothpaste or denture adhesive until you can come in.
  • Prevent decay: The porcelain cannot decay, but the tooth underneath can. Clean well around the gumline.
  • Cleaning under bridges: Use a floss threader to clean beneath the bridge (under the “false tooth”). We’ll demonstrate if needed.
  • Chewing habits: Avoid sticky or hard foods that may disturb temporaries.
  • Mild sensitivity: A small amount of pressure or temperature sensitivity is normal for the first few days.
  • Bite concerns: If chewing feels uneven or uncomfortable, contact us for an adjustment.
  • Persistent sensitivity: If symptoms don’t improve after a few weeks, let us know.
  • Daily care: Brush and floss thoroughly around the restoration.
  • Decay prevention: Keep the margin (edge) clean to avoid future issues.

Root Canals

  • Discomfort is normal: Mild to moderate soreness or pressure may last for a few days.
  • Medication: Take prescribed medications as directed.
  • Temporary filling: Avoid chewing on the treated tooth until numbness wears off; be gentle until the crown/filling is placed.
  • Chewing: Use the opposite side when possible.
  • Oral hygiene: Continue brushing and flossing normally.
  • Final restoration: A permanent crown or filling must be placed on time to protect the tooth.
  • Normal sensations: The tooth may feel “different” for a while, even after healing.
  • Pain persists or worsens for more than a few days.
  • Swelling appears inside or outside the mouth.
  • Your bite feels high or uneven.
  • Your temporary crown/filling comes out.
  • Crown is essential: Most treated teeth need a full-coverage crown for long-term success.
  • Continue routine hygiene and checkups.
  • Monitoring: We’ll track healing at future appointments.

Apicoectomy (Root Canal Surgery)

  • Expect mild swelling and soreness: This is normal for a few days. Use an ice pack (10 minutes on, 10 minutes off) for the first 24 hours.
  • Bleeding: Slight oozing is normal. Bite gently on gauze if needed.
  • Medication: Take all prescribed antibiotics and pain relievers exactly as directed.
  • Do not rinse vigorously for 24 hours.
  • After 24 hours: Rinse gently with warm salt water 2–3 times a day to promote healing.
  • Brushing: Brush normally but avoid the surgical area for a few days.
  • Soft foods only for the first 24 hours.
  • Avoid hard, crunchy, or spicy foods until the area feels better.
  • No smoking or alcohol for 72 hours — these delay healing.
  • Limit physical activity for the first day.

Scaling and Root Planing

  • Mild tenderness: Gums may feel sore for a few days.
  • Temperature sensitivity: Hot and cold sensitivity is common and temporary.
  • Bleeding: Light bleeding during brushing is normal at first.
  • Brush gently but thoroughly around the treated areas.
  • Floss daily: Even if gums are tender, this helps healing.
  • Warm salt rinses (2–3 times daily) help reduce soreness.
  • Soft foods for the first 24 hours.
  • Avoid spicy, acidic, or crunchy foods temporarily.
  • Desensitizing toothpaste can help with sensitivity.
  • Gum maintenance visits are important to keep gum disease controlled.
  • Continue regular cleanings every 3–4 months as recommended.

Tooth Extraction

  • Bite on gauze for 30–45 minutes to control bleeding.
  • Do not spit, rinse, or use a straw for the first 24 hours — this can cause dry socket.
  • Some bleeding is normal: A little oozing for 24 hours is expected.
  • Ice packs for the first day help reduce swelling.
  • Take medications as prescribed.
  • Swelling may peak at 48 hours , then improve.
  • Soft foods only: Yogurt, eggs, mashed potatoes, soup.
  • Avoid smoking for at least 72 hours — major risk of dry socket.
  • Limit exercise for 24–48 hours.
  • Do not brush the extraction site for the first day.
  • After 24 hours: Rinse gently with warm salt water.
  • Brush other teeth normally throughout.

Gum Surgery

  • Expect swelling and mild discomfort: Use ice packs the first day.
  • Bleeding: Light bleeding is normal. Gently bite gauze if needed.
  • Medication: Take antibiotics and pain relievers as instructed.
  • Soft diet for several days.
  • Avoid hard, crunchy, sticky foods that may disturb the area.
  • No smoking or alcohol for at least 72 hours.
  • Avoid exercise for 24–48 hours.
  • Do not brush or floss the surgical site until instructed.
  • Use prescribed mouth rinse if provided.
  • Brush other teeth normally.
  • A protective dressing may be placed—don’t disturb it.
  • If it falls off early , call us for instructions.
  • Sutures may dissolve or require removal depending on type.

Braces Placement

  • Discomfort Management: Mild soreness is normal. You may take over-the-counter pain relievers such as ibuprofen or acetaminophen as needed.
  • Eat Soft Foods: Stick to soups, pasta, mashed potatoes, yogurt, eggs, and smoothies for the first 2–3 days.
  • Avoid Hard & Sticky Foods: No chewing gum, candy, popcorn, nuts, ice, or anything that can break brackets.
  • Wax Application: If brackets or wires irritate your cheeks, apply orthodontic wax to ease discomfort.
  • Oral Hygiene: Brush gently but thoroughly at least twice a day.
  • Use an interdental brush or floss threader to clean around wires.
  • Rinse with warm salt water to soothe gum irritation.
  • Loose Brackets or Wires: If something breaks or pokes, call our office for guidance. Do not try to fix it yourself.

Implant Surgery

  • Bite firmly on the gauze to control bleeding. Replace it every 30–45 minutes as needed.
  • Minor bleeding or oozing is normal for up to 24 hours.
  • Apply an ice pack on the cheek (20 minutes on, 20 minutes off) for the first 6–8 hours to reduce swelling.
  • Do NOT rinse your mouth for the first 24 hours.
  • After 24 hours, rinse gently with warm salt water 3–4 times a day.
  • Brush your teeth carefully, avoiding the surgical site for the first few days.
  • Stick to soft foods for 48–72 hours: yogurt, mashed potatoes, smoothies, eggs, pasta.
