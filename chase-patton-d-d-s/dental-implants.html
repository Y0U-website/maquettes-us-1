Dental implants are considered the gold standard for tooth replacement. By replacing the missing tooth root and fusing with the jawbone, dental implants provide a stable foundation for dental crowns to fully restore functionality to the smile.

Dental implants explained

At the office of Chase Patton D.D.S. in Columbia, MO , we are here to help you navigate through your tooth replacement options after tooth loss to determine whether dental implants are right for you. There are several factors that go into determining the best type of tooth replacement for each patient, including the number of teeth that have been lost, their current oral health and quality of bone, budget, and overall treatment preferences.

Restoring the Implant After healing

After your dental implant is placed by a local oral surgeon, your mouth will go through a healing process known as osseointegration. During this time, the bone fuses around the dental implant to allow the final restoration to be fully functional. This is what makes dental implants so much more effective than alternative tooth replacement options. Dental implants truly mimic the strength and functionality of your natural teeth.

After osseointegration has been completed, you will return to the office of Chase Patton D.D.S. to be fitted for a dental crown restoration that is placed on top of the implant.

The dental crown process after implants

The first step in the restoration process is the removal of the healing abutment which was placed on top of the dental implant during the initial procedure. This can be done using local anesthetic. Once the abutment has been removed, an impression post is placed to support the fibers that were surrounding the abutment. An x-ray is taken to ensure that there is no tissue caught between the impression post and the implant. Once this is confirmed, standard dental crown impressions can be taken. Afterward, the impression post is removed, and the healing abutment is put back in place.

The impressions are then sent off to a dental laboratory along with information on the size and type of dental implant. Once the final dental crown is ready, you will return to the office where we will remove the healing abutment and place the final abutment. Dr. Patton will then cement the dental crown in place to fully restore your smile.

Dental Implants FAQs

Why are dental implants often considered the gold standard for tooth replacement?

Dental implants are distinctive as they replicate not only the visible part of a tooth but also the root structure hidden beneath the gums. This allows them to function more like natural teeth compared to many other replacement options. The implant integrates with the jawbone, creating a stable foundation that supports long-term function, appearance, and comfort while helping preserve the health of the surrounding bone.

Can dental implants replace more than one missing tooth?

Yes. Dental implants are highly versatile and can be used in several ways. A single implant can support one crown, multiple implants can support a bridge, and a series of implants can even help secure full or partial dentures. The number of implants recommended depends on the number of missing teeth, bone quality, and the overall treatment plan developed for the patient.

What happens if I have been missing a tooth for many years?

Long-term tooth loss can sometimes lead to bone loss in the jaw because the bone no longer receives stimulation from a natural tooth root. However, this does not automatically eliminate dental implant eligibility. Many patients can still become candidates through additional procedures that help improve bone support. A comprehensive examination and imaging study can help determine the available treatment options.

How do dental implants help protect surrounding teeth?

Unlike traditional bridges, dental implants do not require neighboring healthy teeth to be altered for support. This allows adjacent teeth to remain intact while still replacing the missing tooth. In addition, implants help maintain spacing within the dental arch, reducing the risk of neighboring teeth shifting into the empty area and affecting bite alignment.

Are dental implants difficult to care for?

Dental implants are designed to be cared for much like natural teeth. Daily brushing, flossing, and regular professional cleanings remain important for maintaining healthy gums and protecting the long-term success of the implant. Patients who practice consistent oral hygiene and attend routine dental visits often enjoy excellent outcomes with their implant restorations.

Can dental implants improve confidence and quality of life?

Many patients report significant improvements in daily life after replacing missing teeth with dental implants. Activities such as eating, speaking, and smiling often feel more comfortable and natural. Because implants are securely anchored, patients generally do not have to worry about movement or instability that can sometimes occur with removable appliances. This added confidence can positively affect both social and professional interactions.

How long can dental implants last?

Dental implants are known for their durability and long-term success. While the crown attached to the implant may eventually require maintenance or replacement due to normal wear, the implant itself can often remain functional for many years when properly cared for. Factors such as oral hygiene, gum health, and regular dental evaluations all contribute to long-term implant success.
