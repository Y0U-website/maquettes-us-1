The Importance of teeth cleanings

Who doesn’t love the feeling of a freshly cleaned and polished set of teeth? Besides making your smile look and feel good, these dental cleanings serve an important purpose. By removing difficult to clean plaque from teeth, these cleanings help prevent tooth decay and cavities. Professional cleanings are also a great teeth stain remover, leaving your pearly whites sparkling.

For individuals who show early signs of gum disease, we offer periodontal cleanings or deep teeth cleanings. These types of cleanings also remove bacteria buildup below the gum line to help restore oral health.

At-home oral hygiene and prevention

Keeping your mouth healthy involves more than visiting the dentist every six months for a check-up and cleaning. It’s important for you to develop and maintain a good at-home oral hygiene routine in order to prevent plaque buildup and disease. We are happy to provide you with resources and recommend custom protocols to help you maintain optimal oral health. As a rule, you should be brushing twice a day and flossing once a day.

Proper nutrition and oral health

Your diet can have a major impact on your teeth and gums. Sugary or acidic foods and drinks can erode the enamel and increase the risk for decay. On the other hand, there are also foods that will enhance your oral health. We’re happy to work with you on a proper nutrition plan as it pertains to your oral health and overall wellness.

Teeth Cleaning and Prevention FAQs

How can professional teeth cleanings help beyond what I do at home?

Despite thorough brushing and flossing, completely eliminating plaque and tartar from specific areas of the mouth can be challenging. Professional cleanings target buildup in hard-to-reach places that can contribute to tooth decay, gum disease, and bad breath. Regular cleanings also allow dental professionals to monitor changes in your oral health and identify potential concerns before they become more serious.

What happens if I skip routine dental cleanings for several years?

When professional cleanings are postponed for extended periods, plaque can harden into tartar, which cannot be removed with brushing alone. Over time, this buildup may increase the risk of cavities, gum inflammation, gum disease, and even tooth loss. Many patients are surprised to learn that oral health problems can develop without causing immediate pain, making preventive visits especially important.

Are dental cleanings different for patients with gum disease?

Yes. Patients with signs of gum disease may require a more extensive cleaning than is provided during a standard preventive appointment. Deep cleaning procedures, often called scaling and root planing, focus on removing bacteria and buildup from below the gumline. These treatments help reduce inflammation, support gum health, and create an environment where the gums can heal more effectively.

Can professional cleanings help improve bad breath?

Persistent bad breath is often linked to bacteria, plaque accumulation, food particles, or gum problems. A professional cleaning can remove many of the sources contributing to unpleasant odors while also identifying any underlying dental conditions that may require treatment. Combined with good home care habits, routine cleanings can play an important role in maintaining fresher breath.

How does nutrition affect my oral health?

The foods and beverages you consume can significantly impact the health of your teeth and gums. Frequent consumption of sugary snacks, soft drinks, and acidic beverages may contribute to enamel erosion and tooth decay. In contrast, foods rich in vitamins, minerals, and calcium can support stronger teeth and healthier gums. Drinking plenty of water also helps rinse away food particles and bacteria throughout the day.

What are some signs that my oral hygiene routine may need improvement?

Common indicators include bleeding gums during brushing or flossing, persistent bad breath, frequent plaque buildup, tooth sensitivity, or recurring cavities. If you notice any of these symptoms, it may be time to review your oral hygiene habits with your dentist. Small adjustments to your brushing technique, flossing routine, or product selection can often make a significant difference.

Why are preventive dental visits important if my teeth feel fine?

Numerous dental issues arise slowly and often do not produce evident symptoms during their initial phases. Cavities, gum disease, enamel wear, and even certain oral health abnormalities can progress silently. Preventive appointments allow these issues to be identified and addressed before they require more extensive treatment. Regular visits are one of the most effective ways to protect your long-term oral health.
