Types of implant systems we plan around

Single-tooth implant systems

Used when one tooth is missing and the goal is a natural-looking replacement with healthy gum contours, stable support, and a crown that feels right when you chew and floss.

Multiple-tooth implant systems

Used when several teeth are missing and the plan needs to account for spacing, implant distribution, restorative access, force control, and hygiene.

Full-arch implant systems

Used when many or all teeth are failing or missing and the goal is a fixed, more stable full-arch solution designed around chewing, speech, comfort, and long-term maintenance.

Narrow-space and limited-bone options

Used when a case involves tighter spaces, reduced bone volume, or anatomy that requires a more selective implant design rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.

How we decide which implant system is right for you

We start with the end result. That means evaluating your bite, available bone, gum shape, smile line, restorative goals, and how the final implant crown, bridge, or full-arch prosthesis will be cleaned and maintained. Digital impressions, photos, and CBCT imaging may also be used when they improve planning and predictability. Your current San Francisco page already emphasizes bite-driven planning, hygiene access, digital records, and CBCT when indicated.

You should leave your consultation knowing why a certain system makes sense for your case, what the sequence looks like, and what the practical options are before treatment begins.
