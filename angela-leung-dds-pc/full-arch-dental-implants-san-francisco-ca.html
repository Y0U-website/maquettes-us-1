What Dr. Leung checks before recommending full arch dental implants

Bone support, gum condition, and infection

Implants need a stable foundation. We look at bone availability, signs of active infection, gum condition, non-restorable teeth, retained roots, and whether any disease needs to be controlled before surgery. If the mouth is inflamed or infected, that is handled first when needed. Rushing past that step creates problems later.

Bite position, clenching, and prosthetic space

A full arch is not just “teeth on implants.” It has to fit the way your jaws close, the forces you generate when you chew, and the amount of room available for a bridge that is strong but still cleanable. We review bite collapse, heavy clenching, uneven wear, jaw relationship, and whether the final design will have enough space to function without feeling bulky.

Smile support, speech, and hygiene access

The teeth have to look right, but they also have to sound right and clean well. We pay attention to smile line, lip support, arch shape, speech concerns, and how you will clean around the bridge or prosthesis at home. This is one of the biggest differences between a result that works for years and a result that becomes frustrating fast.

Your full-arch choices

When people ask about All-on-4 or All-on-6, they are usually asking about fixed full-arch treatment. Both can be effective in the right case. The better choice depends on bone, bite forces, arch shape, hygiene access, healing risk, and how the final bridge needs to function day to day.

What is All-on-4?

All-on-4 is a full-arch implant approach that uses four implants to support a fixed bridge for one arch. In many cases, the back implants are angled to make use of available bone and help avoid certain anatomic limits. For the right patient, this can create a stable full-arch solution without placing more implants than the case actually needs.

That does not mean four implants are automatically the best answer. The plan still has to respect bone quality, clenching forces, gum health, speech, cleanability, and whether temporary teeth are realistic during healing.

What is All-on-6?

All-on-6 is a full-arch implant approach that uses six implants to support a fixed bridge for one arch. With two additional implants, the load can often be spread across more support points , which may help in cases with stronger bite forces, a longer arch, or a design that benefits from broader support.

For some patients, All-on-6 creates more restorative flexibility and a stronger long-term foundation. For others, it adds surgery without adding enough real benefit to justify it. The right choice depends on the anatomy and the treatment goals, not on whichever number sounds more impressive.

All-on-4 vs All-on-6

Neither option wins by default. All-on-4 may make sense when the available bone, anatomy, and treatment plan support a four-implant design that is stable and cleanable. All-on-6 may make more sense when the case would benefit from added support, wider force distribution, or a more demanding bite situation.

The real question is not which term sounds better online. The real question is which design gives you the most stable bite , the most realistic healing path, and a bridge you can actually live with. That answer comes after imaging, bite analysis, and a full review of whether you are better served by a fixed bridge, an implant-supported removable option, or staged treatment.

Surgical Instruction

The treatment timeline we build around your mouth, not around hype

  • Consultation and records - We review your health history, your goals, what is failing, and what is still potentially restorable. Imaging, photos, digital records, and bite evaluation are used when indicated so the plan is based on anatomy, not guesses.
  • Stabilize the foundation - If infection, gum problems, or hopeless teeth are present, those are addressed in the right order. This is also when we decide whether you are heading toward a fixed bridge, an implant-supported removable option, or a staged plan.
  • Surgery and implant placement - Teeth are removed when needed, implants are placed according to the design, and temporary teeth are used when the case supports that approach. The goal is stable healing, not heroics.
  • Healing and bite refinement - During healing, we monitor the implants, the soft tissue response, bite balance, and comfort. If you are in temporary teeth, this stage matters. It lets us refine function, appearance, and speech before the final version is made.
  • Final restoration and maintenance - Your final teeth are delivered only after the foundation is ready. Then we shift into protection: hygiene coaching, bite checks, maintenance visits, and repairs or adjustments if something changes.

What gets better when the full arch is planned correctly

You can usually chew with more confidence, stop working around loose or failing teeth, reduce the daily stress of dentures that move, and feel less guarded in conversations, meals, and photos. Many patients also notice that a stable bite helps them stop chasing one emergency after another.

Just as important, the result can be easier to live with when the design respects hygiene, speech, lip support, and long-term maintenance from the start. The goal is not just to replace teeth. The goal is to give you back a mouth you can actually use.
