ISQ readings in plain language

Osstell uses Resonance Frequency Analysis (RFA) to generate an Implant Stability Quotient (ISQ) value. The important part isn’t the gadget—it’s the decision clarity it adds.

RFA and the ISQ scale

A small sensor is used to take a non-invasive measurement and produce an ISQ number. That number helps your clinician judge stability at placement and monitor change during healing.

What a stability trend tells us

One number is a snapshot. A trend over time helps answer:

  • Is integration progressing as expected?
  • Is it smart to proceed—or safer to extend healing?
  • Do we need to unload the bite, adjust a temporary, or change sequencing?

Your Osstell checkpoint plan

We use Osstell as a checkpoint inside a bigger, risk-based implant plan—built around your anatomy, bite forces, and restoration design.

Baseline reading

When clinically appropriate, we capture a baseline stability reading after placement to document initial conditions.

Re-check visits tied to your risk profile

Follow-up stability checks are scheduled based on factors like bone density, grafting, bite force, and whether the case is single-tooth, multi-unit, or full-arch.

“Go / hold / protect” decisions

Osstell supports practical decisions such as:

  • Proceeding to restoration when stability supports it
  • Pausing and extending healing when stability is lagging
  • Protecting integration with bite management (occlusion adjustments, temporary modifications, and night-guard planning when needed)
  • Your likely sequencing (staged vs. consolidated) based on safety and predictability

Cases where Osstell matters most

Bruxism and heavy bite forces

If you clench or grind, your plan must include force control. That can change:

  • Temporary design and whether it should be out of occlusion
  • Material selection and occlusal scheme
  • Whether splinting or staging is safer
  • Night-guard strategy after restoration

Grafted sites and low-density bone

Prior implant problems or dental work that didn’t last

If you’ve had loosening, repeated breakage, or disappointing outcomes, measurable checkpoints help separate:

  • normal healing sensations vs overload signals
  • inflammation vs biomechanical stress
  • “wait and protect” vs “change the plan now”
