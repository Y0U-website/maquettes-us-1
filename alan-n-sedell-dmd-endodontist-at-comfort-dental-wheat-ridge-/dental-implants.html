STEP BY STEP

the dental implant process

Our implant process is carefully structured to ensure safety, comfort, and lasting results at every stage.

STEP 01

Consultation & Planning

Getting a dental implant is a multi-step process that takes several months to complete, but the results are worth the investment. It all begins with a comprehensive consultation, during which we assess your oral health, take digital X-rays or 3D scans, and discuss your goals and options.

STEP 02

Surgical Placement

The first surgical step is placing the implant into the jawbone. This is done under local anesthesia or sedation to ensure your comfort. After placement, the implant needs time to heal and integrate with the surrounding bone, which typically takes a few months.

STEP 03

Abutment Attachment

Once healing is complete, we attach a small connector called an abutment to the implant. The abutment holds the final restoration securely in place. Finally, a custom-made crown, bridge, or denture is created to match your natural teeth and restore your smile. Once your restoration is placed, your new tooth will look and function just like the real thing.

STEP 04

Final Restoration

The final step is the placement of your custom-crafted dental crown, which is permanently attached to the abutment. This crown is designed to blend seamlessly with your surrounding teeth in terms of color, size, and shape, giving you a natural and complete-looking smile. Once the restoration is in place, your implant is fully functional — you can eat, speak, and smile with complete confidence. With proper care, your implant can last a lifetime.

AFTERCARE

how to care for

dental implants

One of the biggest advantages of dental implants is that they are easy to care for. However, good oral hygiene is essential to ensure your implant lasts as long as possible. Here are some key steps for maintaining your dental implants:

Brush Twice Daily

Use a soft-bristled toothbrush and non-abrasive toothpaste to clean around the implant and crown just as you would your natural teeth.

Floss Daily

Use implant-specific floss or an interdental brush to clean the areas around and beneath the crown where plaque and bacteria can accumulate.

Use Antibacterial Mouthwash

Rinsing with an antibacterial mouthwash helps reduce bacteria levels in the mouth, protecting both the implant and the surrounding gum tissue.

Attend Regular Dental Visits

Avoid Chewing on Hard Objects

Habits like chewing ice, pens, or hard candy can put excessive pressure on the implant crown and potentially damage the restoration.

Wear a Night Guard if Needed

If you grind or clench your teeth at night, a custom-fitted night guard can protect both the implant and your natural teeth from excessive wear.

With proper care, your dental implants can remain healthy, functional, and beautiful for decades. Many patients find that implants are so natural-feeling they forget they even have them.

Did You Know?

With proper care, dental implants can last 25 years or more — and many patients keep them for a lifetime. Their success rate is over 95 %, making them one of the most reliable restorations in modern dentistry.

Small, consistent choices each day make the biggest difference in the long-term success of your implant.

what are dental implants?

Dental implants are small titanium posts that are surgically placed into the jawbone to replace the roots of missing teeth. Once the implant fuses with the bone through a process called osseointegration, it provides a stable foundation for a custom-made crown , bridge, or denture. Dental implants look, feel, and function like natural teeth, allowing you to eat, speak, and smile with confidence.

Unlike other tooth replacement options, dental implants are designed to be a permanent solution. They help prevent bone loss in the jaw, maintain facial structure, and protect neighboring teeth from shifting out of place. With proper care, dental implants can last for many years or even a lifetime.

who is a good candidate for dental implants?

Many people are excellent candidates for dental implants, but a few key factors must be considered to ensure success. Good candidates typically have healthy gums , sufficient jawbone density, and good overall oral health. It is also important that candidates are committed to maintaining excellent oral hygiene and attending regular dental checkups after their implants are placed.

In some cases, medical conditions such as uncontrolled diabetes, certain autoimmune disorders, or habits like smoking can affect healing and the success of dental implants. However, even if you have these conditions, it may still be possible to proceed with treatment with proper planning and management. A full evaluation by our team at Comfort Dental will help determine whether you are ready for implants or if preliminary steps are needed first.

how to care for dental implants

  • Brush your teeth at least twice a day using a soft-bristled toothbrush and non-abrasive toothpaste.
  • Floss daily, paying special attention to clean around the implant and beneath the gumline.
  • Use an antibacterial mouthwash if recommended by your dentist to reduce plaque buildup.
  • Visit your dentist for professional cleanings and exams at least twice a year, or more often if advised.
  • Avoid chewing on hard objects like ice or pens, which can damage the implant or surrounding teeth.
  • If you grind your teeth at night, a night guard may be recommended to protect your implant and natural teeth.
