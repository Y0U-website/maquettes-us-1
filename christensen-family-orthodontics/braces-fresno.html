How Do Braces Work?

Braces use gentle, consistent pressure to gradually move your teeth into their ideal positions, giving you a healthier, more confident smile. They’re made up of four key parts: brackets that attach to your teeth, bonds that secure the brackets, an arch wire that guides the teeth, and small elastics that hold it all in place. Dr. Christensen carefully designs each treatment plan to ensure effective, lasting results, helping you achieve the smile you’ve always wanted.

What to Expect During Braces Treatment

We begin by designing a personalized treatment plan tailored to your unique smile. We start with a comprehensive assessment using state-of-the-art digital scans, giving us a clear, precise picture of your dental structure—no messy putty impressions needed.

Once your plan is set, we place your braces, attaching the brackets that guide your teeth into alignment. Throughout your treatment, you’ll visit us regularly for adjustments, and our AI-driven apps help track your progress with accuracy, ensuring your braces are working effectively.

When your smile transformation is complete, we’ll remove your braces and fit you with a custom retainer to help maintain your beautiful new smile.

Do braces hurt?

Braces can cause mild, temporary discomfort, especially when first applied, after adjustments, or if a bracket breaks. Manage soreness with soft foods, pain relievers, cold compresses, and orthodontic wax. Pain usually fades within a few days. Contact our office if it’s severe or persistent.

How long do braces take?

Treatment time varies depending on individual needs and the type of orthodontic solution chosen. On average, braces may take about 18-24 months, while treatments with aligners can vary. Dr. Christensen will provide a personalized timeline during your initial consultation.

What can you eat with braces?

When you first get braces, your mouth may feel sore, so it’s best to stick to soft foods like oatmeal, scrambled eggs, soups, pasta, soft vegetables, mashed potatoes, yogurt, and ice cream. Avoid hard, sticky, or crunchy foods that can damage braces, such as hard candy, popcorn, nuts, sticky candy, crunchy raw vegetables, and hard bread. Instead, cut your food into small, manageable pieces, chew gently, and opt for softer textures when possible.
