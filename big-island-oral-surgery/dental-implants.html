Dental Implant Procedures & Implant Process (Oral Surgery in Hilo)

As an oral surgery practice serving Hilo and Hawai‘i County, we provide complete dental implant procedures from tooth extraction to your new replacement tooth . Dr. Rotwein plans and performs your implant surgery for precision and comfort. Here’s how the implant process typically works:

This sequence is tailored to your case during your dental implant consultation. Dental implants are made from medical-grade titanium for durability and biocompatibility, and dental implants include the implant post, the abutment , and the final crown that restores your bite.

Quick facts: Dental implants are made from medical-grade titanium; dental implants include an implant post, an abutment, and a dental crown; and dental implants placed by our team are carefully planned for fit and comfort.

  • Tooth extraction and site preparation. If needed, a gentle tooth extraction is performed. In some cases, implant placement can occur at the same visit.
  • Implant placement. A small titanium implant post is placed in the jawbone to act like a tooth root .
  • Healing and integration. Over several weeks, the fixture bonds with your jawbone. This is the foundation that allows implants to support replacement teeth .
  • Abutment and impression. An abutment is connected to the implant to anchor your dental crown or bridge.
  • Final crown and bite check. Your new replacement tooth (a custom dental crown) is attached and adjusted for comfort.

From Tooth Extraction to New Replacement Tooth

Timing can vary. Some patients qualify for immediate steps on the same visit, while others heal for a short period before receiving the new replacement tooth. Either way, the goal is a stable result that looks like natural teeth and feels comfortable.

Abutment, Implant Post & Dental Crown

The implant post fuses to the jawbone and behaves like a tooth root. The abutment attaches to the implant and supports a lifelike dental crown . Together, these parts create a permanent tooth replacement that is secure and natural-looking.

Bone Graft, Gum Health & Jawbone Support

If your jawbone needs more volume or density, a bone graft may be recommended to strengthen the site for implant placement. Healthy gum tissue and strong bone provide the stability that helps results last for years with proper care and excellent oral hygiene .

Tooth Replacement Options: Bridges and Dentures vs. Dental Implants

Traditional dentures and dental bridges can replace missing teeth, but they don’t stimulate the jawbone the way implants do. Dentures rest on the gums and may shift; bridges require altering neighboring teeth. Implant dentures , by contrast, use implants to support a secure, comfortable prosthesis that improves chewing and confidence. We can also use implants to support an overdenture or fixed bridge for improved stability.

Single Dental Implant for One Tooth

When tooth loss affects one tooth , a single dental implant can replace the missing space without touching neighboring teeth. For many people this is the best dental approach to replace missing teeth one at a time.

Multiple Dental Implants & Full Arch Solutions

If you’re missing one or more teeth in a row—or you have many implants planned—multiple implants may be used to support bridges or an entire full arch of teeth . Advanced full-arch solutions secure a complete smile to a small number of anchors for impressive stability (including All-on-4 (4) when appropriate). For complex cases requiring many implants , we’ll stage care for comfort and predictability.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Implants

Am I a candidate for dental implants?

If you have adequate jawbone and healthy gum tissue—or can benefit from a bone graft —you may be a candidate for dental implants . We confirm this during your dental implant consultation with an exam and x-ray .

What do dental implants include, and how are dental implants placed?

Dental implants include the implant post (the titanium tooth root ), the abutment , and the dental crown . Dental implants placed in healthy bone are designed to integrate securely and support artificial teeth with everyday strength.

Do dental implants may require a bone graft?

Dental implants may require a bone graft if the jawbone needs reinforcement for long-term stability; implants may also be staged to match healing.

How many will I need—the number of implants for my situation?

Dr. Rotwein will explain the number of implants recommended in your plan, whether you need one implant , several, or a full-arch solution.

How long until my new replacement tooth is ready?

Depending on your case, you may receive a temporary tooth the visit of surgery, with a final crown once healing is complete.

What affects the price?

Total cost depends on the number of anchors, need for bone graft procedures, abutment and crown materials, and the overall complexity of your dental implant surgery . We’ll review prices clearly at your consultation .

Do dental implants allow me to eat normally again?

Yes. With a secure foundation in the bone, dental implants allow confident chewing and biting, similar to natural teeth .
