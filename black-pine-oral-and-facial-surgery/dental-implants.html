Types of Dental Implants Offered at Black Pine OMS

Single-Tooth Implants

For patients missing one tooth, a single implant post is placed in the jaw, and a custom crown is attached once the site has healed. This is the most common implant procedure and typically takes two to four appointments over four to six months.

Implant-Supported Bridges

When multiple adjacent teeth are missing, two implant posts can support a bridge of three or more crowns — eliminating the need to alter healthy neighboring teeth. This is a popular option for patients in Boise, ID who have lost several teeth in the same area of the mouth.

All-on-4 Full-Arch Implants

For patients who have lost most or all of their teeth on one or both arches, the All-on-4 dental implants in Boise system provides a full set of fixed, non-removable teeth using just four strategically placed implants. This approach is more affordable than individual implant placement for each missing tooth and can often be completed in a single surgical day. Black Pine OMS serves All-on-4 patients from throughout the Treasure Valley, including patients who travel from Nampa and Eagle specifically for this procedure.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Most adults in good general health who are missing one or more teeth are candidates for dental implants. During your consultation at Black Pine OMS in Boise, ID, your surgeon will evaluate several factors:

Patients who do not have enough native bone may still be excellent candidates after a bone grafting procedure. Many Boise, ID patients who initially thought they were disqualified have gone on to receive successful implants after a preparatory graft.

  • Adequate bone volume: The jaw must have sufficient bone to anchor the implant. If bone loss has occurred following an extraction, a bone graft in Boise, ID may be needed to build up the site before implant placement.
  • Healthy gums: Active gum disease must be treated before proceeding with implants.
  • No uncontrolled systemic conditions: Conditions such as unmanaged diabetes or certain autoimmune disorders can affect healing. Your surgeon will coordinate with your primary care provider as needed.
  • Non-smoker (or willingness to quit): Smoking significantly increases the risk of implant failure and is the single most controllable risk factor in implant outcomes.

The Dental Implant Process: Step by Step

Step 1: Consultation and 3D Imaging

Your journey begins with a comprehensive consultation at Black Pine OMS. We use cone beam CT (CBCT) 3D imaging to assess the quality and quantity of your jawbone in detail, plan the precise placement of each implant, and identify any anatomical structures — such as the inferior alveolar nerve — that must be avoided. This level of planning dramatically reduces surgical risk and improves outcomes.

Step 2: Preparatory Procedures (If Needed)

If you require a tooth extraction or a bone graft before implant placement, those procedures are completed first and given time to heal. The tooth extraction process in Boise, ID is performed with sedation for your comfort, and the site is often grafted at the same appointment to preserve bone volume for the future implant.

Step 3: Implant Placement Surgery

Under local anesthesia and IV sedation, your oral surgeon makes a small incision in the gum tissue and places the titanium implant post into the prepared site in the jawbone. The gum is then sutured closed over the implant. Most patients return to light activity within two to three days and experience manageable soreness for about a week.

Step 4: Osseointegration (Healing Phase)

Over the next three to six months, the implant post fuses with the surrounding bone. This is a passive phase — you will have a temporary restoration in place and simply allow the body to heal. Follow-up appointments at our Boise, ID office allow us to monitor your progress.

Step 5: Crown, Bridge, or Prosthetic Placement

Once osseointegration is confirmed, a small connector piece called an abutment is attached to the implant, and your permanent crown, bridge, or full-arch prosthetic is secured on top. The final restoration is custom-fabricated to match the color, shape, and size of your natural teeth.

Frequently Asked Questions: Dental Implants in Boise, ID

How long do dental implants last?

With proper oral hygiene and regular dental check-ups, dental implants can last 20 to 30 years or more. The titanium post itself often lasts a lifetime; the crown on top may need replacement after 10 to 15 years due to normal wear.

Are dental implants painful?

The procedure is performed under local anesthesia and IV sedation, so most patients feel little to no discomfort during surgery. Post-operative soreness is typically mild and manageable with over-the-counter pain medication for the first three to five days.

How much do dental implants cost in Boise, ID?

Costs vary based on the number of implants, any preparatory procedures needed, and the type of restoration. Black Pine OMS provides a detailed cost estimate at your initial consultation and offers financing options through CareCredit and other partners to make treatment accessible.

What is the difference between dental implants and All-on-4?

Traditional dental implants replace individual teeth, one implant per missing tooth. All-on-4 dental implants use just four implant posts to support an entire arch of teeth — making it a more cost-effective solution for patients who have lost most or all of their teeth on one or both arches.

Do I need a bone graft before getting dental implants?

Not always. If you have sufficient bone density and volume at the implant site, a graft is not necessary. However, if bone loss has occurred — which is common following tooth extractions or with long-standing gum disease — a bone graft in Boise, ID may be recommended to create a stable foundation for the implant. Your surgeon will determine this during your 3D imaging consultation.
