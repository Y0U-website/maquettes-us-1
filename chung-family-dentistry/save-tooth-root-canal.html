What Is Root Canal Therapy?

Root canal therapy is a procedure used to treat a tooth in which the pulp the soft tissue inside the tooth containing nerves and blood vessels has become infected or inflamed. This typically occurs when a cavity is left untreated and bacteria erode through the outer layers of the tooth and reach the root. A root canal removes the infected pulp, cleans and disinfects the canals, and seals the tooth to prevent reinfection. In most cases, a crown is then placed over the treated tooth to restore its strength and appearance.

Signs You May Need a Root Canal

Many patients don’t realize they have an infected tooth until the pain becomes difficult to ignore. Some common signs that you may need root canal therapy include:

Persistent or severe toothache, especially when chewing or applying pressure

Prolonged sensitivity to hot or cold temperatures

Swollen, tender, or inflamed gums near the affected tooth

A persistent pimple-like bump on the gums (dental abscess)

A cracked or chipped tooth that has become painful

If you are experiencing any of these symptoms in Newport News, VA, or the surrounding area, contact our office promptly. Early treatment leads to better outcomes and faster relief.

The Root Canal Procedure Explained

Root canal therapy is typically completed in one to two appointments. The area is first numbed with a local anesthetic to ensure you are completely comfortable throughout the procedure. Our dentist then creates a small access point in the top of the tooth to reach the pulp chamber and root canals. The infected tissue is carefully removed, the canals are cleaned and shaped, and the space is filled with a biocompatible material called gutta-percha. The tooth is sealed, and in most cases a custom crown is fabricated to restore full function.

Root Canal Therapy for Pain Relief

Contrary to popular belief, a root canal does not cause pain it relieves it. The discomfort patients experience before treatment is caused by the infection itself, not the procedure. With modern anesthesia techniques and a skilled dental team, most patients report that root canal therapy feels no different from having a routine filling placed. After the procedure, some mild soreness is normal for a few days and is easily managed with over-the-counter pain relievers.

Preserving Your Natural Tooth

Whenever possible, preserving your natural tooth is the best outcome for your long-term oral health. Natural teeth support healthy jawbone structure, maintain proper bite alignment, and allow you to chew and speak naturally. Root canal therapy makes it possible to keep a tooth that might otherwise need to be extracted, avoiding the need for replacement options such as implants or bridges.
