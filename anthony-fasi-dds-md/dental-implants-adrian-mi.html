Types of Anesthesia

There are three main types of anesthesia that we use at our practice:

Local Local anesthesia is commonly used for minor oral surgical procedures such as simple dental extractions, single dental implant placement, or small soft tissue biopsies in the mouth. Local anesthetic only affects the site to be worked on.

IV Sedation IV sedation is administered directly into the patient’s vein. The medications act to give the patient a combination of tranquility, sedation, and amnesia. The amount and type of medication given will determine the depth of anesthesia. This technique is most commonly used during the removal of impacted wisdom teeth, extensive dental implant surgery, or any surgical treatment that is more complex.

Nitrous Oxide This is commonly known as laughing gas. This is a mild sedative that is administered through inhalation. It is commonly used in young children and is always accompanied by a local anesthetic injection in the area to be treated. After the treatment is completed, the patient will breathe some oxygen for a few minutes, and the sedative effects of the gas will dissipate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Total treatment time for your procedure depends on several factors, such as the number of dental implants, the position of the missing teeth, oral health, and if additional treatments, such as bone grafting , are needed.

First, your oral surgeon will make sure you are informed of the process and all your questions have been answered. They will then administer the form of anesthesia selected during your consultation. Once the anesthesia has taken effect, your surgeon will make an incision in the gums and place the dental implant post in a targeted area of the jaw bone. The incision site will then be stitched closed. Over time, the dental implant post will fuse with the jaw bone to create a stable base for the rest of the implant.

All surgery carries some degree of discomfort. It is our job as specialists and our mission as compassionate providers to make every effort to minimize this discomfort throughout the procedure and recovery process. We offer several forms of anesthesia to keep you comfortable during surgery and will send you home with post-operative instructions to optimize your healing.

Let our office know right away if you are experiencing severe pain, swelling, or bleeding after surgery. We will address this issue promptly.

The recovery process varies from person to person. To reduce the chance of complications, we will send you home with post-operative instructions to follow carefully. Patients should continue their oral hygiene routine but take extra care around the implant site. Certain foods may also need to be avoided during this time. Please ask our office if you have any questions about your recovery.
