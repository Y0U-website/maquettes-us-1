Caring for Your Dental Implants

Frequently Asked Question

A dental implant is a permanent tooth replacement option that uses a titanium post placed in the jawbone to support a crown. It functions like a natural tooth, restoring appearance, strength, and stability while preventing bone loss over time.

A good candidate for dental implants is someone with healthy gums, sufficient jawbone, and overall good oral health. Even if bone loss is present, treatments like bone grafting can make implants possible for many patients.

The cost of dental implants varies based on the number of implants, materials used, and any additional procedures needed. While they may seem expensive upfront, implants are a long-term investment due to their durability and low maintenance.

Dental implants are often preferred over dentures because they are fixed, more stable, and look and feel like natural teeth. Unlike dentures, implants do not slip or require adhesives and help maintain jawbone health.

With proper care and regular dental checkups, dental implants can last 20 years or more, often a lifetime. The crown may need replacement over time, but the implant itself is designed for long-term durability.

The dental implant procedure is typically performed under local anesthesia, making it comfortable for most patients. Mild discomfort after the procedure is normal and can be managed with medication, with most people resuming normal activities quickly.
