What is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a small, medical-grade post—usually made of titanium—that acts exactly like the natural root of a tooth. Unlike bridges or dentures, which sit on top of the gums and can sometimes slip or slide, an implant is placed securely into the jawbone. This secure bond between the post and the bone creates a rock-solid foundation for a custom-made crown, which is the visible porcelain part of the tooth you see when you smile. Because the post actually fuses with your bone over time, it becomes a permanent part of your body that feels and functions just like a real tooth.

An implant does more than fill a gap in your smile; it provides essential health benefits that other options cannot offer. For instance, it is the only dental treatment that stimulates the jawbone, helping prevent it from shrinking or thinning over time. Additionally, the implant acts as a sturdy placeholder, preventing your surrounding natural teeth from shifting out of their proper alignment. Because it is made of durable, biocompatible materials, an implant is a long-term solution designed to last for decades, or even a lifetime, with simple routine care.
