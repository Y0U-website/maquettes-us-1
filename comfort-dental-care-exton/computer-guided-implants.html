How Computer Guided Implant Placement Works

Step 1: 3D Imaging With CBCT

A CBCT scan creates a detailed 3D model of your jaws. This view helps assess bone height, width, and density and shows nearby structures. The scan is quick, and radiation exposure is kept as low as reasonably achievable.

Step 2: Digital Implant Planning

Using the scan, the implant position is selected to support the final crown. Digital implant planning considers load distribution, esthetics, and available bone. This is where questions like “What are computer guided dental implants?” and “How does guided implant surgery work?” are addressed in detail with images.

Step 3: Surgical Guide Fabrication

A custom guide is 3D-printed or milled. It fits over the teeth or gums and includes sleeves that direct implant drills to the planned depth and angle. The guide helps translate the digital plan with high accuracy.

Step 4: Guided Implant Surgery

On the day of surgery, local anesthesia is used, and sedation may be available when appropriate. The guide is seated, and the implant is placed as planned. Some cases permit smaller incisions, while others still need a small flap for visibility or grafting. Guided implant placement is designed to be efficient and precise.

Step 5: Healing and Restoration

After placement, the implant integrates with the bone in a process called osseointegration, which usually takes several months. A temporary tooth may be placed in select cases, while others use a healing cap until the final crown is attached. Our dentist will review whether immediate load or a staged timeline is best for long-term success.

What to Expect

Before treatment, you will have a consultation, a CBCT scan, and a review of medical history. The team will explain digital implant planning, grafting needs if any, and the expected timeline for healing and restoration.

After surgery, mild soreness and swelling are common for a few days. Use prescribed or recommended pain relief, keep the site clean, and eat soft foods as directed. Avoid smoking and heavy exercise until advised. Most patients return to routine activities within a day or two.

Risks include infection, implant failure, and injury to nearby structures. Computer guidance reduces these risks by improving visibility and accuracy, but it does not eliminate them. Regular follow-ups and excellent home care are essential for long-term success.
