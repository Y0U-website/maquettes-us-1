How is a dental implant different from a bridge or denture?

Dental implants are the gold standard in tooth replacement, giving you a natural look and the ability to bite down and chew with ease. If you’re missing one or more teeth, a bridge or denture can fill the gaps in your smile, but only a dental implant can stimulate your jawbone and mimic your missing tooth’s roots.

How Is a Dental Implant Different?

We surgically place a titanium post into your jawbone. This implant acts as your tooth’s roots, giving you a stable foundation and keeping your jawbone from deteriorating. Once your implant heals, we can add a custom crown, bridge, or denture to complete your smile.

Although a dental bridge and a traditional denture can replace your missing teeth, neither of these restorations stimulates your jawbone, making a dental implant the best option for your oral health.

Are Dental Implants the Best Option for Me?

A dental implant is the only tooth replacement option today that restores your tooth and your root making it a virtual do-over where tooth loss is concerned.

If you have missing teeth, your jawbone shrinks away, possibly making you look older than your age. Because a dental implant restores your tooth root, you don’t have to worry about jawbone shrinkage . Also, if you have missing teeth, your healthy teeth drift toward your smile gaps over time, which leads to bite issues, tooth wear, and may even contribute to additional tooth loss.

Unlike other tooth replacement options, dental implants do not require special care. There are no adjustment appointments, no refitting, and no messy pastes. With dental implants, you simply brush and floss as you would with natural teeth.

With proper home care and regular appointments for dental exams and cleanings , your implants may serve you for life.

However, determining whether dental implants are the best option for you starts with an appointment in our dentist office. In some cases, you may need dental work before you re able to get dental implants. For example, if you have significant bone loss.

How Can I Avoid Additional Tooth Loss?

The best way to avoid tooth loss, including extractions for decay, is through preventive dentistry.

Preventive dental care creates the best foundation for healthy teeth long term. These routine treatments lower your risks for tooth decay, root infection, and periodontal disease.

General and preventive dentistry includes:

Taken together, these practices visiting the dentist regularly and brushing and flossing daily help lower your risks for future tooth loss.

  • Dental exams and teeth cleanings every six months
  • Brushing and flossing twice-daily at home more if necessary
  • Avoiding sugary and acidic foods and beverages
  • Wearing a mouthguard during sports
  • Wearing a nightguard for bruxism
