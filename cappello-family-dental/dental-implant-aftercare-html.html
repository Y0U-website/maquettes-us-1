Dental Implant Aftercare

If you ever have any questions about how to clean your mouth at home or which products you should use, your dentist and the hygienists in our office would be happy to coach you.

  • Floss daily. If you have an implant-supported bridge, you may need to use a floss threader or water flosser to clean the area beneath your pontics (artificial teeth).
  • Use an antibacterial mouth rinse. Swishing for 30 60 seconds before or after you brush can reduce the amount of harmful bacteria in your mouth.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Physical trauma is another potential cause of dental implant failure. To protect your implants, you should:

  • Be alert for signs of bruxism. Bruxism, teeth grinding and clenching, usually occurs at night. If you often wake up with a sore jaw or your partner tells you that they hear you grinding your teeth while you are asleep, you may need to start wearing a mouthguard every night. A custom mouthguard from a dentist provides better protection than a store-bought one.
  • Take precautions when you are playing sports. If you regularly engage in any type of sport, especially if it is a contact sport, a custom mouthguard can do much to reduce the risk of damage to both your dental implants and your natural teeth.

Schedule Regular Checkups & Cleanings

Implant-supported teeth cannot get cavities, but that does not excuse you from the need to schedule regular dental appointments. At such appointments, our dental team will clean both your natural teeth and your implant restorations. We may also temporarily remove your restorations to thoroughly clean your implant abutments (the small pieces that connect your implants to your prosthetic teeth).

During your exam, Dr. Cappello will look for any signs of problems with your implants. If he notices any cause for concern, it might be possible to take care of such issues before they pose a serious threat to your smile’s long-term well-being.
