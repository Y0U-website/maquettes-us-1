What Are Dental Implants?

Replacing missing teeth effectively requires addressing both the visible crown and the essential root structure below the gumline. Dental implants accomplish this uniquely through a biocompatible titanium post surgically placed into the jawbone, serving as a durable artificial tooth root.

Your natural bone bonds securely with this post over several months (osseointegration), creating an exceptionally stable anchor. An abutment then connects the integrated implant post to your final, custom-designed replacement tooth or dental prosthesis. This complete system provides stability and health benefits far superior to removable dentures or conventional bridges resting only on adjacent teeth or gums.

Benefits

Complete Functional Restoration

Eat, speak, and laugh without limitation or worry; implant-supported teeth possess stability nearly identical to strong, healthy natural teeth for unrestricted daily activity.

Jawbone Health Maintenance

Implants transmit chewing forces like natural roots, providing the necessary stimulation that prevents the jawbone from shrinking and deteriorating after tooth loss, preserving your facial profile.

Superb Natural Aesthetics

Your final implant crowns or prostheses are meticulously crafted to match the shape, size, and color of your surrounding teeth precisely, resulting in a beautifully restored, seamless smile appearance.

Impressive Longevity

Designed as a permanent tooth replacement foundation, titanium dental implants integrate fully with bone and can easily last for decades—often a lifetime—when maintained with good oral hygiene habits.

Preservation of Adjacent Teeth

Unlike traditional fixed bridges, placing dental implants requires absolutely no grinding down or alteration of neighboring healthy teeth, keeping their natural structure fully intact long-term.

Unmatched Comfort & Confidence

Securely anchored implants eliminate the movement, potential sore spots, and need for adhesives associated with removable options, delivering exceptional daily comfort and renewed self-confidence.

Types of Dental Implants

Understanding that each patient presents unique clinical needs and aesthetic desires, our Hoover office provides a comprehensive spectrum of advanced dental implant solutions. Drs. Walsh and Vila expertly guide you toward the optimal choice following thorough evaluation:

  • Single Implants : The ideal method for replacing one missing tooth independently without altering adjacent natural teeth; involves one post supporting a custom crown.
  • Multiple Implants : Restores several adjacent missing teeth by utilizing implants as stable anchors for a fixed dental bridge, avoiding reliance on natural teeth for support comfortably.
  • Full-Arch Restoration : A transformative protocol replacing a full upper or lower set of teeth using minimal implants (often 4+) to support a fixed prosthesis, frequently allowing immediate usability after surgery.
  • Zygomatic & Pterygoid Implants : Sophisticated techniques employed for severe upper jaw bone loss, anchoring specialized longer implants into the strong cheekbone or pterygoid plate instead of deficient maxillary bone, often circumventing major grafting.
  • Remote Anchorage Implants : Leverages alternative stable bone sites using specialized implant designs for secure placement in complex cases where conventional jawbone locations lack sufficient volume or quality for implants.
  • Same-Day Custom Provisional Crown : Our integrated digital workflow permits, for many qualifying single-implant cases, the immediate attachment of a precisely fitted, aesthetic temporary crown concurrently with the implant placement procedure.

Types of Anesthesia

We offer several levels of anesthesia and sedation to match the procedure complexity and your personal comfort requirements:

Local Anesthesia

Applied directly to the surgical site, local anesthesia thoroughly numbs the area, eliminating pain while you remain fully conscious and aware. It is typically sufficient for less involved procedures where patient anxiety is minimal.

Nitrous Oxide (Laughing Gas)

Mild sedation achieved by inhaling nitrous oxide mixed with oxygen through a comfortable nasal mask. This option induces a state of calm relaxation while you remain awake and responsive. Its effects wear off very quickly post-procedure, usually allowing you to drive yourself home.

Intravenous (IV) Sedation

A deeper level of sedation administered directly into a vein, often called "twilight sleep." Suitable for more complex surgeries or for patients with moderate to significant anxiety, IV sedation induces profound relaxation where you are semi-conscious but unlikely to remember the procedure afterward. Our oral surgeons possess extensive training for its safe administration.

General Anesthesia

This involves inducing a state of complete unconsciousness, rendering you fully asleep throughout the entire surgical procedure. General anesthesia is typically reserved for the most extensive oral surgeries or for patients with specific medical considerations or severe anxiety, and is administered under careful monitoring by our qualified team.
