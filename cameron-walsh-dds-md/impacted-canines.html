The Canine Exposure Procedure at W.I.S.E. Oral Surgery & Dental Implant Specialists

Successfully exposing an impacted canine involves meticulous planning between your orthodontist and our oral surgery team, often utilizing advanced 3D CBCT scans for precise localization. During your consultation at our Trussville , Prattville , or Hoover office, Dr. Walsh or Dr. Vila will review this plan and discuss suitable anesthesia options (typically local anesthesia, potentially with sedation) to ensure complete comfort.

The surgical procedure itself involves carefully lifting the gum tissue over the impacted canine. Minimal overlying bone might be gently removed if necessary to fully expose the tooth s crown. Depending on the pre-arranged plan with your orthodontist, our surgeon may bond an orthodontic bracket with a small chain directly onto the exposed canine, or simply prepare the site for your orthodontist to easily place the bracket soon after. The gum tissue is then carefully repositioned with sutures, allowing access for the orthodontic forces needed to guide the tooth.

Frequently Asked Questions

An impacted canine is typically an upper "eye tooth" that has become stuck beneath the gumline or within the jawbone and cannot erupt into its proper place in the dental arch naturally. This usually happens due to lack of space, obstructions, or an incorrect eruption path during development.

Canine teeth play a crucial role in a healthy bite and the aesthetic appearance of your smile; they are very strong and important for function. Exposure allows your orthodontist to carefully guide this vital tooth into its correct position, preserving it within your smile rather than removing it and requiring later replacement like an implant or bridge.

The procedure itself is performed comfortably under effective local anesthesia, often combined with sedation if needed for anxiety. Post-operative soreness is expected but usually mild to moderate – generally less than wisdom tooth removal – and readily managed with pain relievers and following simple aftercare instructions.

The surgical exposure typically takes about an hour, varying slightly with complexity. Initial recovery involves a few days of managing mild swelling or soreness with a soft diet. While the surgical site heals quickly, the subsequent orthodontic movement of the canine into alignment takes many months to complete successfully.

Our oral surgeons coordinate directly with your orthodontist regarding optimal timing. Generally, your orthodontist will see you within one to two weeks following the surgical exposure procedure. Once initial gum healing permits comfortable access to the attached bracket or exposed crown surface, they can begin applying gentle orthodontic forces to start guiding the canine s eruption path.
