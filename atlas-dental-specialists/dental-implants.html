Our prosthodontists will give you a clear, honest assessment of what your specific situation requires.

  • Healthy or treated gum tissue — Active periodontal disease needs to be resolved first. We evaluate gum health as part of your initial exam and can refer you for treatment if needed before implant placement.
  • Overall medical health — Conditions like uncontrolled diabetes or autoimmune disorders that affect healing are evaluated. Most controlled conditions are not disqualifiers — we’ll discuss your specific situation.
  • Non-smoker or willingness to quit — Smoking significantly increases implant failure rates. We discuss the impact honestly with patients who currently smoke and can provide support resources.
  • Completed jaw development — Implants are typically not placed in patients whose jawbones are still growing, generally meaning 18 and older.

Do all dentists place dental implants?

General dentists, oral surgeons, periodontists, and prosthodontists can all place dental implants, but their training scope differs significantly. Oral surgeons and periodontists receive advanced training in the surgical placement of the implant itself, but typically refer patients to a restorative dentist or prosthodontist for the crown and finishing work. That handoff between providers adds coordination complexity, extends treatment timelines, and can create inconsistency in how the final restoration is designed.

Prosthodontists are the only specialists with advanced formalized training in both implant placement and implant-supported restoration — meaning they plan the crown from the beginning and engineer the surgical approach around the final result. At Atlas Dental Specialists, Drs. Mangini place the implant, fabricate the abutment, and deliver the final crown — all in-house, without referring out. For complex cases involving full-mouth reconstruction, multiple missing teeth, or bite correction, that unified approach makes a measurable difference in outcomes.

What are the benefits of dental implants compared to other options like dental bridges?

The key structural difference is how each option interacts with the jawbone. A dental bridge spans a gap by anchoring to the teeth on either side, which must be permanently prepared (filed down) to accept the bridge crowns. The gap underneath the bridge gets no stimulation, so the jawbone gradually shrinks beneath it — a process that becomes visible as a sunken appearance over time. The bridge itself typically needs replacement after 10–15 years.

Is dental implant surgery considered a major procedure?

Dental implant placement is classified as minor oral surgery — it’s performed in our Downtown Pittsburgh office under local anesthesia, not in a hospital or general anesthesia facility. Most patients compare it to a tooth extraction in terms of discomfort during and after the procedure. The surgical window is typically 30–60 minutes per implant, and most patients return to normal activity within 2–5 days.
