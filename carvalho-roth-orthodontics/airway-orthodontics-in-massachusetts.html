What Is Airway Orthodontics?

Airway orthodontics is more than straightening teeth. It focuses on how the mouth, jaw, and airway work together. When the airway is narrow or blocked, it can affect breathing during the day and at night. Orthodontic treatments like expanders or braces don’t just create space for teeth; they can also create more room for the airway, leading to easier breathing and better sleep.

For parents, this means that orthodontics can impact more than appearance. It can support your child’s growth, energy, and focus. For adults, it can ease health concerns like snoring or sleep apnea. At Carvalho & Roth Orthodontics , we combine advanced training and experience to give families real solutions that go beyond a straight smile.

Pediatric Airway & Sleep Health

Children are often the first to show signs of airway-related concerns, but the symptoms can be easy to miss. Snoring or mouth breathing at night might not seem serious, but these can point to issues with jaw development or airway size. Kids who breathe through their mouths often struggle with restless sleep, bedwetting, or even trouble concentrating at school.

Airway orthodontics can guide jaw growth in a healthier way. With tools like palatal expanders , we can gently create more space for the airway, helping kids breathe through their nose and sleep more soundly. Parents often notice that after treatment, their child feels more rested, focused, and energetic during the day.

Adult Airway Concerns

Airway health doesn’t only affect children. Many adults live with sleep apnea, snoring, or constant fatigue without realizing orthodontics could help. A small jaw or narrow palate can limit airflow at night, leading to interrupted sleep and long-term health risks like high blood pressure or heart concerns.

At Carvalho & Roth Orthodontics, we evaluate each adult patient with both smile health and airway health in mind. In some cases, orthodontic treatment can open space in the airway, reducing the severity of sleep apnea symptoms or improving breathing. With discreet options like Invisalign or ceramic braces, adults can improve both their smile and their health at the same time.

Treatments & Approaches We Offer

We use several orthodontic approaches that support airway health while creating a beautiful smile:

Every treatment is personalized. Our doctors listen closely to your concerns and recommend the best path to improve both breathing and smile health.

  • Palatal Expanders: Used mainly in children, expanders gently widen the upper jaw to create more room for teeth and airflow.
  • Mandibular Advancement Devices: These appliances adjust jaw position to keep the airway open, especially helpful for adults with sleep apnea.
  • Functional Appliances: These guide jaw growth in younger patients, supporting both facial balance and breathing.
  • Early Orthodontic Intervention: Detecting issues early allows us to prevent future problems with jaw size, bite, and airway development.
  • Braces and Invisalign: Beyond alignment, these treatments can improve bite function and overall oral health, which plays a role in airway support.

Signs & Symptoms Parents Should Watch For

If you’re wondering whether your child might benefit from airway orthodontics, here are some signs to look for:

Even one or two of these symptoms can signal an airway concern. An orthodontic evaluation can give you answers and options.

  • Frequent snoring or noisy breathing during sleep
  • Mouth breathing, especially at night
  • Restless sleep, tossing and turning
  • Bedwetting beyond the early years
  • Daytime fatigue or irritability
  • Trouble paying attention at school
  • Teeth grinding at night
  • Loud snoring
  • Waking up gasping or choking
  • Morning headaches
  • Daytime sleepiness
  • Difficulty concentrating

Common Questions Parents Ask

Can braces help with sleep apnea?

Yes, in some cases. Braces and other orthodontic treatments can adjust the position of the teeth and jaw, which may open the airway and improve airflow at night. While braces alone don’t cure sleep apnea, they can be part of a larger treatment plan to reduce symptoms.

What age should my child be evaluated for airway orthodontics?

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends that children have their first orthodontic check-up by age 7. At this age, we can spot early signs of airway problems, like mouth breathing or snoring, and guide jaw growth to prevent bigger issues later.

Is airway orthodontics only for kids?

No, airway orthodontics helps both children and adults. For kids, we can guide growth with expanders or appliances. For adults, we may use braces, Invisalign, or jaw-positioning devices to improve airflow and support better sleep.

How do I know if my child needs airway orthodontics?

Look for signs like snoring, restless sleep, mouth breathing, or trouble concentrating during the day. If you notice these, an orthodontic evaluation can help you understand whether airway treatment might help.

Does airway orthodontic treatment hurt?

Most treatments cause only mild discomfort at first, similar to regular orthodontics. Kids and adults usually adjust quickly. Our team makes sure every step is comfortable and supportive.

How long does airway orthodontic treatment take?

Treatment time depends on age, growth, and the type of appliance used. Some children may only need an expander for a few months, while others may need braces or Invisalign for a year or two. We’ll explain your timeline clearly at your consultation.
