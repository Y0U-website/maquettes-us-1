Dental Implants Centerville

If you aren’t a good candidate for dental implants now, this doesn’t completely rule them out. Many patients need to have preliminary procedures completed beforehand, including tooth extractions, gum disease treatment, or bone grafting. We are more than happy to help you get these procedures scheduled so you can work towards getting dental implants in the future.

  • Oral Health: Cavities and gum disease increase your risk of dental implant failure. If you have any existing oral health issues, they need to be addressed before you can get implants.
  • Jawbone Density: You must have an adequate enough jawbone to successfully support dental implants.

Missing One Tooth

To replace one missing tooth, a single dental crown is affixed to one dental implant without having to rely on the surrounding healthy teeth for support unlike a dental bridge. The crown is color-matched to fit in perfectly with your surrounding teeth. No one will be able to tell the difference between your replacement tooth and natural teeth!

Missing Multiple Teeth

Three or more consecutive missing teeth can utilize two dental implants to secure a bridge. Dental implants can also be used to support a partial denture if you are missing multiple teeth in different locations of an arch.

Missing All Teeth

Even if you’ve lost all your teeth, dental implants can help. Through four to six strategically placed implants, your full dentures can be firmly set in place and give you a fully functional smile once again.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

One of the many reasons that dental implants are considered the gold standard in tooth replacement is that they can last for 30+ years with proper care. More specifically, we encourage our patients in Centerville to:

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Remember, even though your dental implants cannot decay, the gums that help to support them can develop an infection if neglected. Not only that, but any remaining natural teeth you have can develop a large cavity, potentially resulting in further tooth loss. So, it’s extremely important that you prioritize healthy habits, starting with brushing for two full minutes and extending to rinsing with mouthwash for 30-60 seconds.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Of course, your diet can also impact the condition of your dental implants. That’s why we advise against eating lots of sugary and starchy snacks, like potato chips, hard pretzels, and chocolate chip cookies. Instead, stick to mostly nutrient-dense, well-balanced meals and exercise caution with crunchy foods, like raw carrots and almonds.

Tip: Make water your go-to beverage, as well! This will help ensure that you stay hydrated, reduce your risk of dry mouth, and help clear away food particles from your teeth.

Break Bad Habits

Although the restoration secured to your dental implant is crafted from only the best materials, it can break with enough pressure. For that reason, it’s important to break bad habits, like using your teeth to remove bottle caps or tear into plastic wrapping. Additionally, you should avoid tobacco products, chewing on ice cubes, and biting your nails.

Protect Your Dental Implants

Breaking the bad habits we mentioned above will go a long way when it comes to protecting your dental implants. However, that’s just the beginning. If you stay active by playing sports, lifting weights, or riding your bike, you should also wear a mouthguard so your teeth, gums, and dental implants are protected. Similarly, if you clench or grind your teeth at night, you should wear a nightguard.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

A common misconception that many patients have is that they don’t need to schedule routine dental visits if they don’t have any of their natural teeth left. That’s not the case. In fact, biannual checkups and cleanings are a crucial component of dental implant care in Centerville! So, if it has been more than six months since your last visit, use this as a reminder to get your next appointment on the calendar.

Preliminary Treatments & Dental Implant Surgery

Preliminary treatments (procedures performed before dental implant surgery) and dental implant surgery are considered as separate costs. Not every patient needs preliminary treatments, but for those who do, the goal is to promote successful dental implants. Conditions like jawbone loss and gum disease can cause complications, so it’s best to address them when necessary.

The Parts of Your Dental Implant

Dental implants have three main parts: the titanium post, a small connector piece, and the final restoration. While the post and connector piece are a standard part of all dental implant treatments, the final restoration can vary. A singular implant crown is usually less expensive than an implant bridge, which tends to be less expensive than an implant denture. That said, implant dentures are still very cost effective if you need to replace an entire arch of teeth.

How Dental Implants Can Save You Money

Though they do cost more than non-implant alternatives, dental implant restorations can save you money over time. They last decades longer, are significantly more durable, and prevent jawbone loss. Because of these benefits, patients find that they end up spending less on repairs, replacements, and frequent visits to the dentist.

Does My Dental Insurance Cover Dental Implants?

It will ultimately depend on the details of your unique policy. Many dental insurance plans classify dental implants as not medically necessary, and therefore don’t cover them directly. However, your plan will likely cover other costs associated with your treatment, such as X-rays, dental implant surgery, and your final restoration. We encourage patients to double-check their plans and call their dental insurance provider to confirm their benefits.
