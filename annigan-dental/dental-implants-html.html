What are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a comprehensive tooth replacement technique that is composed of three parts: the implant post, the prosthetic tooth, and a connecting abutment that attaches the two. The post is surgically implanted beneath the gum line and restored with the prosthetic after it has fused with the jawbone.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

Dental implants take longer to get than dentures or dental bridges, with treatment often lasting months. However, you needn’t fear the posts’ benefits make them worth the time and effort! Annigan Dental places dental implants in-house, so you can expect the best results possible. Dr. Hsue will make sure of that with his advanced skills and expertise! As he and our team operate, the implant process will include four stages: consultation, surgery, osseointegration, and delivery of the final restoration.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

At the start, you’ll visit us for a consultation. This visit helps us learn about your treatment goals, oral health status, and similar points. After assessing these details, we’ll confirm whether dental implants suit your needs.

Please don’t panic if we decide you don’t qualify for treatment. To be honest, you can become a good implant candidate later through extra dental work extractions, periodontal therapy, etc. Such add-on services would make your mouth strong enough to handle implant posts.

Dental Implant Surgery

Here at Annigan Dental, we perform dental implant surgery in-house. Doing so lets our dentists oversee the placement work and further ensures fantastic results!

Your dental implant surgery will feature four steps:

  • Numbing Before anything else, we’ll numb the treatment site with a local anesthetic. This medicine keeps you from feeling pain during care; the most you’ll sense is a slight pressure.
  • Incision Work We’ll make an incision in your gums once your mouth is numb. That way, your mouth will have enough space for the dental implant.
  • Placement Once the incision is made, we’ll place your dental implant and suture your gums shut afterward.
  • Capping Our team will conclude surgery by putting a protective cap over your dental implant.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

Following your surgery, your dental implant will start fusing with your jaw. This process of osseointegration takes roughly 4-6 months to finish. By its end, your implant post will be a secure and permanent part of your mouth.

You’ll see us for a follow-up surgery after the implant fusion process. At that time, we’ll place your implant’s abutment a metal connector that secures the final restoration. A dental lab will craft said restoration while you heal from the abutment work.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restoration(s)

Our team will deliver your final restoration at the end of your treatment. Based on your needs, this device could be a dental crown, dental bridge, or implant denture. Its type and nature will depend on how many teeth you’re replacing.

Overall, it won’t take us long to fit your final restoration. Setting it in place only requires that we apply some dental cement and last-minute changes. After we’ve confirmed you have good results, we’ll then send you off to enjoy the benefits of your restored smile!

Understanding the Cost of Dental Implants

What Factors Affect the Cost of Dental Implants?

During a dental implant consultation, your implant dentist will consider a few factors to give you a proper estimate of what you can expect to pay for your treatment. These include the following:

  • How many teeth are missing : Because dental implants are priced individually, the number of implant posts that you require will be a significant factor in what you will pay for your procedure overall.
  • The health of your jawbone : Patients who have had gum disease or those who have been missing teeth for a while may require a preliminary bone graft procedure. This increases volume and makes it more likely your dental implants will be successful, but it also drives up the cost of dental implants.
  • What the dental implants are made of : Most implant posts are titanium, but patients who have metal allergies may require an alternative material. Ceramic and zirconia implant posts cost more.
  • Where in the jaw the implants will be placed : Front teeth require a more complex placement due to aesthetic concerns, which means it may cost extra to place and restore these dental implants.

Dental Implant Post-Op Instructions

Now that you have your dental implants, the next step is to make sure they stay in place long-term. After surgery, you can expect our team at Annigan Dental to provide essential dental implant post-op instructions in Bothell. These will help you understand what to expect, common side effects, what to eat and what to avoid, and how to clean your teeth and gums carefully. In preparation for the next phase of treatment, we’ve provided a brief breakdown of these guidelines for you to review.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

Once you are recovering at home after dental implant surgery, avoid strenuous activities, as this can increase blood flow and make it harder for your mouth to heal. You will also need to avoid smoking, using a straw, or spitting. It is essential that blood clots form in the surgical areas, and any of these actions can dislodge them, resulting in a longer recovery period and bothersome pain.

Common Side Effects

Do not be surprised if you notice minimal bleeding, swelling, and discomfort in the days following your procedure. This is common and will dissipate over time. To manage these side effects, use clean gauze to control bleeding, apply a cold compress to reduce swelling, and take an over-the-counter pain reliever to relieve discomfort.

If you notice these issues worsening rather than improving over the coming days, call our office and alert a member of our team.

Diet

Within the first few days after surgery, you will likely need to eat a soft food diet. This prevents unnecessary pressure from chewing and allows your gums and bone to heal more swiftly. Yogurt, oatmeal, mashed potatoes, warm soup, and similar foods are examples of what you may want to eat during this time. As your mouth heals and begins to work normally without pain, you can start adding solid foods to your diet.

Health & Oral Hygiene

You will need to wait 24 hours before completing your normal oral hygiene routine. After this time, you can start brushing your teeth and flossing, but you will need to be extra careful when nearing the surgical areas. You do not want to irritate the tissues or implants. Instead of using traditional mouthwash, cleanse your mouth with a saltwater rinse. However, make sure you don’t swish it around vigorously in your mouth. Instead, move your head side to side and allow the liquid to pour out. Spitting can accidentally dislodge the blood clots.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

The only potential issue you might face after your new teeth are attached is slight sensitivity. Fortunately, this will go away after a short time, so you can begin enjoying your new smile and the endless benefits it offers.

Dental Implant FAQs

What are dental implants made of?

Dental implants are made of titanium, a material that actually bonds with your jawbone in the process known as osseointegration. Over a recovery period of three to six months, the bone tissue fuses together with each dental implant.

How are dental implants placed?

Dental implants are surgically placed by a qualified dentist, periodontist, or oral surgeon. Local anesthesia and sedation are available for your comfort throughout the procedure. After the implants have healed together with your jawbone, they are ready for restoration. You return to your dentist’s office for the placement of the crown, bridge, or denture.

How do dental implants compare to other tooth replacement options?

Dental implants are by far the best tooth replacement currently available. Nothing else rebuilds your missing tooth structure in quite the same way. By giving you back your tooth’s root as well as its visible crown, dental implants provide the ultimate stability for your smile.

How long do dental implants last?

With proper care and maintenance, dental implants can be expected to last for a lifetime. Compare that to traditional dentures and fixed bridges and you see that dental implants are indeed the best financial investment you can make.

How much do dental implants cost?

The cost of dental implant procedures varies based on how many teeth are being replaced, where in the mouth they are located, and whether or not you need a preparatory procedure (like a bone graft or sinus lift) first. But in general, each dental implant costs between $3,000 and $4,500, including the prosthetic that is affixed to it. Your dentist will help you know what to expect so you can prepare your budget for your tooth replacement.

Does getting dental implants hurt?

Rest assured, there’s nothing painful about getting a dental implant. Your jaw will be completely numb throughout implantation, and we also offer options in sedation dentistry for additional relaxation.

Am I a good candidate for dental implants?

People who are in solid oral and overall health are usually good candidates for implant dentistry. Health conditions affecting the immune system, like uncontrolled diabetes or advanced stage cancers, affect your ability to properly heal. Non-invasive replacement options are preferred in these instances.
