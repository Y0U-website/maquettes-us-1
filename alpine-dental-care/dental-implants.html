Implant-Supported Bridges

An implant-supported bridge may replace several adjacent missing teeth. Instead of attaching the bridge to natural teeth, dental implants provide support at planned positions within the missing-tooth area.

This option may reduce the need for a removable partial denture and may be considered when several teeth are missing in the same region. The number of implants required depends on the location of the missing teeth, bone availability, bite forces, and the design of the final restoration.

Patients can also learn about traditional dental bridges as a non-implant alternative.

The Dental Implant Process

Step 1: Preparatory Treatment

Before implant placement, active infection, gum disease, decay, or other oral health concerns may need to be addressed.

When a tooth cannot be preserved, learn more about tooth extraction when a tooth cannot be saved .

When preserving the natural tooth may still be possible, Dr. Pyle may discuss repairing a damaged natural tooth .

  • Removal of a tooth that cannot be restored
  • Treatment of gum disease
  • Bone grafting
  • Repair of nearby teeth
  • Adjustment of an existing temporary appliance
  • Coordination with a dental specialist when needed

Step 2: Implant Placement

The dental implant is placed into the jawbone at the planned position. Local anesthesia is used to numb the area. Additional comfort options may be discussed when appropriate.

After placement, patients may experience temporary soreness, swelling, bruising, or tenderness. Post-treatment instructions will explain how to care for the site, what foods to avoid, and when to contact the office.

Step 3: Osseointegration

The implant is given time to integrate with the surrounding bone. This healing stage often takes several months, although the exact timeline varies.

Factors that may influence healing include:

A temporary tooth-replacement option may be available in some cases.

  • Implant location
  • Bone density
  • Bone grafting
  • Tobacco use
  • Overall health
  • Oral hygiene
  • Individual healing response

Step 4: Abutment and Final Restoration

Once the implant is ready to support a restoration, an abutment connects the implant to the replacement tooth or prosthesis.

The restoration is designed to fit the bite and complement surrounding teeth. Patients can review information about custom dental crowns when a crown is part of the treatment plan.

  • A dental crown
  • An implant-supported bridge
  • A removable implant-supported denture
  • A fixed full-arch restoration
