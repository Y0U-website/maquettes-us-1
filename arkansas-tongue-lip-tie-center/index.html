The practice

Tongue tie (ankyloglossia) and lip tie occur when the small bands of tissue beneath the tongue or upper lip are too short, thick, or tight restricting normal movement. These conditions affect newborns, infants, children, and adults, and can interfere with breastfeeding, speech, dental development, and overall quality of life.

A restricted lingual frenulum limits how far and freely the tongue can move. This affects a baby’s ability to latch deeply, reduces feeding efficiency, and can later interfere with speech articulation particularly sounds that require tongue elevation or mobility.

A tight maxillary labial frenulum prevents the upper lip from flanging outward properly, leading to a shallow latch during breastfeeding. It can also contribute to a gap between the upper front teeth and cause dental spacing concerns as the child grows.
