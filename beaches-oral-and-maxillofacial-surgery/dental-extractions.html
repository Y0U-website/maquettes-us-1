Dental Extractions

Dental extractions, also known as tooth removal, are procedures where an oral surgeon removes a tooth from its socket in the jawbone. This may be necessary for various reasons, such as severe decay, infection, crowding, or damage that cannot be fixed with other treatments.

While no one loves the idea of losing a tooth, dental extractions in Jacksonville Beach, FL, are sometimes necessary to maintain overall oral health and prevent further complications. Our oral surgeon will assess your situation thoroughly before recommending an extraction as part of your treatment plan.

Reasons for Dental Extractions

There are several reasons why dental extractions in Jacksonville Beach, FL, may be necessary.

  • One common reason is severe tooth decay that has damaged the tooth beyond repair. In such cases, extracting the tooth can prevent further pain and infection.
  • Another reason for dental extractions is the overcrowding of teeth in the mouth. This can occur when there isn’t enough space for all the teeth to properly align, leading to misalignment or overlapping of teeth. Extracting a tooth can help create space for proper alignment through orthodontic treatment.
  • Impacted wisdom teeth are also a frequent cause of dental extractions. When wisdom teeth do not have enough room to emerge properly, they can become impacted beneath the gum line, causing pain and potential damage to the surrounding teeth.
  • In some cases, trauma or injury to a tooth may be severe enough that extraction is the best course of action to prevent further complications.
  • Advanced gum disease that has caused significant damage to the supporting structures of a tooth may require the tooth’s extraction too.

Types of Dental Extractions

When it comes to dental extractions in Jacksonville Beach, FL, there are two main types that patients may undergo: simple extractions and surgical extractions.

Both types of extractions require precision and skill from our qualified oral surgeon in Jacksonville Beach, FL, to ensure a successful outcome and minimal discomfort for the patient. It’s essential to follow postoperative care instructions diligently for optimal healing after any type of extraction procedure. Contact us to learn more!

  • Simple extractions are typically performed on teeth that are visible in the mouth. An oral surgeon will use a tool called an elevator to loosen the tooth and then extract it using forceps.
  • On the other hand, surgical extractions are more complex procedures usually done on impacted teeth or those that have broken off at the gum line. In this case, the oral surgeon may need to make an incision in the gum tissue to access the tooth and remove it.

The Procedure for a Dental Extraction

When it comes to the procedure for a dental extraction, it’s important to understand what to expect. Our oral surgeon in Jacksonville Beach, FL, will numb the area around the tooth to ensure you don’t feel any pain during the extraction process. This may involve local anesthesia or sedation depending on the complexity of the extraction.

Once you are comfortable and numb, the surgeon will use specialized tools to gently loosen and remove the tooth from its socket. In some cases, a small incision in the gum may be necessary to access impacted teeth.

After extracting the tooth, the surgeon will provide guidance on postoperative care, such as biting down on gauze to control bleeding and reduce swelling by applying an ice pack externally for short intervals.

It’s normal to experience some discomfort after a dental extraction, but following our oral surgeon in Jacksonville Beach, FL’s aftercare instructions diligently can help promote faster healing and prevent complications.

Aftercare Tips for a Successful Recovery

After undergoing a dental extraction in Jacksonville Beach, FL, proper aftercare is essential for a smooth and successful recovery. Here are some tips to help you heal effectively:

By following these aftercare tips diligently, you can ensure a successful recovery process after your dental extraction!

  • Bite on gauze: Bite down gently on the gauze provided by our oral surgeon near me to help control bleeding and promote clot formation.
  • Avoid vigorous rinsing: Refrain from rinsing your mouth vigorously within the first 24 hours as it can dislodge the blood clot.
  • Take prescribed medications: Follow our military-friendly oral surgeon’s instructions regarding pain relief and antibiotics to prevent infection.
  • Apply ice packs: Use ice packs on the outside of your face to reduce swelling in the first 24 hours post-extraction.
  • Eat soft foods: Stick to a diet of soft foods like yogurt, mashed potatoes, or soup during the initial days following the extraction.
  • Maintain good oral hygiene: Gently brush your teeth but avoid the extraction site while it heals, and rinse with salt water after meals.
